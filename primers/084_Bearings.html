1 · The industry in sixty seconds

Explain it to a ten-year-old

Imagine pushing a heavy cupboard across a floor. Sliding it is hard; putting rollers underneath makes movement easier. A rolling bearing puts carefully shaped balls or rollers between a moving part and a stationary support. The surfaces must be smooth, the pieces must fit, and dirt must stay outside. A plain bearing works differently: the shaft slides against a suitable surface, often separated by a film of oil. Both help a machine move while carrying a load. Making the little pieces is only the start. The supplier must know how fast the machine runs, how hot it gets, which way the force acts and how the customer will maintain it.

Say it to a client in one minute

Bearings connect industrial production with maintenance. Original equipment manufacturers buy them when building vehicles, motors, rail equipment and machinery; owners buy replacements when installed equipment needs service. Schaeffler, Timken, the SKF pair and NRB sell bearing systems or finished products. Harsha sells cages, Rolex sells forged rings, and SKP and Vishal sell rolling elements. Menon also has engine plain bearings and non-bearing subsidiaries. These businesses can grow at different times and keep different portions of the customer's spending.

The eleven-stock basket rose 29.7% in 2026 through 18 September, but Menon and NRB accounted for much of the excitement. Removing Menon reduces that return to 18.5%. The accounting also refuses a simple sector story: Schaeffler's higher profit came with weaker half-year operating cash flow, Harsha's engineering margin declined, Galaxy had substantial other income, and several smaller companies remained financially weak. SKF's demerger makes old and new earnings perimeters particularly easy to confuse.

The central investment question is whether a company earns a repeat return for solving a difficult motion problem after paying for precision equipment, customer qualification, inventory, warranty exposure and working capital. A high-quality product does not automatically produce a high-quality listed investment. The price paid for the shares, the rights held by the Indian subsidiary, the durability of approved programmes and the cash absorbed by growth all still matter.

Use this primer in three passes. First identify the actual product and customer; a cage is not a finished bearing and an engine shell is not a ball bearing. Then reconcile the accounts, including the legal entity, ordinary other income and exceptional items. Finally place the current price move against the company's own operating evidence. The order matters: a chart can tell you that expectations changed, but it cannot tell you which specification was won or whether a debtor paid.

2 · What the products actually do

A rolling bearing normally combines inner and outer rings, raceways, rolling elements and a cage. Seals, shields and lubricant may be supplied as part of the assembly. The rings provide the paths on which the balls or rollers move. The cage spaces the elements and guides their movement; it is not simply decorative sheet metal. The complete unit must suit the application's load, speed, temperature, alignment and contamination conditions. The SKF selection guide explains why bearing selection is a system decision rather than a catalogue-price exercise.

Different shapes solve different load problems

Deep-groove ball bearings are widely used in rotating equipment because their geometry can accommodate radial load and some axial load. That does not make every ball bearing interchangeable. Clearance, seal design, grease, precision and permissible speed can differ even where the outside dimensions match. In a quiet motor, noise and vibration may matter as much as the static load rating. An approved supplier must keep those characteristics consistent through production, not just deliver one impressive sample.

Angular-contact bearings use a contact geometry suited to combined loads; arrangement and preload matter. Tapered roller bearings carry combinations of radial and axial load and are important in applications such as wheel ends and heavy equipment. Cylindrical roller bearings suit substantial radial loads, while spherical roller bearings can accommodate misalignment within their design limits. These are broad engineering descriptions, not a recommendation to substitute one design for another. Application engineers select a complete arrangement, including the shaft and housing.

Needle bearings use long, slender rollers where radial space is limited. NRB's and SNL's businesses show why this category deserves separate attention: a compact transmission or linkage may need a different combination of geometry, surface finish and assembly precision from a large industrial bearing. The NRB annual report and SNL annual report describe their product and customer positions. Counting all of these products in tonnes loses much of the information that determines selling price and qualification difficulty.

Plain bearings support sliding motion rather than placing rolling elements between races. Engine bearings often rely on an oil film and layered materials suited to their duty. Bushes and thrust washers perform related functions in many mechanisms. Menon's annual report covers its bearing business alongside aluminium components and brakes. Timken's acquisition of GGB brings another plain-bearing exposure into its consolidated accounts. Treating these businesses as identical to miniature electric-motor ball bearings would obscure their different demand drivers.

Components are a distinct economic layer

Harsha's cages help finished-bearing manufacturers control rolling-element spacing and motion. Steel, brass and polymer designs have different manufacturing routes and applications. A cage supplier can be deeply embedded in customer programmes without owning the final bearing brand or the replacement channel. Its negotiating position depends on difficult tooling, dimensional consistency, development relationships and qualified capacity. The Harsha presentation separates its engineering activity from solar operations; the consolidated revenue headline should not be mistaken for cage revenue.

Rolex supplies forged and machined rings and other automotive components. Its customer still has further work to do before a ring becomes a finished bearing. The Rolex annual report is therefore a better guide to its economics than a retailer's bearing catalogue. Ring geometry, steel yield, machining depth, heat treatment and customer approvals affect value added. Higher bearing demand helps only to the extent it becomes orders for the particular rings and programmes that Rolex can supply profitably.

SKP and Vishal supply rolling elements. A roller's diameter, roundness, surface condition and consistency across a batch are critical to how an assembled bearing behaves. A defect is small in physical size but can be important in service. This gives precision a commercial role, yet it does not eliminate competition or customer bargaining power. SKP's report and Vishal's report also show that a technically relevant product can coexist with weak group earnings or problematic disclosure.

Finally, an aftermarket bearing sale is not merely a second factory sale. The customer may need identification, stock availability, correct fitting advice and confidence that the part is genuine. A distributor can carry thousands of possible variants while demand for any particular one is intermittent. The manufacturer earns a channel advantage only when availability, authenticity and service justify the inventory and credit required. A wide catalogue is useful; a slow-moving catalogue financed by the supplier can destroy the benefit.

3 · From steel to a running machine

The chain begins with appropriate steel or other materials, then moves through forming, machining, heat treatment, grinding, superfinishing, cleaning, measurement, assembly and testing. The exact sequence depends on the product. A forged ring, a pressed cage and a precision roller do not use the same equipment or have the same rejection risks. Finished-bearing manufacturers may make some components internally and buy others from approved suppliers. A listed component maker's opportunity is partly determined by what the customer chooses to outsource.

Heat treatment is not simply heating a part until it looks right. The material must achieve suitable properties without unacceptable distortion or damage. Grinding and finishing then create the contact surfaces on which the bearing's performance depends. Measurement must detect problems consistently, including errors that are difficult to see with the naked eye. These stages explain why installed machinery and nominal tonnes of capacity are incomplete measures of an operating franchise. Saleable output depends on yield, process stability and the customer's acceptance criteria.

At the finished-product stage, the manufacturer chooses the arrangement, assembles it cleanly and validates performance. A customer may test samples, audit processes and approve a production source for a particular programme. The qualification belongs to that application and production process; it is not a universal permission to sell every new design to every customer. A factory expansion can require fresh approval, especially where machinery, material source or production location changes. Revenue can therefore lag capital spending even when the end market is healthy.

The next fork is distribution. An OEM order follows a production schedule and a negotiated commercial arrangement. Replacement demand follows the installed machine base, maintenance practices, operating hours and failures. Export component orders may follow a global bearing company's inventory decisions before they follow the final user's equipment orders. A distributor destocking cages or bearings can make the supplier's revenue fall even while end-user machines continue to run. Conversely, stocking a new channel can lift dispatches before final consumption rises.

Who receives the value created?

A bearing that prevents an expensive breakdown may save its customer far more than its purchase price. That does not mean the supplier automatically captures the entire saving. Procurement may qualify another supplier, redesign the assembly or demand annual productivity concessions. The strongest commercial position combines a difficult specification, repeat performance and a credible cost of switching. The weakest position supplies an easily substituted part while financing inventory for a much larger buyer. Both businesses can use sophisticated machinery; their returns on that machinery can differ sharply.

A component producer also faces customer concentration by design. Selling to a small number of global bearing groups can provide long programmes and technical learning, but the same groups can direct production among factories and countries. Ask whether a new contract adds a customer, an application, or merely another part number for an existing customer. Those are different forms of diversification. A new geography can even increase concentration if it serves the same purchasing organisation under the same global pricing agreement.

For a finished-bearing brand, technical service extends the chain into the customer's machine. Failure analysis may identify contamination, electrical damage, misalignment or mounting error. Correcting the cause can be more valuable than supplying another identical bearing. The SKF discussion of contamination and life illustrates why the bearing and its environment cannot be separated. A company needs trained people and local response capability to monetise this relationship; service language in a presentation is not proof of meaningful service revenue.

The chain also allocates blame. When a machine stops, several parties may be involved: bearing maker, distributor, installer, lubricant supplier, housing designer and operator. Contract terms and evidence determine who bears warranty cost. A low reported warranty charge is reassuring only alongside sound field performance and adequate provisioning. If the manufacturer routinely replaces products informally through the channel, the economic cost may appear in discounts, returns or selling expense rather than in a line labelled warranty.

Apply the earlier family tests at these specific points. The specification-and-wear test from 078 Castings asks whether the producer is approved for a demanding application and what creates replacement demand. The depth-of-work test from 080 Forgings separates a rough ring from a finished, measured part. The quote-to-delivery input-risk test from 081 Fabrication asks who absorbs steel or brass inflation while an order works through production. Here the answers must be found in actual programmes, processing steps and reset clauses.

Fund-manager lens · the value chain

Harsha can have an important qualification position without owning the replacement customer; Rolex can perform valuable precision work without selling a finished bearing. Conversely, a branded supplier can earn attractive margins while accumulating inventory that consumes cash. Locate the company at the point where it invoices, identify who sets its price, and test the capital needed to keep that position. Product importance is evidence of customer need, not sufficient evidence of shareholder returns.

5 · Money, margin and the profit that belongs to shareholders

Read revenue first, then ordinary Other Income, exceptional items, finance costs, tax, associates and minority interests before accepting PAT. All figures here are crore rupees unless labelled otherwise. June 2026 is the latest quarterly period used; for Schaeffler it is Q2 of calendar 2026, whereas most others call it Q1 FY27. A growth comparison uses June 2025, not March 2026 merely because it is the adjacent column. Annual checks use FY26, except Schaeffler's calendar-year accounts.

Schaeffler: profit growth and cash conversion diverge

The consolidated June filing reports revenue of ₹2,760.55 cr and Other Income of ₹36.71 cr before PAT of ₹325.78 cr, against ₹287.11 cr a year earlier. EPS ₹20.84 multiplied by 15.63 cr shares gives about ₹325.73 cr, close to the reported profit at the published EPS precision. Parent revenue of ₹2,681.41 cr and PAT of ₹336.73 cr are different figures for a different perimeter. Mixing parent margin with consolidated sales would invent a ratio the company never earned.

Half-year operating cash flow fell to ₹389.35 cr from ₹711.90 cr despite group half-year PAT of ₹641.85 cr. The cash-flow statement records ₹489.21 cr absorbed by inventory and ₹161.58 cr by receivables. This does not prove those balances are bad: growth, stocking and timing can consume cash. It does mean that stronger accounting earnings cannot be described as an equally strong cash outcome. Half-year EPS ₹41.07 times the same share count is about ₹641.92 cr, providing the independent scale check.

The review also identifies KRSV subsidiary loss of ₹17.42 cr and negative net assets of ₹127.59 cr. These are subsidiary figures, not listed-company EPS numerators. The investment question is whether the acquired distribution activity improves customer access sufficiently to justify its losses and capital. It should be monitored separately from the established industrial and automotive franchise. Consolidation makes the shareholder responsible for both, even when the core operation is performing well.

Timken: growth with a changed group boundary

Timken's June filing is in millions, so dividing by ten gives consolidated revenue of ₹943.320 cr, Other Income of ₹10.733 cr and PAT of ₹119.659 cr. EPS ₹15.91 times 7.5219 cr shares gives ₹119.673 cr. The comparative PAT is ₹108.425 cr, with primary-filing EPS ₹14.41; a summary displays ₹14.42. FY26 PAT of ₹414.885 cr reconciles to EPS ₹55.16 times those shares, about ₹414.908 cr. These differences are small; the reporting perimeter change is more consequential.

Timken acquired GGB for ₹128.8 cr in December 2025 under common control, and its consolidated comparatives were restated from April 2024. The June filing explains that the subsidiary's prior-year comparative information was compiled by management and not reviewed to the same extent as the parent. GGB adds plain-bearing exposure, so neither the new consolidated growth rate nor the product mix should be spliced blindly onto an older standalone series. The June tax charge also includes a ₹3.373 cr reversal relating to earlier periods, versus ₹8.254 cr in the prior comparison. Profit growth is therefore not a pure operating-margin statistic.

Harsha: higher activity is not yet higher group profit

The statutory June accounts show consolidated revenue of ₹457.43 cr, Other Income of ₹5.13 cr, a small ₹0.03 cr joint-venture contribution and PAT of ₹37.38 cr against ₹37.93 cr. EPS ₹4.11 times 9.104 cr shares gives ₹37.42 cr within rounding. FY26 PAT of ₹155.20 cr similarly checks against EPS ₹17.05. The presentation separates engineering revenue of ₹421.08 cr from solar revenue of ₹36.35 cr. It is the engineering operation, not the entire group revenue, that is closest to the bearing-components thesis.

Engineering EBITDA of ₹69.79 cr and margin of 16.6%, against 18.7% a year earlier, use the company's definition based on PBT plus finance costs and depreciation. This includes Other Income and is not directly interchangeable with a screen's operating-profit margin. The call transcript gives working capital at 116 days and describes raw-material inflation of 6–8%. A summary that compresses this into “68%” radically changes the story. The useful question is the lag between input inflation, customer price resets and cash collection.

NRB: distinguish group profit from owner profit

NRB's consolidated quarter has revenue of ₹369.53 cr, ordinary Other Income of ₹5.47 cr and a separate insurance gain of ₹2.65 cr. Group PAT of ₹37.76 cr includes ₹0.92 cr belonging to minority shareholders, leaving ₹36.84 cr for the listed parent's owners. EPS ₹3.80 times 9.69 cr shares gives ₹36.82 cr. Last year's owner profit was ₹32.09 cr after subtracting ₹0.72 cr minority interest from ₹32.81 cr group PAT. FY26 owner profit of ₹142.75 cr similarly reconciles to EPS ₹14.73.

The insurance receipt is not evidence that bearings became easier to manufacture. The filing separately gives EPS before exceptional items, which helps the reader avoid attributing all improvement to operations. It also flags ₹25.12 cr of overdue foreign receivables. The correct follow-up is collection status and expected credit loss, not a blanket assumption that every export invoice is equivalent to cash. A company can have solid product demand while a small portion of its customer book needs unusual attention.

Menon: strong growth, but do not use the wrong annual sales line

The image table in Menon's June filing gives consolidated sales of ₹91.7888 cr and Other Income of ₹2.5137 cr before PAT of ₹14.1067 cr, against ₹8.4295 cr. EPS ₹2.52 times 5.604 cr shares gives ₹14.1221 cr, within rounding. The primary FY26 sales figure is ₹293.8086 cr, with Other Income of ₹6.4349 cr and PAT of ₹38.2513 cr. There is also a smaller EBITDA presentation difference. More importantly, group growth includes aluminium and brake activities: the full earnings acceleration cannot be called a bearing-volume acceleration.

Rolex: an exceptional charge makes the March base misleading

Rolex's June revenue was ₹304.337 cr, with Other Income of ₹19.836 cr before PAT of ₹60.140 cr, versus ₹49.156 cr. EPS ₹2.21 times 27.233312 cr shares gives about ₹60.186 cr within two-decimal rounding. FY26 PAT of ₹141.098 cr checks against EPS ₹5.18. March's near-zero result followed a ₹50.40 cr right-of-recompense expense, partly offset by a labour-provision reversal. A June-versus-March profit explosion would therefore be a poor description of current ring demand.

The October 2025 ten-for-one split changed face value to ₹1; historical EPS was restated. A buyback completed after June changes later shares, but those later shares should not be multiplied by June EPS. Keep the quarter's appropriate denominator. Other Income also contributes materially to June PBT, so the investor needs operating contribution, material pass-through and customer volumes before concluding that processing margins have strengthened by the same amount as PAT.

The honest read · money and margins

Schaeffler shows that profit growth can outpace cash; Harsha shows that revenue growth can coexist with margin pressure; Menon shows that a classification error can distort the annual base; Rolex shows how an exceptional March charge can exaggerate a sequential recovery. Carry an operating bridge and a cash bridge alongside reported PAT. None of these adjustments justifies discarding an inconvenient statutory expense without explaining its source, recurrence and tax treatment.

6 · SKF: two businesses, one dangerous comparison

The SKF separation is central to this primer because a familiar name conceals a changed economic asset. The industrial undertaking moved into SKF India Industrial, with the scheme effective on 1 October 2025 and a one-for-one allotment to eligible SKF India shareholders. The industrial security began trading on 5 December 2025. The continuing SKF India business is principally automotive. The parent report and industrial report should be read together before comparing either share with the old combined company.

A shareholder who held the predecessor through the separation received exposure to both businesses. Looking only at the continuing parent's current price omits part of that package. Conversely, adding two percentage returns from incompatible starting dates does not reconstruct the package correctly. A defensible total-return series needs the entitlement, relevant prices, distributions and a consistent starting holding. This audit did not establish that full series, so the predecessor is excluded from the long-history basket. The industrial share is separately excluded for insufficient observations.

The automotive comparison

In June's continuing standalone table, revenue is ₹587.79 cr versus ₹462.50 cr, Other Income is ₹12.37 cr versus ₹4.79 cr, and PAT is ₹61.84 cr versus ₹46.74 cr. These are the useful same-business comparisons. Printed EPS of ₹12.5, shown to one decimal, multiplied by 4.9437963 cr shares produces about ₹61.80 cr; the small difference is compatible with that coarse rounding. Prior EPS of ₹9.5 is similarly too rounded to reproduce PAT exactly.

The consolidated June PAT of ₹61.92 cr includes ₹0.08 cr from an associate. The previous June consolidated PAT of ₹118.21 cr belongs to the old industrial-plus-automotive perimeter and is explicitly not comparable. Treating the difference as an automotive profit collapse would be wrong. Equally, taking the old combined revenue and assigning an automotive multiple to it would overstate the continuing business. The correct denominator in a valuation must match the assets and earnings that the current shareholder owns.

FY26 consolidated PAT of ₹265.94 cr is not a clean recurring automotive annual base. Continuing-business PAT of ₹117.22 cr was affected by tax and exceptional items. The March tax expense includes a bilateral advance-pricing agreement adjustment of ₹61.48 cr, including interest of ₹7.28 cr. This belongs in the bridge from operating performance to statutory profit. Calling all of it an operating collapse would misread the business; deleting it without considering future tax economics would be equally casual.

The industrial comparison

SKF India's industrial successor reports June revenue of ₹970.77 cr versus reconstructed industrial revenue of ₹820.63 cr. Ordinary Other Income is ₹9.55 cr. PAT is ₹61.92 cr against ₹71.87 cr; EPS ₹12.52 times 4.9437963 cr shares gives approximately ₹61.90 cr. The coincidental equality with the other SKF company's rounded current consolidated PAT does not make them the same entity or the same earnings stream.

FY26 industrial revenue was ₹3,440.36 cr, Other Income ₹59.12 cr, exceptional expenses ₹196.10 cr and PAT ₹217.67 cr. Annual EPS ₹44.03 times the issued shares gives approximately ₹217.68 cr. The exceptional costs include substantial land-transfer expense associated with the separation. March PAT of ₹118.97 cr also includes a ₹29.00 cr tax credit; a note identifies a ₹55.66 cr adjustment relating to pre-demerger profit tax. These details make a simple March-times-four earnings estimate unsafe.

Some summary historical EPS values use a tiny pre-allotment denominator and become economically absurd. Those values do not describe the earning power of today's ordinary share. Use the statutory scheme-adjusted denominator and explain the reconstruction. More generally, a newly listed demerged company can have an old operating business and a short security history at the same time. Its plant experience does not create missing market-price observations, while its recent listing does not erase its industrial track record.

What the separation could change

Separate management attention and capital allocation may make the automotive and industrial businesses easier to evaluate. Their customer cycles, import requirements, product mix and cash needs can diverge. But separation itself creates no automatic increase in bearing demand. The investor must track independent overheads, related-party arrangements, procurement terms, brand and technology access, tax settlement and the assets transferred. A sum-of-parts story is credible only when those economic boundaries are understood.

For the first clean comparisons, prefer continuing-business revenue and operating costs, with a separate ledger of separation charges and tax effects. Monitor whether industrial growth converts to cash after stock and receivables, and whether automotive growth retains margin without relying on higher Other Income. A lower profit base caused by one-time costs can produce attractive future growth percentages even if the underlying business merely returns to normal. That is an earnings-base effect, not proof of a new competitive advantage.

Galaxy: sanctions removed, recovery still to be earned

The June filing gives revenue of ₹15.9492 cr, Other Income of ₹2.9303 cr, PBT of ₹3.9751 cr and PAT of ₹2.9877 cr. EPS ₹9.40 times 0.318 cr shares gives ₹2.9892 cr. FY26 PAT was ₹3.3099 cr, consistent with EPS ₹10.41 and the same shares. A search headline calling that annual profit ₹33.1 cr is ten times too large. The primary filing and share arithmetic resolve the unit error without requiring an opinion about the company's prospects.

Other Income is large relative to pretax profit. Subtracting ₹2.9303 cr from reported PBT leaves ₹1.0448 cr before considering whether every item in that remainder is recurring. The filing also identifies ₹0.969 cr of legal costs associated with the sanctions matter. It would be inconsistent to remove those costs while leaving all non-operating income in a supposed normal operating profit. A recovery model should show both adjustments explicitly and apply a defensible tax assumption rather than subtracting pretax items directly from PAT.

OFAC designated Galaxy on 30 October 2024 and removed it on 30 June 2026. The removal is confirmed by the official action, not inferred from a company's share-price rise. 8 · Critical inputs and where inflation lands

Bearing steel quality, brass, polymers, lubricants, energy, tooling and precision machinery all influence cost. The relevant steel is not simply whatever benchmark steel price appears in a newspaper. Cleanliness, chemistry, heat-treatment response and consistency matter because the material will undergo repeated contact stress. A cheaper input can raise rejection, shorten service life or invalidate qualification. The correct purchasing question is delivered cost per accepted part, including process yield and working capital, rather than price per tonne alone.

For a cage producer, brass or steel prices can move revenue even without a corresponding change in physical output. If the customer reimburses metal through a formula, higher revenue may mostly pass through higher material cost. The margin percentage can fall while contribution per unit remains stable; alternatively, a lagged reset can hurt both contribution and cash. Harsha's June call discusses raw-material increases of 6–8% and working-capital pressure. Its observed margin decline is a reason to ask about reset timing, not to assume the whole increase was immediately recovered.

For a ring producer, machining allowance and scrap recovery matter alongside the steel invoice. Buying more metal than ends in the finished ring is normal in many manufacturing routes, but the amount of recoverable scrap and its sale price affect net material cost. A part redesign can improve yield without changing the selling price per ring. Conversely, a difficult small-batch programme can require tooling changes and more inspection. The Rolex report provides the company context; a model should separate volume, mix and material pass-through before assigning margin expansion to a stronger moat.

Energy is consumed in heat treatment and processing, while precision equipment creates depreciation and maintenance expense. When utilisation is low, fixed plant costs are spread over fewer saleable units. When a plant is full, overtime, outsourced processing or accelerated maintenance can limit the apparent operating leverage. Neither a fixed-cost ratio nor nameplate capacity is a complete forecast. The investor needs bottleneck-level information: a finishing machine or inspection station can constrain output even if upstream furnaces have spare capacity.

Imports add currency and lead-time exposure. A local manufacturer may still import specialised components, machinery or finished products from its parent. A weaker rupee can help export realisations while increasing those costs. The net exposure depends on invoice currencies, hedges and contractual pass-through, not on whether the company calls itself an exporter. Likewise, a global parent can support supply reliability but also influence transfer prices and the product mandate allocated to India.

Inventory deserves a technical reading. Raw material stocks may secure a qualified source; work in progress may reflect a long manufacturing route; finished goods may support a replacement promise. Each can be commercially sensible. The question is whether the stock turns within its planned cycle and remains usable. A customer redesign, obsolete variant or cancelled programme can turn strategic stock into a write-down. Benara's inventory loss shows why the balance-sheet asset needs evidence of saleability, not merely an auditor's arithmetic.

Apply the 081 quote-to-delivery test to a bearing order by naming the reset reference, frequency, lag and currency. Then apply the 080 processing-depth test to determine which conversion stages the supplier actually owns. A company that passes through metal but performs difficult finishing has different risk from one that quotes a fixed price before buying most of its material. Both may report the same revenue growth during an inflationary quarter.

9 · The operating calendar is not the share-price calendar

OEM bearing demand follows customer production schedules. Automotive platforms, railway procurement, industrial projects and export customers do not share one calendar. Replacement demand depends on machine hours, planned shutdowns, lubrication and stock availability. A manufacturer can have strong maintenance sales during a weak new-equipment year, but that cushioning must be demonstrated in its mix. The existence of an installed base does not make every supplier's revenue recurring or predictable.

Production holidays and customer shutdowns can alter monthly dispatches. A component manufacturer may build stock before a customer's shutdown or receive fewer orders while that customer reduces inventory. Export transit times can shift revenue recognition and receivables across quarter ends. A single weak month can therefore represent timing rather than lost share, while a strong final-quarter dispatch can represent channel stocking rather than end-user demand. Follow the next collection and reorder before deciding which explanation is correct.

There is also a reporting calendar. Schaeffler's June release is a half-year checkpoint within a December year end. Most of this roster reports March financial years. SME reporting can differ, and Benara's available table is half-year based. Compare matching periods and read the date printed above the actual column. A quarterly screen that labels a company “Q1” does not override its statutory year end. This matters for annual reconciliation, seasonality of costs and apparent sequential growth.

The market calendar captures expectations about these businesses, broader liquidity, valuation and company events. The price can rise before order growth appears and fall while current earnings remain strong. It is therefore possible for the basket's April tendency to coexist with a business that has no universal April shipment peak. The statistics in the next section are observations from shares, not direct measurements of factory orders. A causal explanation needs separate operating evidence.

For practical monitoring, use a rolling sequence: customer schedules, company dispatches, reported sales, inventory and receivables, then operating cash flow. If dispatches accelerate but receivables and inventory rise faster, ask whether the growth is building a useful installed position or simply stretching the balance sheet. If sales slow while stock falls and cash rises, destocking may improve near-term cash even as reported growth weakens. The direction of one line never answers the entire cycle question.

11 · What actually moves earnings

Volume and the customer's production mix. A bearing maker may sell more units because vehicle output rises, a new platform ramps up, an industrial plant expands or replacement activity strengthens. Those routes have different durability. A platform launch can create years of supply but also concentration in one customer. An emergency replacement order can be profitable yet non-recurring. Ask for the contribution of existing programmes and new wins separately, and resist applying one headline volume number across different bearing sizes and types.

Value per unit. Better geometry, larger size, tighter precision, integrated seals or difficult materials can raise selling value. So can simple metal inflation. The investor needs a bridge that distinguishes these causes. Harsha's engineering margin pressure despite higher activity is a reminder that a more valuable invoice does not necessarily contain more retained conversion profit. A technical upgrade should eventually show up in contribution, programme durability or customer retention; a higher average price alone does not prove it.

Localisation. Making a product in India that was previously imported can reduce transit time and improve response, but the listed company must have the mandate and process capability to make it. The global parent may retain certain products elsewhere. Imported inputs, royalties, development charges and related-party purchasing can limit the local value captured. Compare local production growth with bought-out product sales and capital employed. A company can grow total revenue by importing more without building the same manufacturing return.

Factory utilisation and yield. New machinery brings depreciation, people, trials and qualification costs before steady sales. As accepted output rises, fixed costs may spread more efficiently. That leverage is conditional on quality and demand. A factory running quickly with high rejection does not have attractive effective utilisation. Track accepted units, scrap, rework and customer returns together. Management's capacity number should specify whether it refers to installed equipment, qualified capacity or expected saleable output.

Aftermarket access. A strong replacement channel can sell convenience, authenticity and technical support. It may have a different price structure from OEM supply. But stocking and dealer credit consume cash, and the company may compete with independent distributors or counterfeit goods. Schaeffler's consolidated distribution exposure illustrates why channel expansion must be assessed through both commercial reach and subsidiary economics. Access to the customer is valuable only if it produces a sustainable return after the cost of serving that customer.

Finance and balance-sheet capacity. A company with losses and negative net worth faces a different cycle from a cash-generative supplier. It may need promoter funding before a new order can be fulfilled. Faster growth can then increase financing need rather than solve it. NRB Industrial's support dependence and Benara's write-offs require a financing analysis alongside product demand. A sector upturn can help operating leverage, but it cannot retroactively turn impaired inventory into collectible cash.

Exceptional events. Insurance receipts, tax settlements, buybacks, demergers, sanctions and legal expenses can move reported earnings or valuation independently of bearing consumption. NRB's insurance gain, SKF's separation costs, Rolex's recompense expense and Galaxy's legal history all matter in this roster. Maintain an event ledger with date, pretax amount, tax effect and recurrence. That ledger allows a reader to distinguish an improving operation from a cleaner comparison base without pretending the exceptional cash cost never occurred.

The practical model starts with a small number of drivers for each company. For Harsha, engineering volume, conversion margin, foreign operations and working capital are central. For Rolex, customer schedules, ring mix, material pass-through and the exceptional bridge matter. For the multinational subsidiaries, local product mandates, bought-out sales, technology access and cash conversion deserve attention. For distressed small names, financing runway and break-even come before optimistic market-share assumptions. A uniform spreadsheet would conceal these differences.

13 · Global competition and the Indian opportunity

The bearing industry competes across global branded suppliers, regional finished-bearing makers, specialist component producers and low-cost replacement channels. India participates through local subsidiaries of global groups, domestic manufacturers and exports of rings, cages and rolling elements. The opportunity is not one undifferentiated transfer of factories from another country. Each product requires an economic case, a qualified process and a customer willing to allocate business.

Global groups can bring design knowledge, validation methods, application support and multinational customers. Their Indian listed subsidiaries may benefit from that network. Yet the shareholder owns the local legal entity, not the whole global technology portfolio. A product announced by a foreign parent may not be manufactured, sold or monetised by the Indian company. Read the local report, related-party transactions and product mandate before attributing global innovation to Indian earnings. The SKF industrial report, Timken report and Schaeffler filing establish the relevant local boundaries.

Domestic suppliers can compete through cost, flexibility, shorter lead times, specialised knowledge and customer relationships. Small scale can help serve difficult low-volume variants, but can limit investment in testing, automation and inventory. A large customer may appreciate flexibility while retaining price leverage. Export revenue demonstrates market access; it does not establish pricing power or diversified demand. Identify customers, programme duration and settlement currencies where disclosed.

For Harsha and Rolex, localisation by a global customer can create opportunity even when the final brand remains foreign. However, global purchasing can rebalance sourcing among plants. A new Indian programme may substitute for another location rather than represent incremental worldwide demand. Supplier growth can be real either way, but the risks differ: share gain depends on qualification and cost, whereas market growth depends on end demand. A forecast should state which it assumes.

Harsha's overseas operations show that geographic expansion carries execution costs as well as customer access. The management call discusses performance across locations and work needed to improve them. A foreign plant is not automatically a hedge against domestic weakness if it serves the same customer cycle or has a structurally higher cost base. Track operating contribution and cash needs by location rather than celebrating the number of countries on a map.

Galaxy adds a different international risk: legal and banking access can interrupt a technically viable export business. Official sanctions removal changes that constraint, but customers may take time to restore purchasing and payment arrangements. Read the annual report alongside the official removal action. This is a company-specific reopening case; it is not evidence that all Indian bearing exports were sanctioned or that every earlier commercial effect has disappeared.

Country leadership varies by application. Large industrial bearings, high-speed motor bearings, precision spindle bearings and standard replacements demand different capabilities. Without a current, well-defined dataset, this primer does not assign unsupported market-share percentages or rank countries across the whole industry. For this listed universe, ask which products are being localised, which remain imported, and which company receives the revenue and capital burden.

14 · Technology: life, friction and electrification

Lower friction can reduce energy loss, but performance balances load capacity, stiffness, speed, sealing, lubrication, life and cost. A seal that excludes contaminants may add drag. A lubricant suited to one temperature or speed may perform poorly in another. Reducing clearance without considering temperature and fit can create problems. The SKF selection material explains why the surrounding machine and operating conditions belong in the design process.

Lubrication and contamination

A rolling contact needs suitable lubrication for its intended duty. Too little lubricant, unsuitable viscosity, contamination or incorrect replenishment can reduce useful life. More grease is not automatically better: quantity and replenishment must match the arrangement. The SKF explanation of grease mechanisms shows why lubricant is not incidental. Technical support can create value by preventing an avoidable failure rather than simply selling a more expensive bearing.

Contaminants can damage raceways and alter contact conditions. Filtration, seals, mounting cleanliness and storage affect service results. Failure in a dirty assembly is not necessarily proof of bad bearing steel; blaming installation also does not eliminate responsibility to specify a suitable arrangement. SKF's contamination discussion supports the importance of the environment. The commercial question is whether the supplier converts this knowledge into repeat relationships, paid services or stronger product acceptance.

Electrification changes the product mix

Battery electric vehicles remove some engine-related applications while creating or changing demand in motors, reduction gears, wheel ends and other systems. Menon's engine plain-bearing exposure needs a different transition analysis from high-speed motor bearings. Neither “EVs need no bearings” nor “every bearing company benefits equally” is credible. The model needs existing application mix, addressable products, approved programmes and capital required to reach them.

High-speed electric motors can introduce demanding noise, lubrication and electrical-damage requirements. Current passing through a bearing can damage contact surfaces; insulation or hybrid designs may address particular applications. The NSK technical journal discusses engineering developments rather than promising revenue to an Indian issuer. Before assigning an EV premium, establish that the listed company has the product, customer approval and local rights to earn from it.

A component maker can participate without selling a whole motor bearing. A cage design, ring or precision rolling element may be relevant. But the component must be approved for the new conditions, and the programme must replace or exceed disappearing legacy content. “EV compatible” is an early capability claim, not an earnings forecast. Ask for commercial production rather than treating development projects as booked revenue.

Measurement and condition monitoring

Automation can improve consistency, measurement and traceability. It can also increase fixed capital and skilled-maintenance requirements. A new line creates value when it raises accepted output, reduces scrap or enables a higher-value approved product. Measure the return after depreciation, tooling, software, people and working capital. A machine photograph establishes equipment exists; it does not establish economic utilisation or a customer obligation to buy its output.

Condition monitoring uses information such as vibration and temperature to identify developing problems. It can support maintenance decisions where suitable. This may deepen customer relationships, yet can extend replacement intervals by preventing avoidable failures. Not every reliability improvement increases bearing unit demand. Value may appear in service revenue, retention or premium products; the company must demonstrate how it captures that value.

Life calculations are design tools with stated assumptions, not unconditional warranties. Field life depends on installation, loading, lubrication and contamination. A catalogue life number cannot be compared across products without understanding its basis. The financial counterpart is the warranty and failure-cost record. Claims of superior performance should be supported by validation, customer acceptance and field experience consistent with commercial disclosures.

15 · Policy, qualification and authenticity

Standards, customer approvals and government rules are different things. A customer may require a validation procedure or approved source without any blanket import restriction. A quality-management certificate is not approval of every product for every rail, aerospace or automotive application. Identify the scope, product, site and validity of an approval before using it as evidence of a competitive barrier.

The DPIIT Bearings Quality Control Order document reviewed here has a blank notification number and Gazette date. It is a draft, not sufficient evidence of an enacted blanket mandatory regime. It indicates a policy proposal; this primer does not convert that into guaranteed import substitution. A valid claim requires the final notification, products covered, effective dates, exemptions and enforcement.

Even an effective rule would not automatically award demand to listed domestic manufacturers. Suppliers may obtain certification, customers may qualify alternatives, and some products may remain difficult to localise. Beneficiaries depend on approved range and saleable capacity. A narrow portfolio cannot be assigned the value of the entire import market. The relevant evidence is an order won and produced under the actual rule.

Counterfeit bearings differ from genuine low-cost competition. The World Bearing Association's initiative encourages authentication and authorised sourcing. Counterfeits can compromise reliability and traceability. Trusted channels can support a brand, but channel control and customer education cost money. No unsupported estimate of the counterfeit market's monetary size is used here.

Trade and sanctions interpretation requires dates and company-specific facts. Galaxy's removal on 30 June 2026 is verified. It does not imply that every bank and customer immediately restored normal terms. Repeating the old designation without the removal would also misstate the position. Monitor order conversion, payment access, collections and the cost of rebuilding business.

Related-party arrangements influence both operations and governance. An Indian subsidiary may buy products, technology or services from its group and sell abroad within that group. These transactions create access but influence locally retained profit. Read disclosed royalties, fees and transaction volumes. Global brand strength does not replace an understanding of what Indian minority shareholders own and what they pay the parent to use.

18 · Proxies and valuation

No single listed proxy captures every bearing application. Multinational subsidiaries have different local mandates. NRB includes a controlled listed subsidiary. Harsha and Rolex supply components; Menon includes plain bearings and other manufacturing. An investor seeking industrial replacement demand should not automatically choose the name containing “bearing” without checking product and customer mix.

Match enterprise value with consistently defined operating earnings and equity value with owner earnings. Treat treasury income, surplus assets, minority interests and debt consistently. If an exceptional expense is removed, establish whether the cash payment remains a claim on value. A sum-of-parts calculation cannot erase a loss-making subsidiary while retaining the value of its growth plan without the capital it needs.

Value SKF's current entities using their own continuing businesses. Do not use predecessor profit to make one successor look cheap. Recognise Timken's GGB perimeter. Do not add SNL's whole value to a consolidated NRB valuation without accounting for ownership already embedded. Schaeffler's acquired distribution business needs an assessment of future cash requirements as well as strategic benefit.

Component-supplier revenue multiples are vulnerable to metal pass-through. Higher steel or brass prices can increase sales without more retained profit. Conversion contribution, incremental capital returns and cash after maintenance requirements are more informative. A new plant can depress current returns temporarily, but the forecast must show the volume, margin and investment needed for recovery. Calling depreciation a growth expense is not sufficient reason to ignore it.

For distressed firms, a low price-to-book ratio is unhelpful if assets require write-downs or new equity is needed. Benara's stale quote cannot establish executable cheapness. NRB Industrial needs financing analysis. For SKP's tiny profit, small earnings changes cause large multiple changes. 19 · Questions worth asking on a results call

  1. What changed in accepted physical output? Separate existing programmes, approvals, price increases and material pass-through. Where a single unit measure is unsuitable, provide a consistent product-family bridge. Explain why contribution moved differently from sales.
  2. Which capacity is customer-approved? Distinguish installed equipment, trials, qualification and commercial shipments. Identify the bottleneck and avoid counting one programme in several plants. This tests whether capacity can produce accepted, invoiced output.
  3. When do input resets reach invoices? State material, currency, index, lag and revenue coverage. Explain old-cost stock effects. Saying contracts have pass-through does not establish that cash and margin are protected during a price change.
  4. How much cash funds growth? Split raw material, work in progress, finished goods and aged receivables. Link increases to confirmed demand or explain delays. Give milestones for normalisation rather than a general assurance.
  5. What profit belongs to owners? Reconcile group PAT, associates and minority interest. Supply the tax treatment of each adjustment. NRB illustrates why group PAT and the EPS numerator are not necessarily identical.
  6. What is the clean SKF base? Show each continuing operation's sales, costs and capital. Identify remaining separation payments and tax adjustments. This prevents predecessor earnings from being reused in a successor valuation.
  7. What does new capacity earn? For Harsha and SKP, separate established and ramping units. Report accepted dispatches, rejection, losses and cash required to break even. Customer logos alone do not establish commercial economics.
  8. What resumed after Galaxy's removal? Separate legal clearance, banking access, orders, shipments and collections. Show operating profit apart from Other Income and legal costs. This makes recovery measurable.
  9. Who owns the new technology rights? Specify Indian manufacture, resale, royalties and approvals. Global announcements cannot be counted as local earnings until the listed company's role is established.
  10. Will the accounting inconsistency be corrected? Vishal needs a PAT–OCI–EPS bridge; SKP needs a quarterly-to-annual bridge and clarity on overseas information. A corrected filing is stronger evidence than an informal reassurance.
  11. What finances recovery? For loss-making companies, specify support, debt terms, dilution and cash break-even. A revenue target without funding is incomplete where the business is already consuming capital.
  12. What would delay the next project? Ask for return thresholds, commitments and downside assumptions. A credible answer ties investment to demand and financing capacity, avoiding another layer of fixed cost before the first earns its return.

20 · Myths and the evidence that resolves them

“Wear makes demand automatically recurring.” Replacement timing depends on operating hours, maintenance and application. The company must own the qualified replacement position or channel. A ring supplier does not automatically receive future aftermarket orders for the complete machine. Look for repeat demand and profitable distribution.

“A critical part guarantees pricing power.” Criticality creates switching friction, but customers can maintain multiple approved sources and demand cost reductions. Test retained conversion margin, customer durability, capital allocation and cash. Technical importance explains the need; financial outcomes show who keeps the value.

“EVs destroy all bearing demand.” Some engine content is exposed, while motors, gears and wheel ends need suitable motion products. Analyse existing revenue at risk, approved replacements, investment and time to production. Neither blanket collapse nor blanket benefit describes this roster.

“The lowest PE is the cheapest share.” Other Income, tax credits, insurance and changed denominators can distort earnings. Stale prices may be untradeable. Subsidiary losses may require cash. Start with normalised owner earnings and claims on the balance sheet.

“Localisation guarantees a windfall.” The company needs product rights, process capability, approval and economic returns. Draft policy is not enacted protection. Imported inputs and group fees can limit value retained locally. Commercial shipments and returns are stronger evidence than a theoretical market size.

“A strong basket proves a broad upcycle.” Several shares and smaller-company accounts remain weak in 2026. Removing Menon materially changes the headline. The 2018 record also shows an average supported by one large mover while the median was negative. Test breadth, earnings and cash.

“A clean move scan validates corporate actions.” SKF disproves that shortcut. A vendor can adjust a chart smoothly while the shareholder's economic holding changes. Verify entitlements and the complete package; when that cannot be done, disclose the exclusion.

21 · Glossary

Parts, loads and applications

Bearing
A component that supports relative movement while carrying load. Rolling and plain bearings achieve this through different mechanisms and need different application and material analysis.
Rolling bearing
An assembly using balls or rollers between raceways. Its performance depends on the complete arrangement, including load, fit, lubricant, sealing and the surrounding machine.
Plain bearing
A bearing based on sliding contact rather than rolling elements. Depending on design and duty, an oil film or suitable bearing material supports the moving surfaces.
Inner ring
The ring generally fitted around the shaft. Its raceway, dimensions and fit contribute to load distribution and operating performance; outside dimensions alone do not define suitability.
Outer ring
The ring generally mounted within the housing. Housing accuracy and fit affect the assembly, so a sound bearing cannot always compensate for an unsuitable surrounding structure.
Raceway
The contact path on which rolling elements travel. Surface condition and geometry matter because repeated load passes through this small contact region during operation.
Rolling element
A ball or roller that transmits load between raceways. Precision and consistency across a batch are important to assembled performance and customer acceptance.
Cage
The part that separates and guides rolling elements. Harsha supplies this component layer; a cage supplier does not automatically own the finished-bearing brand or replacement channel.
Deep-groove ball bearing
A common ball-bearing geometry used across many rotating applications. Actual suitability still depends on speed, load, clearance, sealing, lubricant and customer requirements.
Angular-contact bearing
A bearing whose contact geometry suits combined loading. Arrangement and preload influence performance, making it an application-engineering decision rather than a simple dimensional substitution.
Tapered roller bearing
A roller bearing with tapered elements and raceways, suited to combinations of radial and axial load. Wheel-end and heavy-equipment duties are relevant application examples.
Cylindrical roller bearing
A bearing using cylindrical rollers, often selected for substantial radial load. Particular designs differ in their axial-load and displacement capabilities.
Spherical roller bearing
A roller-bearing arrangement that can accommodate misalignment within its design limits. That capability does not remove the need for correct mounting and operating conditions.
Needle bearing
A bearing using long, slender rollers where radial space is constrained. Compact geometry and precise manufacture make it a distinct product category within this roster.
Bush
A sleeve-like component supporting sliding movement or guiding a moving part. Its material and lubrication requirements depend on application; it is not necessarily a rolling bearing.
Thrust washer
A component used to support or control axial movement in an assembly. Menon’s product context includes such parts alongside its bearing activities.
Radial load
Force acting across the shaft axis. The magnitude and pattern of loading help determine bearing selection and cannot be inferred solely from shaft speed.
Axial load
Force acting along the shaft axis. A machine may impose radial and axial loads together, requiring a suitable bearing arrangement.
Preload
An intentionally applied internal loading condition in an arrangement. It can influence stiffness and running behaviour, but requires engineering control rather than arbitrary tightening.
Internal clearance
The relative movement available within a bearing before operation. Fits and temperatures affect operating clearance, so catalogue clearance is only part of the selection problem.
Wheel end
The assembly around a vehicle wheel’s support and rotation. Bearing requirements depend on the vehicle and assembly design rather than on a universal standard unit.
Engine bearing
A bearing serving an internal-combustion engine application, often a plain-bearing design. Its transition risk differs from bearings used in electric motors and wheel ends.

Manufacturing, reliability and technology

Forged ring
A ring formed through a forging route before further processing. Rolex’s role in this layer differs from selling the final assembled, branded bearing.
Heat treatment
Controlled heating and cooling to develop required material properties. Distortion, consistency and subsequent processing matter to the amount of accepted output.
Grinding
Precision material removal used to achieve required dimensions and surfaces. It can be an important process bottleneck rather than merely a finishing expense.
Superfinishing
A finishing process used to improve contact-surface characteristics. It adds processing depth beyond metal weight and can support demanding customer specifications.
Roundness
How closely a circular component meets its intended circular form. Small geometric errors can matter in repeated rolling contact even when they are visually inconspicuous.
Tolerance
The permitted variation around a specified dimension or property. Tighter tolerances matter commercially only when the company can maintain them at accepted production yield.
Surface finish
The condition and texture of a manufactured surface. In a bearing contact it interacts with lubrication and operating conditions, making appearance alone an insufficient test.
Metrology
The measurement discipline used to verify dimensions and other requirements. Reliable measurement helps prevent defective output from reaching the customer.
Traceability
The ability to connect a supplied part with its material batch and production history. It supports quality investigation, customer requirements and credible failure analysis.
Yield
The proportion of input or production that becomes acceptable output. Higher nominal capacity can have weak economics if scrap and rework consume the apparent benefit.
Scrap recovery
Value recovered from material not retained in saleable parts. Its amount and price influence a ring producer’s net material economics alongside the purchase price.
Rework
Additional processing needed to bring a part within acceptance requirements. It consumes time and money and can reduce effective capacity even when the part is eventually sold.
Qualification
Customer acceptance of a product and production process for a specific use. Approval can take time and may need revisiting after material, equipment or site changes.
Saleable capacity
Output that can meet customer requirements and be sold. It is more useful economically than a machine’s theoretical production rate without yield and approval constraints.
Lubricant film
The lubricating layer supporting separation of moving surfaces under suitable conditions. Its adequacy depends on the lubricant and actual operating duty.
Viscosity
A fluid’s resistance to flow. Lubricant viscosity must suit the operating arrangement; a higher number is not an automatic improvement for every bearing.
Contamination
Unwanted material in or around the bearing and lubricant. It can damage contact surfaces, making sealing, handling and cleanliness commercially important.
Misalignment
A departure from the intended alignment of components. Some designs accommodate limited misalignment, but that does not excuse an unsuitable housing or installation.
Electrical erosion
Contact-surface damage associated with current passing through a bearing. It is relevant to some electrified applications and can require specialised design responses.
Hybrid bearing
A bearing combining different material types, commonly ceramic rolling elements with steel rings in relevant designs. Commercial suitability depends on the particular duty and cost.
Condition monitoring
Observation of indicators such as vibration or temperature to identify developing problems. It can support maintenance and customer relationships without necessarily increasing replacement units.
Rating life
A calculated life measure under defined assumptions. It helps selection but should not be read as an unconditional promise of field life in any environment.

Commercial economics and accounting

OEM
Original equipment manufacturer: the customer building a new vehicle or machine. Its production schedule and commercial terms differ from replacement-market purchasing.
Aftermarket
Demand arising after original equipment is sold, including replacement parts and support. Capturing it requires channel access, stock, authenticity and customer trust.
Programme
A defined customer product or application supplied over a period. Winning a new part number within the same customer does not necessarily diversify purchasing risk.
Localisation
Moving or developing a supply activity locally. For shareholders, the key is which legal company receives the product mandate, investment obligation and resulting profit.
Material pass-through
A pricing mechanism that transfers some input-cost change to customers. Coverage, reference price, timing and lag determine whether it protects margin and cash.
Conversion contribution
Value retained after relevant material costs to cover processing and other costs. It can be more informative than revenue when metal prices pass through invoices.
Operating leverage
The effect of spreading relatively fixed costs over changing output. It helps only when additional production is accepted and sold at sufficient contribution.
Working capital
Funds tied in operating assets such as stock and receivables after relevant operating liabilities. Growth can absorb cash even while the income statement improves.
Receivable ageing
The distribution of customer balances by how long they remain unpaid. It helps distinguish ordinary credit from collection problems and potential impairment.
Other Income
Income reported apart from operating revenue. Interest, gains and other items require examination before PAT is treated as evidence of manufacturing profitability.
Exceptional item
A separately identified unusual item in the accounts. Its label does not determine its cash effect or justify automatically removing it from valuation.
PAT
Profit after tax. Check its units, reporting entity and ownership attribution before comparing it with EPS or using it in an equity multiple.
Owner profit
The portion of group profit attributable to the listed parent’s shareholders. NRB’s minority interests show why this can differ from total consolidated PAT.
Minority interest
The part of a consolidated subsidiary’s economics belonging to outside shareholders. It must be separated when matching group earnings to the parent’s EPS.
Associate profit
The investor’s recognised share of an associate’s result. It is not the same as operating sales or cash dividends received during the period.
OCI
Other comprehensive income, presented separately from net profit. Vishal’s filing appears to use it in printed EPS, creating a material reconciliation issue.
EPS
Earnings per share, using the appropriate earnings numerator and weighted share denominator. Stock splits, buybacks, minorities and rounding all require attention.
Continuing operations
The business remaining after a disposal or separation. SKF’s comparable automotive figures must be distinguished from the predecessor’s combined industrial and automotive results.
Common-control combination
A combination involving businesses under common control. Timken’s GGB transaction resulted in restated consolidated comparatives, changing the historical financial perimeter.
Qualified review
An auditor’s review conclusion containing a qualification. Read the actual basis and affected information rather than assuming every number has identical assurance.
Going concern
The assumption that a business can continue operating. Negative net worth and dependence on support require analysis of funding and evidence, not merely a standard accounting phrase.
Return on incremental capital
The earnings or cash generated by additional investment. It tests whether growth creates value after machinery, qualification and working-capital requirements.

Price data, governance and policy

ISIN
A security identification code used here to distinguish legal shares. Similar names, such as NRB Bearings and NRB Industrial, do not establish identical ownership.
Demerger
A separation of a business into another legal entity. Shareholder return may require tracking both resulting securities rather than one successor chart.
Share split
A change in the number and face value of shares without the same proportional change in business value. Price and EPS history require consistent adjustment.
Buyback
A company’s repurchase of shares. The timing changes the appropriate denominator; later outstanding shares should not automatically be multiplied by an earlier quarter’s EPS.
Splice
Joining price histories for the same security across exchanges using an overlap. Austin and Menon needed this to preserve older BSE history before NSE coverage.
Stale quote
An old trade displayed as the latest price because no newer trade is available. It cannot be silently carried forward to satisfy a fixed-date basket requirement.
Trading gap
A long interval without observations. It can weaken the interpretation of returns and realistic execution, especially in small and illiquid securities.
Equal weighting
Giving available constituents the same weight at a reset. It gives small companies more influence than a market-cap-weighted industry benchmark would.
Monthly rebalancing
Resetting portfolio weights each month. Its compounded basket return need not equal the simple average of constituents’ full-year returns.
Price-only return
Return calculated from share prices without adding dividends. It differs from a total-return measure of the shareholder’s full economic experience.
Survivorship bias
The distortion from using today’s surviving roster to describe the past. Failed, removed or otherwise absent historical companies are not automatically represented.
Return clipping
Capping extreme observations before aggregation. This pipeline clips individual monthly returns at ±50%, so the basket is not a literal unconstrained investment record.
Excess return
The basket return minus the comparator return for the same period. A positive basket return can still be negative excess return if the Nifty rises more.
Hit rate
The share of observed periods in which the basket beats the comparator. It must be read with the denominator, magnitude and sample limitations.
Median
The middle observation in an ordered sample. It reduces the influence of extreme values but cannot eliminate selection bias or a small sample.
Sensitivity test
Recomputing results after a justified change in assumptions or members. Removing Menon or the historically gapped names shows which conclusions depend on composition.
Reporting perimeter
The legal businesses included in a financial figure. Comparing different perimeters can create false growth, particularly after acquisitions or demergers.
Related-party transaction
A transaction with a connected entity. Product purchases, royalties and services can influence how much of a global group’s economics remains with the listed subsidiary.
QCO
Quality Control Order. A draft proposal is not proof of an effective final rule; product coverage, notification dates and exemptions need verification.
OFAC designation
A sanctions action by the US Treasury’s Office of Foreign Assets Control. Galaxy’s historical designation must be read together with its June 2026 removal.
Counterfeit bearing
A product falsely represented as another supplier’s genuine bearing. Authentication and trusted sourcing address a different problem from legitimate low-cost competition.
Investment scenario
A conditional estimate built from stated operating and financing assumptions. It is not management guidance converted into a guaranteed outcome.

It supplies cages and engineering components, while the final bearing producer owns assembly and often the replacement relationship. Qualification can be valuable without giving Harsha the whole downstream margin.

Half-year operating cash flow declined while inventory and receivables absorbed cash. The acquired distribution subsidiary also needs separate monitoring.

The reviewed document lacks a final notification number and date. Enactment, product scope, timing and company capability must be established before assuming protected demand.

Specification and replacement position are tested at the finished bearing; processing depth at cages, rollers and rings; quote-to-delivery risk at material resets. The earlier primers provide the underlying frameworks.

Customer-approved production, accepted shipments, sustainable contribution and cash conversion should improve. Equipment installation or a customer logo alone does not demonstrate a return on invested capital.
