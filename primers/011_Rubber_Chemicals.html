Rubber Chemicals

Raw rubber is sticky in summer and brittle in winter. Add sulphur and a pinch of accelerators and antioxidants, heat it, and it becomes a tyre that lasts 50,000 km. This industry makes those pinches and sells most of them to tyre companies.

1 · The industry in 60 seconds

The spices that make rubber work

Explain it to a 10-year-old

Natural rubber from a tree is like chewing gum: soft, sticky, and gooey when hot. In 1839 Charles Goodyear found that cooking rubber with sulphur makes it strong and bouncy. That cooking is called vulcanisation. On its own it takes hours, so factories add accelerators that make it happen in minutes, like baking powder in a cake. They also add antioxidants so the rubber doesn't crack in sun and air, like sunscreen for tyres. These chemicals are a tiny part of a tyre, but without them there would be no tyres, no hoses, no shoe soles and no rubber gloves.

Say it to a client in one minute

Rubber chemicals (accelerators, antidegradants, insoluble sulphur and processing aids) are sold mainly to tyre makers. It's a volume business tied to tyre production, but prices are set by Chinese exporters: China Sunsine alone calls itself the world's largest accelerator maker. India has three listed plays.

  • NOCIL is the national champion. Its margin fell from ~30% (FY18–19) to ~10% as Chinese dumping intensified, and a five-year anti-dumping duty on sulphenamide accelerators from June 2026 is the first real defence. Q1 FY27 profit rose 61% and the margin reached 11.2%.
  • OCCL is one of a handful of insoluble-sulphur makers globally. The 2026 sulphur and acid spike tripled its profit.
  • Sunshield is mostly surfactants plus antioxidants.

The industry's structural question is 6PPD, the antiozonant in every tyre, whose breakdown product kills salmon. California is forcing an alternative, and whoever makes the replacement wins a global product.

Analogy

Think of rubber as dough. Sulphur is the heat that sets it, accelerators are the baking powder that makes it set fast and evenly, antioxidants are the preservative that stops it going stale, and the PVI is the fridge that stops the dough rising before you're ready. Insoluble sulphur is a special flour that doesn't leak out of the dough before baking.

Everyday examples

Every car, bike and truck tyre in India likely contains NOCIL or Chinese CBS/TBBS. The brown bloom on an old tyre sidewall is 6PPD doing its job, moving to the surface to meet ozone. Rubber gloves, hot-water bottles, conveyor belts, V-belts, hoses, shoe soles and condoms all need accelerators too, often the fast-acting thiurams and dithiocarbamates.

Each stage for a 10-year-old

  • Raw materials: aniline is made from benzene (from oil) and is the backbone of most accelerators and antioxidants. Sulphur comes out of refineries when they clean crude oil.
  • Rubber chemicals: factories react these into white or grey powders and pellets, each with one job: speed, protection or timing.
  • Compounding: a tyre plant mixes rubber, carbon black and about a dozen chemicals in a huge mixer, like a giant dough kneader. Every tyre model has its own recipe.
  • Tyres: the mixed rubber is shaped, layered with steel and fabric, and cooked in a mould for about 10–15 minutes. Accelerators are what make that time possible.
  • Vehicles: new cars take new tyres, and old ones need replacements. Replacement demand keeps going even when car sales slow.
Fund-manager lens

A tyre maker spends little on rubber chemicals compared with natural rubber and carbon black, so quality and reliability matter more than a few rupees. That makes approvals sticky, taking 6–18 months per product per plant. But once several suppliers are approved, they compete on price, and Chinese capacity is huge. So pricing power exists only when China is disrupted or trade defences apply. Volume is steady; the spread is the swing factor.

Why margins swing so much

  1. Operating leverage: the plants are capital-intensive, with fixed costs spread over steady volumes. A 10% price change on the same volume moves EBITDA by far more.
  2. China sets the marginal price: Chinese capacity is several times India's. When Chinese plants run hard, their export prices fall to cash cost.
  3. Contract lags: global tyre makers often fix quarterly or annual prices, so input spikes hurt first and are passed on later (NOCIL's 2026 price rises followed input costs).
  4. Duty: anti-dumping duty lifts the floor on imports, which lifts domestic prices, but only for the covered products (here CBS and TBBS).
Analogy

A rubber-chemical plant is like a bus on a fixed route. The bus runs full every day (tyre demand is steady), but the ticket price is set by a much bigger rival bus company next door (China). When the rival raises fares, you earn a fortune; when it slashes them, you still have to run the route. Anti-dumping duty is a toll the government charges only the rival's buses.

7 · The business calendar

Tyre schedules drive orders

  • October–March: the peak Indian auto and tyre production season (festive and year-end); tyre makers build inventory ahead of it from August.
  • April–June: replacement tyre demand from fleets before the monsoon; export tyre schedules.
  • Chinese New Year (January–February): Chinese plants pause, and export prices can firm for a few weeks.
  • Contracts: global tyre makers set many annual or quarterly contracts in Q4 of the calendar year.
  • Results: the tyre makers' quarterly volume commentary (MRF, Apollo, CEAT) is the best real-time read.

Reading it: April (12 of 15), June (11 of 15) and July (12 of 16) are the strong months, and April–July beat the Nifty in 11 of 15 years with a median of +19.5 points, one of the strongest windows in the library. February (5 of 15) is the weakest month. Take about 3 points off April for the general small-cap effect. The long history is essentially two stocks (NOCIL and Sunshield); OCCL joined after its 2024 demerger, so treat the pattern as indicative.

A tendency, not a rule: small samples, survivorship bias (only names still listed) and equal weighting. Use it for timing context, not as a signal.

The exceptions, and why

  • April misses: 2019 (−8.1), 2023 (−0.7), 2025 (−0.5). 2019 was the Indian auto slowdown; 2025 was the US tariff shock. The best Aprils were 2021 (+21.2), 2014 (+19.8) and 2020 (+19.7).
  • April–July failures: 2013 (−7.3), 2018 (−20.2), 2019 (−25.1), 2022 (−2.9). These were the rupee crisis, the small-cap sell-off with Chinese supply returning, the auto slowdown, and flat 2022. The biggest runs were 2021 (+62.1) and 2020 (+54.7), both post-COVID tyre booms.
  • February–March beat the Nifty in 5 of 15 years. The worst were 2012 (−17.6), 2025 (−17.3) and 2016 (−15.3); the best were 2022 (+25.3) and 2014 (+19.9).
  • August–September is weak (6 of 14); the worst was 2024 (−15.7), when Chinese dumping bit.
  • October–December has big outliers: 2013 (+55.7) and 2017 (+29.4) on the upside; 2025 (−16.1) and 2014 and 2019 (−12.8 each) on the downside.
How to use this

April–July is the clear window: 11 of 15 years, a median of +19.5 points, and a mean of +20.6. It fits tyre makers' pre-monsoon replacement demand and export schedules, but with two stocks for most of the history, treat it as a lean, not a rule. Annual results follow Chinese pricing and, from 2026, the duty.

9 · What moves demand and margins

Seven drivers

  1. Chinese export prices: China Sunsine and other Shandong producers set the world price. Chinese environmental closures (2016–18) and energy curbs (2021) gave Indian makers their best years; Chinese oversupply (2023–25) gave them the worst.
  2. Trade defence: the June 2026 anti-dumping duty on CBS and TBBS from China, the EU and the US, at $75–1,748/t for five years.
  3. Tyre production: Indian OEM and replacement volumes, and tyre exports. India's 2019 auto slowdown and the 2020–21 recovery show up directly.
  4. Input costs: aniline (benzene), sulphur, carbon disulphide, amines, energy.
  5. Global tyre makers' sourcing: Michelin, Bridgestone, Goodyear and Continental diversifying away from China (China+1) add export volume for NOCIL.
  6. Regulation of specific molecules: 6PPD (salmon toxicity), nitrosamine-forming accelerators, zinc in run-off.
  7. Capacity cycles: NOCIL's Dahej expansions and Chinese additions; new capacity during a price downturn deepens it.

The anatomy of a rubber-chemicals cycle

  1. China is disrupted (2017 inspections, 2021 energy curbs). Chinese export prices jump, and NOCIL's margin heads towards 25–30%.
  2. Indian makers expand into the good times (NOCIL's Dahej capex).
  3. China restarts and oversupplies. Prices fall to Chinese cash cost, NOCIL's margin halves or worse, and the stock falls 40–60% (2018–19, 2024–25).
  4. Trade defence or another disruption resets the floor (the 2026 duty).

Patterns that repeat

  • Margins, not volumes, drive the stock. Tyre volumes rarely fall more than a few per cent; NOCIL's margin has moved threefold.
  • Duty announcements move the stock instantly (NOCIL +20% on 22 June 2026), but the earnings effect depends on how much of the portfolio is covered (sulphenamides only).
  • Commodity tailwinds help OCCL differently: a sulphur spike hurts most chemical makers but lifts insoluble sulphur and acid realisations.
  • Indian auto cycles matter. 2019's slowdown was the basket's worst year (−35%).

11 · The global picture

From Akron and Leverkusen to Shandong

The industry grew up next to the tyre makers: Akron, Ohio (Goodyear, Firestone, Monsanto's rubber chemicals) and Germany (Bayer). Consolidation in the 1990s–2000s created Flexsys (originally a Monsanto–Akzo venture) and Lanxess (Bayer's chemicals spin-off, 2004). From the 2000s China built capacity at a scale that now sets world prices, centred in Shandong province, near China's own huge tyre industry.

Leadership shifts, and what caused them

  • US and Germany → China (2000s): China's tyre industry became the world's biggest, and chemical suppliers followed with cheap capacity.
  • China's crackdown (2016–18) → India's opening: NOCIL won global approvals and its margins peaked.
  • China's rebound (2019–25): Chinese exporters regained share on price, and India turned to trade defence.
  • The next shift is regulatory: whoever commercialises a safe 6PPD replacement will set the next global standard.

12 · Where technology is going

Five shifts

  1. Replacing 6PPD: when 6PPD reacts with ozone it forms 6PPD-quinone, which is lethal to coho salmon at tiny concentrations in road run-off. California listed 6PPD in tyres as a "priority product" in 2023, and a final alternatives analysis is due in August 2026. Candidates include Lanxess's CCPD and bio-based options (Flexsys with the USDA), but development will take years. This is the biggest product shift in decades.
  2. Nitrosamine-safe accelerators: some secondary-amine accelerators (TMTD, ZDEC) can form nitrosamines, which are suspected carcinogens. The move is to "safe" alternatives such as TBzTD and ZBEC, especially in Europe and in products that touch skin or food.
  3. Green tyres and silica: low-rolling-resistance tyres use silica instead of some carbon black, which changes the accelerator package (more secondary accelerators and coupling agents).
  4. EV tyres: heavier cars and instant torque mean faster wear and more heat, which raises demand for better antidegradants and cure systems.
  5. Lower zinc: zinc in tyre-wear particles harms aquatic life, so research targets lower zinc-oxide loadings and alternative activators.

17 · Questions for management

Concall checklist

  1. What share of revenue is covered by the new anti-dumping duty? How much have domestic prices moved since June?
  2. What are volumes and realisations by product family, and for domestic versus export customers?
  3. How many approvals are pending with global tyre makers, and when do they convert?
  4. What is the utilisation of the Dahej plants, and the ramp-up plan for the new TDQ plant?
  5. What is your 6PPD strategy: are you developing or licensing an alternative?
  6. (OCCL) How much of the Q1 FY27 jump came from sulphuric acid versus insoluble sulphur? What happens if sulphur prices normalise?
  7. (Sunshield) What share of revenue is rubber and latex antioxidants versus surfactants?

Red flags

  • Capacity added while Chinese prices are falling.
  • Rising receivables from tyre makers.
  • Duty lapses or narrowing in sunset reviews.
  • Commodity-driven profit spikes presented as structural.

18 · Myths and live debates

What you'll hear, and the evidence

ClaimEvidence
"The duty fixes NOCIL."It covers sulphenamides, NOCIL's largest family, but not everything. Management still guides ~10% margins for FY27, far below the old peak.
"Rubber chemicals are a steady, defensive business."Volumes are steady; margins swing threefold with Chinese pricing, and the stock has fallen 35–40% in bad years.
"OCCL's profits are a new normal."A large part comes from sulphur and acid prices that spiked with the Gulf disruption.
"6PPD will be banned soon."No drop-in replacement exists yet. California's process runs through alternatives analyses, and development will take years. It's a slow shift, but a certain one.
"China+1 will move tyre-chemical sourcing to India."It helped in 2017–21. When China cut prices in 2023–25, global tyre makers bought on price again.

20 · Test yourself

Ten questions; answers hidden

1. What does an accelerator do, and why do tyre makers need one?

It cuts vulcanisation from hours to minutes, which makes mass tyre production possible.

2. Why did NOCIL's margin fall from ~30% to ~10%?

Chinese capacity restarted after the 2016–18 crackdown and oversupplied in 2023–25, pushing world prices to Chinese cash cost.

3. What did India do in June 2026, and what does it cover?

It imposed a five-year anti-dumping duty of $75–1,748/t on sulphenamide accelerators (CBS and TBBS/NS) from China, the EU and the US.

4. Why did OCCL's profit triple when sulphur prices spiked?

Sulphur is its input, but higher sulphur and acid prices lifted its insoluble-sulphur and sulphuric-acid selling prices by more.

5. What is the 6PPD problem?

6PPD reacts with ozone to form 6PPD-quinone, which kills coho salmon in road run-off; California requires tyre makers to find alternatives.

6. Why is insoluble sulphur used in radial tyres?

It doesn't bloom to the surface, so it keeps the rubber tacky enough to bond the steel-belt layers.

7. Which was the basket's worst year, and why?

2019 (−35%): India's auto slowdown hit tyre output while Chinese prices fell.

8. Which company elsewhere arguably belongs in this primer?

Yasho Industries (in 008), whose rubber accelerators and antioxidants are a large part of its business.

9. What is the strongest seasonal window?

April–July: 11 of 15 years, median +19.5 points.

10. What guidance did NOCIL give for FY27?

Revenue of ₹1,400–1,600 cr, an EBITDA margin around 10% and volume growth of about 10%.
