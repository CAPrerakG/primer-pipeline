Electronic Components & Materials

Every circuit board carries dozens of small parts besides the chips: resistors that measure current, magnets that make motors and relays work, ferrite cores that tame high-frequency noise, strips of bimetal that switch off an iron when it overheats. This section holds India's listed makers of such precision components and materials — a tiny group, but one sitting on two of the biggest shifts in electronics: smart meters and electric vehicles at home, and China's control of rare-earth magnets abroad.

1 · The industry in 60 seconds

Small parts, specialised materials, sticky customers

Explain it to a 10-year-old

Open any gadget and you will see a green board covered with little parts. The big black square is the chip — the brain. Around it are tiny helpers. Some measure how much electricity flows, like a water meter. Some are magnets that make a motor spin or a switch click. Some are little rings of special ceramic that stop electrical "noise". Others are two metals stuck together that bend when they get hot, turning something off before it burns. These parts are cheap one by one, but they must be perfect, and customers keep buying from the same supplier for years once a part is approved.

Say it to a client in one minute

Indian component makers are few and small, but the best of them sit on structural demand and a supply-chain shift away from China.

  • Demand: smart meters (a shunt resistor in every one), EV battery systems, appliances and industrial controls.
  • Supply shift: China makes about 94% of sintered permanent magnets and restricted exports of heavy rare earths and magnets from April 2025; buyers want second sources.
  • Policy: the component scheme (ECMS) is now ₹40,000 cr with 106 projects approved; a ₹7,280 cr rare-earth magnet scheme received 20 bids in August.
  • Results diverge: Shivalik Bimetal revenue +33%, profit +45%, stock +155%; Permanent Magnets profit −14% on a delayed relay project.
  • Watch the tail: the section's second-largest name by value is a granite trader whose share rose from ₹17 to ₹5,500 in 20 months.

Analogy

If a circuit board is a kitchen, the chip is the chef. The components are the knives, scales, thermometers and fuse boxes — cheap compared with the chef, but the kitchen does not work without them, and a restaurant that has approved a particular thermometer does not change it lightly. That is why component makers with qualified parts often keep customers for decades.

Each stage for a 10-year-old

  • Ingredients: metals and special earths dug from the ground — many of the special ones come from China.
  • Special materials: factories turn them into thin strips, powders and alloys.
  • Parts: machines weld, press and bake them into tiny precise parts.
  • Approval: customers test the parts for a long time before trusting them.
  • Boards: the parts are soldered onto circuit boards.
  • Devices: the boards go into meters, cars and appliances.
Fund-manager lens

The critical asset of a component maker is a qualified part in a growing application. Shivalik Bimetal's re-rating in 2026 rests on exactly that: its electron-beam-welded shunts are approved at meter makers and increasingly at automotive customers, and smart meters and EV battery systems each need them. By contrast, a maker of commodity magnets or ferrites competes with Chinese suppliers on price. When valuing these companies, ask what share of revenue comes from parts that took years to qualify, and how much from parts any competitor could supply next quarter.

The economics in plain terms

A component is cheap to the buyer — a shunt might cost a few tens of rupees inside a meter costing a few thousand — so buyers care far more about reliability than price. Once a component is designed into a product and qualified, it tends to stay for the life of that product. That gives established suppliers pricing stability and long runs, but it also means new suppliers wait years for revenue. Exports matter: global electrical and automotive companies buy from Indian component makers when their quality is proven, and "China plus one" sourcing has made that easier since 2020.

The relay line is why Permanent Magnets' delayed relay project matters: prepaid meters need a latching relay to cut and restore supply remotely, and a domestic source for it is a natural extension of a shunt and metering-component business. Shivalik's move into automotive modules at Pune is the same logic in EVs — using a qualified component to sell a larger sub-assembly.

Analogy

Being a qualified component supplier is like being the approved caterer for a large office: getting the contract takes a long trial, but once you have it you serve lunch every day for years — as long as nobody falls ill.

7 · The business calendar

Driven by customers' programmes and tenders

  • April–June: new fiscal-year orders from meter makers and appliance companies.
  • July–September: festive-season appliance build; smart-meter tender awards through the year.
  • October–December: European customers' year-end ordering for exporters.
  • January–March: year-end dispatches; automotive model-year programme decisions.
  • Policy dates: scheme tranches and approvals (ECMS, rare-earth magnets), the February budget.

Reading it: a three-stock basket, so read loosely. April has beaten the Nifty in 12 of 15 years (+9.7 points on average), and April–July in 9 of 15 with a median of +17.9. September–November has worked in 9 of 14 (median +12.7). January–March is the weak stretch — 6 of 15 years, median −5.9. The pattern follows the fiscal-year order cycle of meter and appliance makers, which place large annual orders in April and ahead of the festive season.

A tendency, not a rule: small samples, survivorship bias (only names still listed) and equal weighting. Use it for timing context, not as a signal.

How to read it

  • April is the strongest single month (12 of 15 years), consistent with fiscal-year ordering.
  • January–March is the weakest window (6 of 15, median −5.9).
  • 2021 dominates the averages: the basket rose 536% that year, with Cosmo Ferrites alone up 2,203%.
How to use this

Three thinly traded small caps cannot support a reliable seasonal rule. The pattern is consistent with the business calendar, but the section's returns are dominated by company-specific events — qualification wins, capacity additions, and occasional speculative runs. Use company milestones, not the calendar.

9 · What moves demand and margins

Eight drivers

  1. Smart-meter roll-out: every meter needs a shunt and metering components (see 055).
  2. EV adoption: battery management systems, chargers and motors (see 017, 046).
  3. Appliance and breaker demand: bimetals in irons, kettles and circuit breakers.
  4. Exports and "China plus one": global customers adding Indian suppliers.
  5. Rare-earth supply: China's export controls and India's magnet scheme.
  6. Component incentives: ECMS capital and turnover support.
  7. Metal prices: copper, nickel and alloy costs.
  8. Qualification wins: new automotive and industrial programmes.

11 · The global picture

China dominates, and the world is paying to diversify

China is the world's largest producer of almost every electronic component and dominates the materials behind them. Its share of sintered permanent magnets has risen from about half two decades ago to about 94% today. In April 2025 Beijing introduced export controls on seven heavy rare-earth elements and the magnets made from them; shipments fell sharply, and carmakers in several countries struggled to obtain magnets. Japan (TDK, Murata, Shin-Etsu), Korea (Samsung Electro-Mechanics), Germany and the US remain leaders in high-end passive components and materials. India is a small producer but has become a qualified supplier in niches — bimetals and shunts among them — and is using incentives to widen its base.

Leadership shifts, and what caused them

  • From Japan and Europe to China (1990s–2010s): scale, cost and control of rare-earth processing.
  • From efficiency to security (2020–2026): the pandemic and export controls made supply diversity a priority.
  • Toward magnet-light designs: carmakers developing motors that use fewer or no rare earths.

12 · Where technology is going

Six shifts

  1. High-precision shunts for EV battery management: higher currents, tighter tolerances.
  2. Smart-meter components: shunts replacing current transformers in many designs.
  3. Wide-bandgap power electronics: silicon carbide and gallium nitride raise demand for high-frequency ferrites (see 050).
  4. Rare-earth-free and reduced-dysprosium magnets.
  5. Magnet recycling: recovering rare earths from end-of-life motors.
  6. Integrated sub-assemblies: component makers moving up to modules (Shivalik's Pune plan).

The practical point for a portfolio is that this section offers one liquid, profitable growth story and several small, thinly traded names. Position sizes and exits have to reflect that liquidity: in 2019 the basket fell 54% in a single year, and Cosmo Ferrites is still 73% below its 2022 peak.

The honest read

The structural case for Indian component makers is better than at any time in the past decade: domestic demand from smart meters and EVs, global customers seeking non-Chinese suppliers, and incentives aimed squarely at components and materials. But the listed opportunity is tiny and concentrated. Shivalik Bimetal has earned its re-rating with results, and its valuation now assumes the growth continues. For the rest, the investable question is whether ECMS projects and the magnet scheme create new listed companies — which is where the section may grow over the next few years.

17 · Questions for management

Concall checklist

  1. What share of revenue comes from parts qualified for more than three years?
  2. How concentrated is revenue by customer and by end market?
  3. What is the pipeline of automotive programmes, and when do they start production?
  4. How exposed are you to rare-earth supply, and from where do you source?
  5. Do you expect ECMS support, and for what capacity?
  6. How are metal price changes passed through?
  7. What is export versus domestic growth?
  8. What new competitors are entering under the component scheme?

Red flags

  • Share-price runs without matching revenue.
  • Business descriptions that change with the theme.
  • Heavy customer concentration without long-term contracts.
  • Preferential allotments at prices far above traded history.

19 · Glossary

Every term you'll meet

Components and materials

Shunt resistor
A precise, very low-value resistor across which a small voltage shows how much current flows.
Manganin
A copper-manganese-nickel alloy whose resistance barely changes with temperature — used in shunts.
Electron-beam welding
Joining metals with a focused electron beam in a vacuum; used to make shunts.
Thermostatic bimetal
Two metals with different expansion rates bonded together, which bend when heated.
Thermal protector
A bimetal switch that cuts power when a device overheats.
Permanent magnet
A material that stays magnetised.
Ferrite magnet
A cheap ceramic permanent magnet used in motors and speakers.
Alnico
An aluminium-nickel-cobalt magnet alloy, stable at high temperatures.
NdFeB magnet
Neodymium-iron-boron, the strongest common magnet, used in EV motors.
SmCo magnet
Samarium-cobalt, a heat-resistant rare-earth magnet used in defence and aerospace.
Sintering
Pressing powder and heating it below melting to form a solid part.
Coercivity
How strongly a magnet resists being demagnetised — heavy rare earths raise it.
Remanence
How strong a magnet stays after magnetisation.
Latching relay
A relay that stays in position without power; used in prepaid meters to disconnect supply.
Rare earths
Seventeen elements including neodymium and dysprosium, essential for strong magnets.
Heavy rare earths
Dysprosium, terbium and others that keep magnets strong at high temperature — subject to China's controls.
Soft ferrite
A ceramic that is easily magnetised and demagnetised, used as cores in transformers and chokes.
Ferrite core
A shaped soft-ferrite part around which wire is wound.
Inductor and choke
Components that resist changes in current, used for filtering.
Relay
An electrically operated switch, often magnetic.
Current transformer (CT)
A small transformer that measures current; an alternative to a shunt.
Passive component
A component that does not amplify: resistors, capacitors, inductors.
Active component
A component that controls current, such as a transistor or chip.
Battery management system (BMS)
Electronics that monitor a battery pack — a key shunt application.
Precision stamping
Pressing metal strip into small accurate parts.
Tolerance
How far a component's value may vary from its target.
Temperature coefficient of resistance
How much a resistor's value changes with temperature — near zero for good shunts.

Industry and policy

Qualification
A customer's testing and approval of a supplier's part.
Design-in
A component being specified in a customer's product design.
PPAP
Production part approval process — the automotive industry's formal qualification.
China plus one
Customers adding a non-Chinese supplier.
ECMS
Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme — India's incentive for components and materials.
REPM scheme
India's ₹7,280 cr scheme for sintered rare-earth permanent magnets.
MTPA
Metric tonnes per annum — magnet capacity units.
IREL
IREL (India) Limited, the state rare-earths company.
Export controls
A government's licensing of exports of sensitive goods.
Preferential allotment
Issuing shares to selected investors at a set price.
Upper circuit
The maximum daily price rise allowed by the exchange.
Thinly traded
A share with few trades, whose price can move sharply on small orders.
