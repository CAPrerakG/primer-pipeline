Every chemical, drug, fuel and industrial gas is made inside a vessel: a reactor that must not corrode, a heat exchanger that must not leak, a tank that must stay at −196°C, a fermenter that turns grain into ethanol. This merged primer follows the companies that build those vessels — from glass-lined reactors and pressure vessels to cryogenic tanks, ethanol plants and fired heaters — through order books that are rising, margins that are not, and an ethanol programme that has hit its target.

The industry in 60 seconds

Explain it to a 10-year-old

Think of cooking. You need a pot that will not rust or crack, a lid that holds the steam, a fridge that keeps things cold and an oven that gets hot evenly. Factories that make medicines, chemicals, fuels and gases need the same things, only huge and very precise: a pot lined with glass so strong acids cannot eat it, a pipe-bundle that moves heat from one liquid to another, a giant flask that keeps oxygen liquid, a tank where yeast turns grain into fuel, and a furnace that heats oil. The companies in this primer build those giant pots, fridges and ovens to order. They are paid when they finish, so a delay or a mistake hurts.

Say it to a client in one minute

This primer merges four watchlist sections: Heat Exchangers & Process Equipment (14 names), Cryogenic Equipment (2), Bioenergy & Distillery Equipment (1) and Industrial Furnaces (1). Eleven of eighteen names form the price basket. The leaders are GMM Pfaudler (glass-lined reactors and process systems, global), INOX India (cryogenic tanks for industrial gases and LNG), Praj (ethanol and bio-based process plants), HLE Glascoat, Anup and Kilburn (reactors, heat exchangers, dryers and pressure vessels), and JNK India (fired heaters and furnaces).

Order books are at records at several companies — GMM ₹2,289 crore, JNK ₹1,801 crore, INOX ₹1,686 crore, Anup close to ₹1,000 crore — but June-quarter margins fell at HLE, Anup, Kilburn and GMM. The cycle is in an awkward phase: orders recovering, execution and pricing still weak. Ethanol, Praj’s market, has reached its 20% blending target, so its domestic plant orders now depend on what comes after E20. The basket is up 5.8% this year against a 10.7% Nifty fall, led by INOX and JNK.

Process equipment is custom capital equipment. A pharmaceutical maker orders a 10-kilolitre glass-lined reactor; a refinery orders a shell-and-tube heat exchanger to a thermal design and pressure code; an industrial-gas company orders a vacuum-insulated tank; a distillery orders a complete grain-to-ethanol plant. Each is designed for the customer’s process, built to a pressure-vessel code, inspected, shipped and often installed and commissioned. Revenue is recognised as work progresses or on dispatch, so it follows order books with a lag of months, and margin depends on whether the price agreed at order covered steel, alloys, labour and the time actually taken.

Carry the Engineering family tests forward (see 078, see 080, see 081): who writes the specification and does the part wear out (078 — Castings); how much value lies in processing rather than metal weight (080 — Forgings); and who carries steel and alloy cost between quote and delivery (081 — Fabrication). The order-to-cash and acceptance tests from 090 — Industrial Machinery apply directly. 086 — Pumps and 087 — Valves cover the flow equipment that connects these vessels, and 093 — Industrial Automation the controls that run them.

Two features dominate the current evidence. First, the pharma-chemical equipment cycle turned down in 2024 and is only beginning to recover; GMM and HLE both describe stable demand but price pressure. Second, three of the best-performing names this year — INOX, JNK and Patels — serve energy and gas markets (LNG, industrial gases, refineries) rather than pharma and chemicals.

Why glass lining is a niche franchise

A glass-lined reactor is a carbon-steel vessel coated with several layers of special glass fused at high temperature. It combines the strength of steel with the chemical resistance of glass, which is why pharmaceutical and fine-chemical makers use it for acid reactions. Making it well requires enamel formulation, furnace control and inspection skills built over decades. GMM Pfaudler and HLE Glascoat dominate the Indian market, and Pfaudler is a global leader. The lining can chip or wear and must be repaired or the vessel replaced, which creates a service and replacement market on top of new capacity.

Why a heat exchanger is a project

A large shell-and-tube heat exchanger for a refinery is designed to a thermal duty, built to a pressure code such as ASME, inspected by third parties and delivered to an EPC contractor’s schedule. It is a one-off engineered product. The maker earns margin on design and fabrication skill, but carries the risk of alloy prices, late customer drawings and liquidated damages for delay. Anup and Loyal sell into this market, often for export.

Why a cryogenic tank is a gas-industry product

A cryogenic tank is a double-walled vessel with vacuum insulation that keeps liquefied gases boiling off slowly. Demand follows industrial-gas capacity (steel, healthcare, electronics), LNG adoption as a transport and industrial fuel, and new uses such as hydrogen and semiconductor fabs. INOX India sells across all of these, with more than two-thirds of its order book for export.

The profitable positions are technology ownership (Praj’s bio-process know-how, Pfaudler’s glass and mixing designs), installed-base service (glass-lined relining, cryogenic tank service) and specialised fabrication capability with qualifications that few competitors hold (heavy-wall pressure vessels, cryogenic tanks to international codes). The least profitable is fabricating standard vessels to someone else’s drawing on a fixed price when steel prices move.

GMM Pfaudler’s August 2026 call describes a reorganisation that makes this structure explicit. It replaced its India and International geographic structure with four global divisions: Corrosion Resistant Technologies (glass-lined, with the industry’s largest installed base and a large aftermarket), Process Performance Technologies (mixing, filtration, drying), Heavy Engineering Technologies (defence, nuclear, fertiliser) and Process System Technologies. Fund-manager lens

For each maker, ask what share of revenue comes from aftermarket and service, and what share of orders carry steel price variation. A process-equipment company with a large installed base and pass-through clauses compounds through the cycle; a pure fabricator on fixed prices swings with it.

Margins fell where orders were weakest a year ago

The June-quarter pattern is clear: companies whose order intake slowed in 2025 are now executing thinner books at lower margins. Anup’s margin fell from 23.0% to 7.6% on a 28.5% revenue drop; management said on its call that it had deliberately delayed new order intake the previous year and faced supply-chain challenges, and that its order book has since recovered to ₹985 crore with ₹538 crore booked since April. Anup Q1 FY27 earnings call, August 2026 Kilburn’s margin fell from 25.8% to 17.7% on lower revenue; it described the quarter as timing and said it had secured about ₹190 crore of orders so far in FY27 against a ₹800 crore group target. Kilburn Q1 FY27 earnings call, August 2026 HLE’s margin halved to 7.0%; its pre-tax profit of ₹4.37 crore was smaller than its tax charge of ₹4.80 crore, partly offset by a deferred-tax credit.

Where margins held or rose

INOX held a 20.5% operating margin, among the highest in the roster, though slightly below last year. JNK’s margin rose from 3.3% to 8.8% on 82% revenue growth; management guided to 20–25% revenue growth and about 12% EBITDA margin for FY27, with an order book of ₹1,801 crore at 30 June. JNK Q1 FY27 earnings call, August 2026 Mazda’s revenue rose 62% and margin to 9.1%, though its quarter includes food products. Loyal’s margin held near 15% on 66% revenue growth.

Praj: low margin, large book

Praj’s operating margin is thin at 4.2%, and its June-quarter other income of ₹19.98 crore includes an ₹8.90 crore insurance claim for a March 2025 fire at its Urwade premises. Management reported order intake of ₹10 billion in the quarter (57% domestic) and a backlog of ₹45.9 billion at 30 June, 77% of it bioenergy; exports were 25% of revenue. Praj Q1 FY27 earnings call, August 2026 The backlog is large relative to revenue, but its margin will depend on how much is technology-led bioenergy and how much is fabrication-heavy engineering.

The honest read

Order books are recovering; margins have not. The best current operating margins are at INOX (cryogenics) and Kilburn (dryers), with Loyal and Patels (small pressure-vessel makers) in the low to mid-teens. The two glass-lined leaders and Anup are at cyclical lows. Praj is a low-margin, high-backlog business at a policy crossroads.

Three tiers

The top tier — Kilburn, INOX and Anup at 21–23% — combines specialised engineering with markets where qualification matters: industrial dryers for demanding processes, cryogenic vessels to international codes, heavy-wall exchangers and vessels for refineries and LNG. Their June-quarter margins (17.7%, 20.5% and 7.6%) show how much a single quarter can fall from the annual level when revenue dips, which is the operating leverage of a fixed-cost fabrication shop.

The middle tier — Mazda, Loyal, GMM, JNK and HLE at 10–16% — mixes niche products with more competitive work. The glass-lined leaders sit here in FY26, below what a dominant niche position would suggest, because pharma-chemical demand was soft and both carry acquired overseas businesses still being integrated. GMM’s international operations and HLE’s German subsidiaries add scale and technology but have lower margins and integration costs.

The bottom tier — Patels, BEW and Praj at 5–9% — is either project-heavy or in a down-cycle. Praj’s 4.8% reflects that much of its revenue is plant engineering and supply around its process technology, and that FY26 was the year after the E20 build-out peaked. BEW’s profit fell by two-thirds on rising revenue, which points to pricing or execution problems in a small pharma-equipment maker.

Fund-manager lens

The tiers line up with the share-price leadership in 2026: INOX (top tier, export growth) leads; the glass-lined names in the middle are recovering from lows; the bottom-tier names include the year’s worst performers. Margin tier is a better guide to this group than order-book size.

The glass-lined duopoly: GMM and HLE

GMM Pfaudler and HLE Glascoat together dominate Indian glass-lined equipment, and both have bought into Europe. Their parallel histories show how a niche franchise behaves through a demand cycle.

Both rode the 2019–2021 pharma-chemical capex wave: HLE’s share rose more than threefold in each of 2020 and 2021, and GMM’s doubled in 2020. Both then derated as Indian chemical makers faced Chinese price competition and customers deferred projects. In FY26 GMM earned an 11.4% operating margin on ₹3,523.94 crore of revenue and HLE 10.3% on ₹1,352.98 crore. In the June 2026 quarter GMM’s margin was 10.1% and HLE’s 7.0%.

Their strategies have diverged in emphasis. GMM, much larger and global, has reorganised into four divisions and is reducing debt; its glass-lined division retains the industry’s largest installed base and a large aftermarket, and its process-technology division is growing fastest. HLE has added German businesses — Thaletec in glass-lined equipment and, in FY26, Omeras — whose order books management described as encouraging, with about ₹78 crore at Omeras. HLE’s consolidated order book was about ₹681.6 crore at March 2026. Integration and turnaround of acquired units is a cost before it is a benefit, and HLE’s June-quarter result shows that cost.

For an investor the glass-lined franchise has two attractive features: a concentrated market and a recurring relining and spares business on a large installed base. Its weaknesses are exposure to one customer group (pharma and fine chemicals), the possibility that continuous processing slows batch-reactor demand, and the cost of the overseas acquisitions both companies made near the top of the last cycle. The recovery case requires pharma-chemical capex to resume and the overseas units to earn their cost of capital.

Ethanol after E20: what Praj’s market looks like now

India’s ethanol blending programme is the single largest policy driver in this roster. The petroleum ministry’s July 2026 explainer gives the path: blending of about 8.1% in ESY 2020-21, 10.0% in 2021-22, 12.1% in 2022-23, 14.6% in 2023-24, 19.2% in 2024-25 and 20% in 2025-26 (November to June). It says India’s ethanol production capacity rose from about 400 crore litres to nearly 1,200 crore litres, that the build-out involved investment of nearly ₹1 lakh crore a year in ethanol production and infrastructure, and that the procurement price for maize-based ethanol is about ₹71.86 a litre. The minister has described a phased move towards E25, E27 and E30. PIB, Ethanol Blended Petrol Programme Q&A, 10 July 2026

For an ethanol-plant supplier, reaching the target changes the demand equation. The capacity required for E20 has largely been built; the same ministry document defends E20 partly on the ground that reverting would strand capacity already created. New domestic first-generation plant orders now depend on the timing and certainty of higher blends, on feedstock policy (sugar, grain, maize) and on distillery economics at the administered price. That is consistent with Praj’s share price: it more than doubled in 2021 as the E20 build-out began, rose further in 2023 and 2024, and fell 60.7% in 2025 as the build-out matured.

Praj’s response is diversification. Its June-quarter call described order intake of ₹10 billion, 57% domestic and 43% international, a backlog of ₹45.9 billion with bioenergy at 77%, and new lines including water systems for semiconductors and data centres. Third-party summaries of its presentation report a USD 50 million minimum data-centre supply agreement and a first semiconductor water-treatment contract; these should be verified against the company’s own disclosure before being relied upon. The investment question is whether compressed biogas, sustainable aviation fuel, bio-polymers and engineering exports can replace domestic first-generation ethanol as the growth engine, and at what margin.

Fund-manager lens

Treat Praj as a policy-cycle company that has passed the peak of its largest domestic programme. The next leg needs either a dated E25–E30 roadmap with new capacity requirements, or proof that newer bio-products and exports earn better than the current 4–5% operating margin.

Cryogenics: INOX and the gas economy

INOX India is the clearest growth story in the roster and the reason the basket is positive this year. Its business is storing and moving gases that exist as liquids only at very low temperatures: oxygen, nitrogen and argon for steel, healthcare and electronics; LNG for trucks, ships, factories and remote power; and, increasingly, specialty gases for semiconductor fabs and research. It also makes stainless-steel beverage kegs.

Its August 2026 call reported the highest quarterly order inflow in its history, about ₹532 crore, taking the order book to about ₹1,686 crore, of which more than ₹1,140 crore is export. Management described orders for storage and transport equipment for semiconductor manufacturing facilities, multiple LNG fuelling-station orders, continued leadership in LNG semi-trailers, a mini-LNG terminal project in the Bahamas and an LNG fuel-tank project for Cochin Shipyard that marks its entry into marine LNG. On a standalone basis it reported total income of ₹382 crore, up about 8.3%, EBITDA of ₹90 crore, up 1.4%, and profit after tax of about ₹61 crore, flat. Consolidated revenue rose 9.2% with a 20.5% operating margin. INOX India Q1 FY27 call

The economics differ from the rest of the roster in three ways. First, the order book is two-thirds export, so growth depends on global gas and LNG investment rather than Indian pharma or refinery capex. Second, the products are standardised enough to be built in series — tanks, trailers, kegs — while still requiring code-certified vacuum and welding quality, which supports a 20%-plus margin. Third, new demand pools are opening: management noted that lower global LNG prices have improved the case for LNG as a transition fuel, and semiconductor fabs need ultra-clean gas storage.

The risks are the mirror image. Export orders carry currency and shipping risk and can be lumpy. Margin slipped from 22.4% to 20.5% year on year in June, and EBITDA grew more slowly than revenue. And a business valued for growth must keep converting a record book at record margins; INOX trades 44% above its earlier 2024 high after an 85% rise this year, so expectations are high.

Next candidates: KRN and SETL

Two excluded names will qualify for the basket once they have enough trading history. KRN Heat Exchanger makes fin-and-tube coils for air-conditioning and refrigeration and is moving into bus air-conditioning and complete HVAC systems. On its August call it reported standalone revenue up 59.07% to ₹181.97 crore and consolidated revenue up 118.87% to ₹252.32 crore, with consolidated EBITDA up 178.85% to ₹49.06 crore and net profit up 164.84% to ₹32.90 crore. Its economics are closer to HVAC components than to process equipment. Standard Engineering Technology makes pharma and chemical process equipment; its June-quarter consolidated revenue was ₹247.69 crore and management described a core unexecuted order book of about ₹1,400 crore. Both listed in late 2024 or early 2025 and have fewer than 500 sessions of history.

Order books are the most comparable forward measure across this roster, but they are not equal. GMM’s backlog is mostly short-cycle (10–12 months), so it converts quickly; JNK’s and Praj’s include multi-year EPC and plant projects, so a large book relative to revenue means a long execution tail rather than faster growth. INOX’s is export-heavy, which brings currency and shipping exposure but also access to larger markets. Anup’s recovered book follows a year in which it deliberately slowed intake — a margin-protection decision that cost revenue.

Cash follows contract terms. Process-equipment customers usually pay an advance with the order, progress payments on drawings and material, and the balance on dispatch or commissioning, often with a retention. Rising order books bring advances that support cash; slow execution ties up inventory. BEW’s FY26 shows the risk at the small end: inventory and receivables absorbed ₹34.50 crore while profit fell, and reported operating cash relied on payables and short-term borrowings.

The test to apply

Divide each order book by quarterly revenue to get coverage, then ask what share is short-cycle, what share carries price variation, and how much advance has been received. A deep book with little advance and fixed prices is a risk, not a cushion.

The critical constraint for fabricators is the price of special alloys between quote and procurement. A heat exchanger in duplex stainless steel or a column clad with nickel alloy can have a material cost that moves by double digits in months. Makers that buy material immediately on order or have price-variation clauses protect margin; makers that quote fixed prices and buy later absorb the move. Anup’s call referred to supply-chain challenges and a deliberate slowdown in order intake; HLE’s and GMM’s refer to pricing pressure in a soft pharma-chemical market.

For glass-lined equipment the constraint is furnace capacity and lining quality: a lining defect found at testing means stripping and relining. For cryogenic tanks it is vacuum and welding quality to international codes. For ethanol plants it is the client’s financing and feedstock, not the maker’s capacity.

The year-end window

October–December beat the Nifty in 12 of 14 years with a median of +8.0 points; December alone beat it in 11 of 15 years by 3.8 points on average. The two failures, 2020 and 2025, were years of sharp sector-specific falls. February is the weakest month, beating the Nifty in only 3 of 15 years. The global industry and India’s place

Global process equipment is led by groups such as Alfa Laval (heat transfer, separation, fluid handling), GEA, SPX Flow and Chart Industries (cryogenics), with Pfaudler a leader in glass-lined equipment. Alfa Laval’s 2025 results illustrate the current global cycle: order intake fell 6% to SEK 66.7 billion while net sales rose 8% to SEK 69.7 billion and the adjusted EBITA margin rose to 17.7%, as it executed a strong earlier book; management expected first-quarter 2026 demand at about fourth-quarter levels. Alfa Laval, fourth quarter and full year 2025 The Indian names show the opposite phase — recovering orders, compressed margins — which is consistent with a domestic cycle that turned down earlier.

India’s place is increasingly as an exporter of engineered vessels. INOX’s export order book exceeds ₹1,140 crore, two-thirds of its total, and includes a mini-LNG terminal project in the Bahamas and an LNG fuel tank for Cochin Shipyard. Anup, Loyal and Patels export heat exchangers and pressure vessels to EPC contractors. Praj’s international intake was 43% of the June quarter. GMM is itself a global group with major operations in Germany, the US and elsewhere, now organised by global division rather than by geography.

Indian makers compete on engineering cost and fabrication quality certified to international codes. Their constraints are shop size for the largest vessels, access to special alloys and the working capital needed for long export projects.

Policy, codes and trade

Ethanol blending. The programme reached 20% in ESY 2025-26 and E20 is the base fuel; the government has described a phased move to E25, E27 and E30 without, in the cited document, a dated mandate. Feedstock rules, administered prices and oil-company tenders set distillery economics. This is Praj’s most important variable.

Pressure-vessel codes and inspection. Indian rules for boilers and pressure vessels, and international codes such as ASME for export, govern design, fabrication and inspection. Code stamps and inspection-agency approvals are barriers to entry for small makers and must be renewed.

Chemical-industry trade defence. Indian chemical makers’ capex — the demand base for glass-lined equipment — depends partly on protection against low-priced imports. Anti-dumping duties on specific chemicals support domestic capacity; their absence or expiry can delay customer projects. These are product-specific and should be tracked through DGTR notices for the customer’s products.

Withdrawn machinery order. The 2024 machinery omnibus technical regulation was withdrawn in January 2026, as recorded in 089 — Machine Tools; its timetable is not a pending requirement for this equipment.

LNG and gas policy. LNG as a transport fuel and city-gas expansion support INOX’s domestic LNG business; INOX’s call noted that lower global LNG prices have improved the economics of LNG as a transition fuel.

Where the cycle stands now

Stocks: the basket is +5.8% this year vs -10.7% for the Nifty (to 18 Sep 2026). INOX India +85.1%, JNK +66.2%, Patels +54.8% and GMM +32.8% lead; BEW −58.7%, Loyal −30.5%, Anup −27.0% and Kilburn −17.5% lag. Without the two SME names the basket would be +21.0%; the pharma-chemical equipment makers alone −6.5%.

The process-equipment cycle is at an inflection. Order books are at records at GMM, INOX and JNK and recovering at Anup; June-quarter margins are compressed at HLE, Anup, Kilburn and GMM as thinner, older books are executed. Energy and gas equipment (INOX, JNK, Loyal) is stronger than pharma-chemical equipment (HLE, BEW, Patels). Ethanol has passed its domestic build-out peak, and Praj is diversifying.

What would confirm the recovery

Three things: margins at GMM, HLE and Anup recovering in the December and March quarters as new orders execute; continued order inflow without price cutting; and stable or improving cash as advances arrive with orders. If margins stay low while books grow, the orders were bought with price, and the recovery will be in revenue, not profit.

Where conviction should come from

INOX: export execution and semiconductor/LNG orders. GMM: margin recovery and deleveraging. Anup: margin on the rebuilt book. JNK: delivering guided growth at about 12% EBITDA. Praj: a dated post-E20 roadmap or higher-margin new businesses. HLE: integration of German acquisitions and margin recovery.

Questions to ask management

What share of orders carries alloy price variation?

Give the share of the book with escalation clauses and the material already procured.

What margin is embedded in the current order book?

Compare the expected margin on the current book with last year’s executed margin.

What share of revenue is aftermarket? (GMM, HLE, INOX)

Give relining, spares and service revenue and margin separately.

How fast will the backlog convert? (GMM, JNK, Praj)

Split the book by expected execution period and advance received.

What happens to domestic ethanol orders after E20? (Praj)

Give domestic first-generation intake, pipeline and dependence on E25–E30 timing.

What do the new businesses earn? (Praj)

Give revenue and margin for biogas, aviation fuel, water systems and engineering exports.

When do the German acquisitions break even? (HLE)

Give Thaletec and Omeras revenue, margin and order books.

Why did margins fall so sharply? (Anup, Kilburn)

Separate under-recovery of fixed costs from pricing and material costs.

How is the export book hedged? (INOX)

Give currency, hedging and shipping terms on the export order book.

Which subsidiaries are losing money? (GMM, JNK)

Explain the minority losses and plans for those units.

Why are borrowings in operating cash flow? (BEW)

Restate operating cash with borrowings in financing activities.

Cryogenic, bioenergy and furnace sections have one or two names each; all build engineered vessels and plants for process industries. The merge is disclosed and tested by sensitivity.

Why did Anup’s margin collapse in June?

It had slowed order intake the previous year; a thin book meant under-recovered fixed costs. Its book has since recovered to ₹985 crore.

Why is Praj’s outlook tied to policy?

E20 has been reached and the capacity built; new domestic plant orders depend on higher blends and feedstock policy.

Why is GMM’s owner profit above group profit?

Subsidiaries with minority holders lost money, so minorities bore part of the loss.

What is wrong with BEW’s operating cash flow?

It includes increases in short-term borrowings, which belong in financing activities.

Which names are misclassified?

Winsol (renewable EPC), Integra (rail components), Unison (stainless steel); Cryogenic OGS makes gas skids, not cryogenic equipment.

Sources and method

Primary result filings govern revenue, other income, profit and EPS; figures were read from rendered statement pages where text extraction was unreliable (all eleven basket members). Earnings calls supply order books, guidance and management explanations, dated and attributed. Annual reports supply business descriptions and cash flows. Web summaries of company presentations are flagged where used. All evidence is dated no later than 18 September 2026.
