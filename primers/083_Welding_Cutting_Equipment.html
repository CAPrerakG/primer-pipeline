Welding & Cutting Equipment

Welding turns separate pieces into a load-bearing structure. The visible spark is only one part of the business: a producer must choose a process, a power source, a wire or electrode, a shielding method, a trained operator and an approved procedure. Cutting shapes the metal first; inspection and repair follow. This eight-name watchlist spans consumables, equipment, automation and wear-restoration services. It also contains one automotive sheet-metal company that does not belong in the operating basket. We keep all eight visible, then test which earnings come from making and supplying a sound joint rather than from a land sale or an associate.

1 · The industry in 60 seconds

A sound joint is bought as a system, not a spark

Explain it to a 10-year-old

Imagine joining two bicycle-frame tubes. The machine makes the heat, but it does not supply all the metal in the seam. A wire or stick melts into the gap; a gas or coating protects the hot metal from air. The welder must use the right settings so the joint holds when the bicycle hits a bump. A factory building a bridge or ship does the same thing on a much larger scale. A cheap stick that breaks a critical joint can cost far more than the saving on the stick. After welding, the factory may inspect, grind or repair the joint. A company selling the trusted wire repeatedly can have a different business from a company selling the machine once.

Say it to a client in one minute

Six of the eight watchlist names have usable share-price histories and genuine welding exposure. ESAB India and Ador are broad systems suppliers; GEE, Classic and Rasi concentrate on electrodes and wire; Diffusion sells special consumables, wear plates and repair services. Technocrats makes plasma and automated systems but listed only in August 2026, so its 20 observations cannot support a sixteen-year backtest. Rasandik mainly makes automotive stampings and assemblies, so its listing label does not make it a welding consumables proxy.

The six-stock price basket rose 19.0% in 2026 through 18 September, against a 10.7% Nifty fall. GEE and Ador led; Classic fell sharply. Operating sales improved at several companies, but reported profit needs unusually careful reading. GEE's ₹6.85 cr June PAT includes a ₹3.70 cr property gain. Diffusion's ₹16.68 cr consolidated PAT includes ₹4.46 cr from associates; its standalone PAT was lower year on year. ESAB's FY26 earnings included both a land gain and a labour-code charge. The industry is not one undifferentiated margin story.

The enduring economic question is who owns the approved welding specification. Standard mild-steel electrodes can compete on price and dealer reach; a qualified alloy wire, flux recipe or repair procedure can be harder to replace. A machine sale may be cyclical, yet an installed machine can pull through wires, tips and service. That relationship must be proved in orders and cash, not assumed from a product brochure.

2 · What the products are

Consumables, power sources, cutting systems and repair work

SubjectEvidence and investment test
Covered electrodes

Covered electrodes are rods with a metal core and flux coating. In shielded metal arc welding, the electrode melts into the joint and the coating creates protection and slag. This process is flexible on a construction site and in repair work, but the stick is used up. Ador's Q1 FY27 presentation lists electrodes alongside filler metals and submerged-arc products; GEE's product deck separates mild steel, stainless, low-alloy, cast-iron and hard-facing grades. A sales rupee from a common construction electrode is not equivalent to one from an approved nickel-alloy consumable.

Continuous wire

Solid wire and flux-cored wire feed continuously into semi-automatic or automatic welding. The filler wire is still consumed, but deposition rate, spatter, cleaning time and weld quality change the customer's total cost. Classic says in its FY26 release that it expanded flux-cored wire alongside established electrodes and MIG wires. GEE describes a flux-cored line being installed at Kolkata and a portfolio of solid MIG, TIG and submerged-arc wire. These announcements establish an intended product mix; they do not yet quantify the new line's profitable shipped volume.

Protection

Flux and gas protect the molten metal by different mechanisms. Submerged arc welding blankets a continuous arc with granular flux and suits thick-plate fabrication. Gas metal and tungsten arc processes need a suitable shielding gas. A change in wire chemistry or gas mix can make an approved procedure invalid for a critical project. This creates switching friction only when the supplier is written into a customer's qualified weld procedure and keeps the batch chemistry consistent.

Equipment

Equipment supplies and controls the electrical current. Inverter power sources, torches, wire feeders, rotators, positioners and automation cells cover different budgets. Ador's presentation describes 200–1,200 amp power sources and special-purpose automation; ESAB India calls its reportable business Fabrication Technology. Technocrats' FY26 annual report lists plasma cutters, CNC systems, laser systems, welding power sources and automated cells. A plasma or laser system cuts plate; it does not itself create a weld. For the buyer, machine uptime and local service can matter more than the purchase price.

Wear repair

Wear repair is the bridge from welding to maintenance. Diffusion uses special electrodes, flux-cored wires, wear plates and field service to rebuild or protect industrial parts. Its presentation says the group serves cement, steel, power, mining, sugar and other heavy users, with 11,000-plus projects completed. Those are company-reported counts. The investor should ask how many projects produce repeat consumable orders and what capital the service teams require.

Cutting

Cutting has a distinct cycle. A factory can replace a manual torch with a CNC plasma or laser system because it wants faster, more precise cuts and less scrap; the machine order depends on a capital budget. Electrode and wire demand follows hours of actual welding, maintenance shutdowns and installed fabrication capacity. Putting both into one watchlist is useful for customer context, but it cannot justify assigning the same revenue multiple to a one-time automated cell and a replenished wire spool.

3 · The value chain

From alloy chemistry to an accepted weld

The physical chain starts with steel wire rod, ferroalloys, mineral powders, binders, shielding gases, copper coating, electronic components and steel plate. Manufacturers draw wire, blend flux, coat or fill electrodes, assemble power sources, test chemistry and pack batches. A dealer, distributor or direct technical sales team takes the product to a shipyard, railway shop, repair contractor, OEM or small workshop. At the site, an engineer chooses a welding procedure, an operator makes the joint, and inspectors accept or reject it. The supplier's revenue comes before the final asset's operating life is known; quality failures can surface later.

That chain creates three separable gates. The input gate is consistent metal and flux chemistry. The application gate is a documented procedure: material grade, process, position, heat input, preheat, filler and inspection criteria. The commercial gate is who can actually supply the approved batch on time at a viable price. A catalogue with hundreds of grades helps availability, but only a grade accepted in a customer's procedure creates a durable supply position. GEE claims 500-plus dealers, 500-plus SKUs and approvals across industrial customers. Treat those as management descriptions; ask what percentage of sales is from repeat qualified orders and how much inventory those SKUs require.

Consider an ordinary mild-steel electrode against a corrosion-resistant filler used offshore. The first buyer may have several acceptable brands and select by delivered price. The second buyer may require a documented wire lot, procedure qualification, weld testing and a classification-society sign-off. A higher gross margin may be defensible in the second case, yet only if the producer actually supplies that application and passes through the cost of expensive alloy. Diffusion names chromium, molybdenum, manganese, nickel, metal wire and plate as key inputs. A specialty label without evidence of pricing and inventory turns is not enough.

Equipment suppliers face a further gate after installation: service. A broken power source can stop a fabrication line even if the repair part is inexpensive. Local spares and technicians therefore have real value. But equipment orders are less repeatable than wire consumption, and hardware can become obsolete when control electronics, automation software or cutting heads change. Technocrats' newly listed share has no long history from which to infer its cycle; use its FY26 accounts and the progress of order conversion, not the short launch chart.

Where the customer sees the benefit

A buyer does not measure a filler solely by purchase price per kilogram. A high-deposition wire may shorten a weld, but only if the workpiece and procedure allow it. Less spatter can reduce cleaning time; fewer defects can reduce inspection failures; a low-hydrogen grade can reduce cracking risk when stored and handled correctly. Each benefit has to be measured in the customer's own shop. A supplier that merely claims a higher-grade SKU but cannot demonstrate saved machine hours, accepted welds or lower rework may face the same price competition as an ordinary wire seller. Conversely, a product that reduces a customer's total fabrication cost can justify a premium even if the consumable invoice is a small part of a project.

Approval creates a commercial lag. First a supplier spends on formulation, samples, testing and technical visits. A customer or certifier then validates the procedure. Only after acceptance can regular orders begin; even then the customer may keep a second approved source. That lag can make sales appear slow despite an attractive product pipeline, and a new approval can be less valuable than a repeat order from an existing buyer. The company should disclose which approvals have moved to commercial production, how long they took and whether price resets cover metal inflation. A presentation photograph of a weld or a customer logo does not reveal any of these economics.

For capital equipment the payback calculation changes again. A CNC cutting line can save plate by improving nesting and reduce manual setup, but it needs an operator, consumable torch parts, maintenance and possibly site modification. The equipment maker's initial gross margin may be lowered by bought-out controls and installation costs. If its machine base later buys spares or service, that income can stabilize the business; if customers source replacements elsewhere, the order remains one-off. Ador and Technocrats both discuss automated products, yet neither should receive an installed-base premium without evidence of accepted units, uptime, warranty cost and repeat service receipts.

Fund-manager lens · the value chain

A welding supplier's strongest economic position is a repeat consumable written into an approved procedure, delivered reliably and priced above its fully loaded metal cost. A machine sale can deepen that position if the installed base leads to measurable wire, spares or service revenue. Separate the two streams in every model. At GEE, the company cites approvals and dealer reach while its Q1 operating margin was 7.8%; an asset-sale gain cannot validate the consumable moat. At Diffusion, the customer may buy a saved maintenance shutdown rather than kilograms of wire, but the proof is recurring project and cash evidence.

5 · How the businesses earn

Repeat wire, qualification and application economics

A basic welding consumable earns a spread between selling price and metal, powders, coating, energy, labour, packing, distribution and quality control. For common grades, volumes, plant utilization and dealer coverage can be more important than branding. Specialty grades add alloy cost and technical support; their selling price can be higher, but so can inventory and qualification expenses. A credible premium needs a customer value measure: deposition per hour, lower rework, fewer failed inspections or longer service life. A producer can have high gross margin in a narrow grade and low cash return if it must stock many slow-moving SKUs.

ESAB

ESAB is the largest listed operating earnings engine in this roster. Its June filing records ₹421.12 cr of sales and ₹56.14 cr PAT, versus ₹352.02 cr and ₹40.94 cr a year earlier. Normal Other Income was only ₹1.78 cr, against ₹0.88 cr, so this latest quarter's improvement cannot be explained by a large investment gain. However, its FY26 September and December reported profits were affected by opposing exceptional items: a ₹30.91 cr land-sale gain and a ₹13.65 cr labour-code-related charge, explained in the December exchange filing. Screener places those in its expanded “Other Income” display, producing misleading summary values of about +₹33 cr and −₹13 cr. GEE

GEE's Q1 investor deck reports ₹102.86 cr sales, up 29.9%, and ₹8.00 cr EBITDA, up 76.1%. EBITDA margin improved from 5.7% to 7.8%. That is an operating improvement worth tracking. But the reported ₹6.85 cr PAT includes a separate ₹3.70 cr exceptional property-sale gain; the company gives adjusted PAT of ₹3.15 cr by subtracting that pre-tax gain directly from after-tax PAT. It does not give a tax-adjusted normalized PAT, so ₹3.15 cr is the company's convention, not a precise recurring after-tax figure. The deck's land realization scenario and installed capacity of about 59,000 tonnes are management claims, not current cash. It reports FY26 capacity utilization of 57% and a target near 91% by FY29. The challenge is whether that utilization and new specialty wire actually raise recurring margins without excess working capital.

Diffusion

Diffusion sells more of an engineering solution than an ordinary electrode. Its Q1 presentation cites special consumables, wear plates, heavy equipment and 80% repeat customers. It reports an order in hand of ₹209.66 cr at 30 June and a planned manufacturing expansion. Its press release breaks that order book into ₹159.02 cr heavy engineering, ₹26.42 cr wear plates and parts, and only ₹24.22 cr welding consumables. Roughly three-quarters of the disclosed orders are thus heavy engineering, with project timing and acceptance risk unlike recurring wire. Consolidated profit also includes equity-accounted associates. The June results show ₹110.11 cr operating revenue, actual Other Income around ₹4.10 cr, and about ₹4.46 cr share of associate profit. Together those non-operating or non-controlled contributions help explain why summary sites display roughly ₹9 cr “Other Income.” Consolidated PAT was ₹16.68 cr; standalone PAT was around ₹9.98 cr, below the year-prior parent figure. The release says that prior parent quarter contained ₹5.07 cr dividend income from a wholly owned subsidiary. Thus the apparent standalone profit decline cannot be interpreted as deteriorating welding operations without removing that dividend. Calling the consolidated growth a pure improvement in parent welding margin would also mix perimeters.

Classic

Classic reported FY26 total income of ₹244.21 cr, up 18.5%, EBITDA ₹23.87 cr, up about 7%, and PAT ₹12.64 cr, up 8.8%. EBITDA margin was 9.78%, weaker than the growth in sales might suggest. Its second-half total income rose 20.7% to ₹121.18 cr while EBITDA grew only 2.7% to ₹11.94 cr. The company credits electrodes, MIG and new flux-cored wire. The next test is whether the new wire earns a higher contribution after copper, alloy, distribution and rework costs rather than merely adding tonnage.

Rasi

Rasi is smaller and more exposed to ordinary consumable competition. Its FY26 audited report shows ₹72.02 cr operations against ₹81.44 cr in FY25, but PAT rose from ₹2.74 cr to ₹3.52 cr. Other Income nearly doubled from about ₹0.87 cr to ₹1.79 cr; operating cash flow was ₹6.43 cr, above PAT, as receivables released cash. The June 2026 quarter's operating sales declined from ₹19.42 cr to ₹17.64 cr while PAT rose slightly to ₹1.33 cr; Other Income was ₹0.30 cr versus ₹0.37 cr. A shrinking sales base and improved annual net profit can coexist, but the latter does not establish growth in welding demand.

Technocrats

Technocrats is the equipment-heavy exception. The FY26 report states figures in thousands of rupees: operating revenue 1,313,089.51 thousand equals ₹131.31 cr, and net profit 149,360.06 thousand equals ₹14.94 cr. Other Income is only 975.75 thousand, about ₹0.10 cr. The same report shows EPS ₹11.63 on about 1.284 crore weighted-average shares, giving approximately ₹14.93 cr and validating the unit conversion. Its prior-year EPS table is formatted inconsistently, so the FY26 reconciliation is the one used here. Revenue growth from roughly ₹49.36 cr FY25 is large, but a newly listed capital-equipment supplier needs an order, receivable and installation audit before a smooth growth path is assumed.

Profit per weld is not visible in a consolidated margin

A consumable seller can report high revenue growth through price, volume, product mix or distributor loading. Those have different durability. If steel-wire cost rises and the selling list resets, both revenue and material expense may rise while contribution per accepted weld stays similar. If the same plant ships more qualified alloy grades, revenue and contribution could rise together, but inventory can also become more expensive and slower to move. If sales are booked to dealers who have not sold through, current revenue may pull forward future demand. Therefore the useful unit is not automatically revenue per tonne: for a critical grade, ask for gross contribution after materials, inspection and technical support per repeat customer program.

GEE's own deck describes a roughly half B2B and half B2C mix, more than 500 dealers and about 57% FY26 utilization of 59,075 tonnes of installed capacity. A dealer network may let the factory run longer campaigns and keep popular grades available, yet its cost includes credit, small-batch distribution and a wide catalogue. The 91% utilization goal for FY29 is a management target, and the deck also plans higher capacity. It cannot be translated directly into a margin forecast by multiplying today's operating profit by the target utilization. Evidence would include actual saleable tonnes, grade mix, receivables and cash, all on the enlarged capacity base.

Ador's FY26 balance sheet adds a useful check on its ₹1,140 cr sales and ₹82 cr PAT. Trade receivables rose from about ₹192.7 cr to ₹232.0 cr and inventories from ₹126.7 cr to ₹151.9 cr; trade payables rose more, from ₹124.6 cr to ₹193.7 cr. Those year-end movements are not automatically distress or strength. They show that the expansion of customer credit and stock was largely financed by suppliers at that date. A one-year cash-generation claim should be tested against the maturity and quality of those payables and receivables, particularly while the company is funding its modernization program and resolving the delayed project.

Classic's half-year bridge is another warning against extrapolating sales. H2 total income was ₹121.18 cr versus ₹100.41 cr, while EBITDA was ₹11.94 cr versus ₹11.62 cr. Dividing EBITDA by total income gives about 9.85% in H2 FY26, down from roughly 11.57% a year earlier. Management cites more flux-cored wire and stable electrode demand, but the disclosed consolidated product economics do not yet show an aggregate margin gain from the mix change. A manufactured-versus-traded-sales split and new-product contribution would be more decisive than a product launch announcement.

Technocrats' FY26 profit bridge also matters beyond its striking sales increase. The director's summary reports approximately ₹24.94 cr profit before tax, current tax of about ₹6.27 cr, deferred tax expense of about ₹3.95 cr, a small prior-year tax credit, and ₹14.94 cr profit after tax. A single reported PAT margin therefore includes a substantial deferred-tax charge, while current cash tax is different. Equipment orders can require advances, parts procurement, site work and final acceptance; none of those stages can be inferred from annual revenue alone. The next filing should disclose how receivables and contract assets moved as deliveries were accepted.

The honest margin read

ESAB's June profit improvement is mostly supported by operating sales and margin; Ador's turnaround needs a weak-base and project provision bridge; GEE's ₹6.85 cr reported PAT becomes about ₹3.15 cr after its property gain; Diffusion's associate share contributes ₹4.46 cr to a ₹16.68 cr group PAT; Classic's sales grew much faster than EBITDA; Rasi's FY26 profit improved while sales fell and Other Income rose. Rank these businesses by repeat operating cash and qualified customer share, not one table's net margin.

Two reporting traps deserve emphasis. First, Screener's “Other Income +” expanded line may absorb separately displayed exceptional items or equity-accounted associate results. For GEE, its actual ₹0.31 cr Other Income plus ₹3.70 cr gain rounds to the summary's ₹4.00 cr. For ESAB's FY26 September and December quarters, land and labour-code exceptional items explain the apparent +₹33 cr and −₹13 cr line rather than ordinary treasury income. For Diffusion, associate profit makes the rounded summary near ₹9 cr even though actual Other Income is about ₹4.10 cr. We inspect the statutory line and adjustment before PAT every time.

Second, EPS is attributable to ordinary shareholders, not necessarily all consolidated profit. Diffusion's company release gives ₹4.47 diluted EPS, while the convenient summary displays ₹4.44. Multiplying ₹4.47 by the 3.7426 cr issued shares gives about ₹16.73 cr, around ₹0.05 cr above reported group PAT of ₹16.677 cr. That is a small check, not an exact reconciliation: diluted EPS uses its own weighted denominator and profit attributable to owners, which need to be read directly to explain the remaining gap. The company also has an ESOP trust and minority interests. Ador's and GEE's share checks confirm that the filings' lakh units were converted to crore, while Technocrats' statement is in thousands of rupees. A factor-of-100 error would survive a copied headline but fail EPS × shares. EPS rounding of one or two paisa can produce a small difference; large differences require a basis, unit or share-count explanation.

How to use the audit

Carry three profit lines into a model: reported, adjusted for identified exceptional items, and profit from the controlled welding operation. GEE's ₹6.85 cr, ₹3.15 cr and operating PBT of ₹5.45 cr answer different questions. Diffusion's group PAT and parent PAT also answer different questions. An adjustment is disclosed with its source and tax treatment; it is never a licence to add back every unfavourable expense while keeping one-off gains.

7 · The Engineering tests

Apply the family questions to each weld

The castings primer (078) asks who writes the specification and whether the part wears out. Welding consumables do wear out in use; a wire spool or electrode packet must be reordered. But the specification can be written by the final asset owner, an EPC contractor, a classification society or the consumable maker in consultation with the customer. A manufacturer gains pricing power only if it supplies a qualified grade that is hard to substitute without retesting. A standard electrode replenished often still competes on price. Diffusion's wear-repair products add another kind of repeat demand when a plant must refurbish rollers or feeders after service hours.

The forgings primer (080) asks where the margin lies beyond weight. Here kilograms of wire are a poor universal value measure. Alloy chemistry, controlled hydrogen, deposition efficiency, qualified procedures, testing and on-site application support can dominate metal weight. A tonne of commodity wire and a tonne of critical superalloy filler have different value and risk. At the equipment end, software, controls, precision and service response matter more than machine mass. The question for management is how much revenue and contribution comes from those harder capabilities rather than from simply selling more tonnes.

The fabrication primer (081) asks who bears steel cost between quote and delivery. A wire or electrode producer has a shorter conversion cycle than a large fabricated project, but alloy and wire-rod prices can move before dealer price lists or long-term customer contracts reset. In service and automated equipment projects, the interval can stretch: quoted alloy, power electronics and plate costs may rise while a fixed-price repair or machine order is being executed. Ador's FY26 flares project provision is a concrete warning that project work has risks absent from selling a replenished electrode box.

The fasteners, tools and abrasives primer (082) adds a nearby comparison. Abrasives and welding filler are both consumed in the workshop, but a grinding wheel's performance is often measured by parts finished per wheel while filler quality is judged by weld properties and acceptance of a procedure. Both benefit from repeat replacement only when the customer's machine runs; neither is immune to a fabrication slowdown. These four chapter links locate the economic tests without repeating their company analyses.

8 · Critical inputs

Wire, alloy, flux, power electronics and working capital

Steel wire rod is the base input for many ordinary electrodes and solid wires. Nickel, chromium, molybdenum, manganese and other alloying inputs make specialty grades more expensive and volatile. Mineral powders and binders determine flux performance; copper coating or surface preparation affects feeding and storage. Welding machines add semiconductors, transformers, cooling systems, control boards and software. Cutting machines add torches, nozzles, optics and motion controls. The supplier's cost exposure follows the bill of materials, not the watchlist label.

Diffusion's input list includes ferroalloys, powdered metals, steel plates and wire. GEE's plan proposes bringing stainless-wire drawing and ferroalloy powder production further in-house, and targets a three-to-four-percentage-point material saving. That is a company ambition, not an audited result. It should appear in an investor model only as a scenario with capital spending, yield, quality and cash requirements. Internal production can reduce a supplier margin while increasing fixed cost and inventory.

Raw-material inflation has two timing effects. First, margin compresses if customers buy at a fixed list price while steel or alloy stock is replenished at a higher price. Second, even if prices can be raised, receivables and inventory need more rupees to support the same tonnes. A rising profit margin funded by a larger working-capital deficit may not improve shareholder cash return. Rasi's FY26 cash-flow statement is the opposite case: operating cash of about ₹6.43 cr exceeded PAT partly because receivables and inventory released cash. It is one year, not proof of a permanent cycle.

Product mix can conceal price changes. More stainless or nickel filler raises revenue per kilogram even if tonnage is unchanged; more commodity wire can lift tonnage but dilute margin. A machine installation may include bought-out equipment and on-site integration, which raises gross sales without the same in-house value added. Ask each issuer for tonnes by grade, realization by product family, material pass-through lag and channel inventory. Where those disclosures are absent, describe the unknown instead of projecting a fabricated volume/price bridge.

9 · The business calendar

Projects, maintenance shutdowns and dealer replenishment

SubjectEvidence and investment test
Demand clocks

The physical demand calendar has several clocks. Shipbuilding, rail and infrastructure fabrication follow customer project schedules. Maintenance electrodes and wear plates can be ordered for planned plant shutdowns, then used under pressure when an unexpected part fails. Distributor replenishment can lead or lag the end user's welding hours; a shipment to a dealer is not the same as consumption at a construction site. Equipment orders follow a separate capital approval and installation schedule, especially for robotic or CNC lines.

Fiscal year end

India's fiscal-year end may push project billing, dispatch and customer acceptance into the March quarter. That makes June-versus-March sequential comparisons unsafe as a substitute for June-versus-June. Diffusion's March 2026 quarter sales of about ₹142 cr exceeded its June 2026 ₹110 cr, while the June figure still grew 36.5% from the prior June. Ador's June ₹309 cr was below March ₹319 cr but above June 2025 ₹252 cr. Those observations can fit project timing, but they do not prove a universal seasonal law. Only order, shipment and acceptance records can tie a specific quarter to the underlying work.

GEE quarterly pattern

GEE's quarterly sales went from ₹79.18 cr in June 2025 to ₹85.45 cr in September, ₹92.35 cr in December, ₹112.16 cr in March and ₹102.86 cr in June 2026. That staircase supports improved sales through FY26 followed by a lower sequential June. Yet its June profit comparison must still remove property gain. Classic reports half-yearly; treating March 2026 as a “Q4” in a quarterly seasonal model would split six months of sales into the wrong period. Technocrats' annual equipment accounts give no quarter-by-quarter installation pattern. Reporting frequency is itself a limit on the conclusion.

Consumable replenishment

For a recurring-consumable model, the most useful operating clock is customer production or repair hours. A vendor might ship more wire before a shipyard's build peak, while a plant's unplanned shutdown creates a sudden repair order but reduces normal output. There is no reason to assume these offset in a fixed month across every end market. 11 · What moves the shares

Utilization, mix, property, associates and valuation

The first earnings lever is customer hours of welding. More steel fabrication, vehicle and rail production, shipbuilding, maintenance and plant repairs can increase consumable throughput. But the covered companies do not have equal exposure to those applications. ESAB and Ador have broad ranges; Rasi is narrower; GEE's own deck describes a roughly even B2B and B2C split; Diffusion adds heavy engineering and services. Industrial output is only a rough context measure, not a precise revenue forecast for every issuer.

The second lever is product and service mix. Moving from generic electrodes to controlled-hydrogen or corrosion-resistant grades may improve realization, but alloy input cost, process yield and working capital can offset the price premium. Selling an automated machine may lift one quarter's revenue but carries installation and warranty risk. Diffusion's order book of ₹209.66 cr is an indicator of work in hand; it is not next-quarter profit. GEE's proposed flux-cored capacity and Ador's automation ambitions need orders, acceptance and cash before they count as earnings.

The third lever is margin conversion. Fixed manufacturing and technical staff costs make utilization important. GEE says its capacity utilization was about 57% in FY26, with much higher targets; that provides theoretical operating leverage, but the actual Q1 margin was 7.8% and the ₹3.70 cr property gain sat below operating profit. Classic's FY26 revenue grew faster than EBITDA, an example where volume did not bring the hoped-for leverage. Ador's FY26 delay provision is the opposite risk: one project can consume the profit from many normal consumable shipments.

The fourth lever is accounting and capital allocation. GEE's land rights, ESAB's FY26 land sale, and Diffusion's equity-accounted associate contributions can move PAT without moving welding throughput. Cash received for a sold property may be valuable to shareholders, but it should be valued as a finite asset event rather than capitalized as an electrode margin. Associate profit belongs in the group accounts under accounting rules, but the parent may not control its payout or operating decisions. Those distinctions change both earnings quality and the multiple investors should use.

The fifth lever is the market price paid. A company with strong recurring consumables can still produce poor share returns after excessive valuation; a weaker operating year can coincide with a share rerating. In 2021 this equal-weighted basket gained 147.6%, far more than the Nifty's 24.1%, with Ador, GEE and Rasi all rising sharply. That cannot be explained by a 147.6% physical increase in welds. The 2025 basket decline of 9.4% amid a rising Nifty also cautions against mapping output directly to equity return.

13 · The wider market

Global processes, local approval and service

Arc-welding processes are used globally, and suppliers sell across borders, but the economics are local at the point of qualification and repair. A shipyard or refinery may specify an international filler classification and a particular procedure; the wire still has to be available at the site with test certificates and technical support. Ador says the Middle East is its primary export market. Diffusion's deck says it serves more than 35 countries; GEE claims exports to more than 20. These counts describe reach, not the share of profitable revenue, export currency or customer concentration.

International equipment and consumable groups can bring product designs, power electronics, alloy knowledge and distribution networks. ESAB India's single “Fabrication Technology” reporting segment makes a simple listed comparison possible but does not disclose every process's separate margin. A global brand can improve acceptance; it can also imply related-party purchases, royalties or technology dependencies that must be checked in the annual report. Ador's presentation describes collaborations in plasma cutting, orbital welding and advanced power sources, but a partnership is not an owned technology moat by itself.

Buyers of critical welds care about accepted procedures and liability. An overseas approval may help a manufacturer enter a project, but approval alone does not guarantee a minimum price, volume or share of wallet. GEE's deck lists approvals from industrial and government customers; the investor's next step is to request shipment revenue by approved grade and renewal orders. Diffusion's service-led model can travel to foreign industrial plants, yet on-site repair teams and project execution must be scaled without reducing cash conversion. The same quality gate can be a strength and a cost.

China or other low-cost producers can pressure standard electrode and equipment prices, while specialty wires may compete more on qualification and delivery. We do not insert an unsupported market-size or import-share estimate. The observable issue is whether each company reports import substitution as actual orders with a gross margin after alloy, logistics and testing. A national origin or “Make in India” label may open procurement, but the accepted weld still has to meet the asset owner's specification.

14 · Where technology is going

Better deposition, controlled processes and safer shops

Welding productivity is measured by good joints per hour, not merely arc-on time or wire sold. Flux-cored wires can raise deposition in suitable jobs; inverter sources control current and energy more precisely; fixtures and robots repeat a path; sensors can record parameters for traceability. But automation must fit part geometry, batch size and operator skill. A workshop with short, varied repair jobs may rationally prefer flexible manual equipment; a high-volume vehicle line may justify a robotic cell. Calling all manual welding obsolete would misread the end market.

Ador's product deck spans manual arc sources, power-generating sets and automation, and its management message says automation is a priority as labour costs rise. GEE lists flux-cored products and automated-compatible MIG wire. Classic cites the launch and scale-up of flux-cored wire. Diffusion's model extends from electrodes into wear plates and heavy engineered equipment. Technocrats' FY26 report focuses on plasma and laser cutting and integrated systems. These are different technology bets; the evidence threshold is booked revenue, repeat customers, warranty costs and cash after installation.

Cutting technology can reduce kerf width, scrap and manual setup, but a faster machine may also need more service and consumable torch parts. For a machine vendor, installed base and uptime are stronger recurring indicators than number of catalogued machine types. For a wire vendor, a new process can shift the mix from covered electrodes to solid or flux-cored wire without increasing the total number of fabricated structures. The investable question is whether the company captures enough of that process change to offset any decline in older products.

Safety is also a product and cost issue. Welding fumes, ultraviolet light, sparks, heat and gas-cylinder handling require controls. OSHA's welding rule explains the importance of ventilation, protective clothing and confined-space precautions; it is US guidance used here for the process, not an assertion that US law governs Indian shops. Fume extraction, safer consumable formulations and reliable protective gear can influence supplier selection. An investor should still distinguish a genuine measured exposure reduction from a marketing label.

15 · Standards and policy

A classification is the floor; qualification is the commercial gate

The Bureau of Indian Standards product manual for IS 814 describes covered electrodes for manual metal arc welding of carbon and carbon-manganese steel. It sets a classification and tests such as dimensions, coating concentricity and deposition properties. This is a concrete product-control framework; it does not mean every electrode maker earns a specialty margin. ISO 2560:2020 similarly classifies covered electrodes and deposited metal by strength and impact-energy systems. A grade label helps specify what the product should do, while repeated consistent manufacture determines whether a buyer stays.

ISO 15614-1 addresses procedure qualification for arc and gas welding of steels and arc welding of nickel alloys. The American Welding Society's explanation distinguishes a Welding Procedure Specification, which tells the welder how to make a joint, from a Procedure Qualification Record, which documents the tests used to validate it. The exact governing code depends on the contract and asset. For an investor, the point is that changing filler, process or parameters in a critical application may require retesting and customer approval; the protection is application-specific, not universal across a brand's catalogue.

IEC 60974-1:2021 sets safety and performance requirements for professional arc-welding power sources and plasma-cutting systems. Meeting a standard is a prerequisite for relevant markets but not proof of high margin or an installed service network. Purchasers must still consider uptime, efficiency, support and total cost. The same distinction applies to customer approvals listed in company decks: a certificate is evidence of a gate passed, while repeat profitable shipments are evidence of an economic position.

Public infrastructure, rail, shipbuilding and defence orders can affect demand, but a policy announcement does not automatically become an order for one of these eight issuers. Procurement specifications, vendor registration, accepted trials, dispatch and cash collection each intervene. We therefore avoid assigning an unverified national welding market size or a budget multiplier to company revenue. The verifiable links are the firms' own order and results disclosures and the named technical standards.

16 · Where the cycle stands now

Sales momentum is real; the quality of profit is uneven

GEE (+67%), Ador (+49%) and Diffusion (+33%) led; Rasi (−3%), ESAB (−8%) and Classic (−31%) declined. This is an operating recovery in some companies, not proof of a uniform welding supercycle. 18 · Adjacent industries

Where welding appears without making the seller a welding supplier

Metal Fabrication (081) is the nearest customer chapter: a fabricator buys filler metal, gas, equipment and inspection services to turn plate into a structure. 20 · Myths and debates

Claims that sound plausible until the accounting is read

“Every welding stock is a consumable compounder.” Ador and ESAB sell equipment as well as filler; Diffusion has service and heavy engineering; Technocrats is machine-heavy; Rasandik uses welding to make auto parts. The frequency with which a customer buys an electrode cannot be applied to an entire company's sales. Ask for the recurring consumable share and the service attached to machines.

“A critical weld gives every wire a moat.” Weld failure can be costly, but standard electrodes can have several approved vendors and dealers. The moat depends on specific qualified grades, testing, delivery and switching costs. A broad catalogue or ISO label is necessary for some sales and insufficient as proof of an enduring price premium.

“GEE's PAT rose sevenfold, so welding margin rose sevenfold.” The company reported ₹6.85 cr June PAT versus ₹0.98 cr a year earlier, but ₹3.70 cr of the current profit is an exceptional property gain. Its own adjusted PAT is ₹3.15 cr. EBITDA and operating margin did improve, so removing the property gain does not erase the operating recovery; it puts the recovery at the right scale.

“Diffusion's consolidated PAT tells the whole operating story.” Its ₹16.68 cr group PAT includes about ₹4.46 cr from associates, while standalone PAT was about ₹9.98 cr and declined year on year. The equity-accounted share is economically real under the reporting rules, yet it is not the same as profit on consumables manufactured and sold by the parent. Use the bridge and ask how much can be distributed as cash.

“FY26 ESAB profit is one smooth run rate.” The September 2025 quarter had a ₹30.91 cr exceptional land gain, and December had a ₹13.65 cr exceptional charge associated with labour-code gratuity liabilities. The June 2026 quarter looks cleaner, with normal Other Income of ₹1.78 cr. Compare underlying operations across like periods instead of annualizing the largest reported quarter.

“A land bank is free upside to a welding multiple.” GEE's Thane development rights may have considerable value, but the deck's projected realization depends on a development, market price and multi-year timing. A valuation should show a separate probability- and timing-aware asset case, taxes and cash use. Capitalizing a land gain as perpetual welding earnings and also adding the full land value double-counts it.

“New listing means new industry evidence.” Technocrats' FY26 operating results are evidence for an equipment supplier. Twenty listed price observations are not evidence for a historical seasonality claim. Classic's one year and Diffusion's two years also affect a six-name current basket differently from the older four-name history. State the changing sample every time.

“Welding technology belongs only to the welding equipment industry.” Rasandik's laser and robotic welding are used to assemble vehicle body parts. The company sells those parts and electric vehicles, not welding consumables or power sources as its main output. Product classification follows what the customer buys, not a process visible on the factory floor.

“Seasonality proves a fiscal-quarter buying rule.” February–March underperformed in 12 of 15 year windows against the Nifty, but the basket is small, survivorship-biased and sensitive to equity-market size effects. April–July beat in only 8 of 15. Management shipment and order evidence must explain a calendar pattern before it is used as a forecast.

21 · Glossary

Processes, contracts and accounting in plain language

Joining and cutting processes

Arc
An electrical discharge that generates the heat needed to melt metal for welding or cutting.
Arc-on time
Time during which the welding arc actually operates, which differs from a worker's full shift.
Base metal
The original pieces being joined, as distinct from added filler metal.
Brazing
Joining metals with a filler that melts below the base metals' melting point.
Butt joint
A joint made where two pieces meet edge to edge.
CNC cutting
Computer-controlled movement of a cutting head along a programmed path.
Deposition rate
Mass of filler metal placed into a weld per unit of arc time.
FCAW
Flux-cored arc welding; a continuously fed hollow wire contains flux.
GMAW
Gas metal arc welding, often called MIG, using a continuously fed wire and shielding gas.
GTAW
Gas tungsten arc welding, often called TIG, using a non-consumable tungsten electrode.
Hardfacing
Depositing a wear-resistant layer on a part that faces abrasion or impact.
Heat input
Energy introduced into a weld length, influencing distortion and material properties.
Inverter power source
A welding machine using electronic conversion to control output current and voltage.
Kerf
The width of material removed by a cutting process.
Laser cutting
Using a focused laser beam to cut a programmed shape.
Plasma cutting
Using a high-energy ionized gas jet to cut conductive material.
Positioner
Machine that turns or holds a workpiece at a useful angle for welding.
Rework
Extra work needed to correct a weld or part that failed its first inspection.
Rotator
Equipment that rotates a cylindrical workpiece while it is welded.
SAW
Submerged arc welding; the arc operates beneath granular flux, often on thick plate.
Shielding gas
Gas protecting hot weld metal from unwanted reaction with the surrounding air.
SMAW
Shielded metal arc welding, using a flux-coated consumable stick electrode.
Spot welding
Resistance welding that joins sheet metal at individual spots, common in vehicle bodies.
Torch
Handheld or machine-mounted device delivering the arc, gas and sometimes filler.
Weld pool
Molten metal at the joint before it cools and solidifies.

Consumables, materials and quality

Alloy filler
Added weld metal formulated with elements such as nickel or chromium for specified properties.
Coating concentricity
How evenly flux surrounds an electrode core; uneven coating can affect the arc.
Consumable
A product used up in normal work and therefore bought repeatedly.
Covered electrode
A metal rod with a flux coating that is consumed during manual arc welding.
Flux
Material that helps protect or condition molten weld metal and may form slag.
Flux-cored wire
Continuous hollow filler wire containing flux ingredients.
Fume extraction
Removing welding fumes near the source to reduce worker exposure.
Hydrogen control
Limiting diffusible hydrogen to reduce cracking risk in susceptible welds.
Impact energy
Energy a tested material absorbs before fracture at a stated temperature.
Low-hydrogen electrode
Electrode designed and handled to limit hydrogen entering the weld.
MIG wire
Continuous solid or other filler wire fed into a gas metal arc process.
Non-destructive testing
Inspection, such as ultrasonic or radiographic testing, without cutting the finished part apart.
Powdered metal
Fine metal particles used in some fluxes, fillers and hardfacing formulations.
Procedure Qualification Record (PQR)
Document recording variables and test results used to approve a welding procedure.
Qualification
Testing and approval required before a product, procedure or supplier may be used.
Rejection rate
Share of output that fails the specified quality test or customer acceptance.
Shielded joint
Weld protected from harmful atmospheric contact by gas, flux or both.
Slag
Solidified protective residue from some flux-based welding processes, removed after welding.
Submerged-arc flux
Granular material covering the arc and weld pool in SAW.
Traceability
Ability to link a weld or wire batch to material, tests, production and destination.
Weld metal
Material that solidifies in the joint, including melted base and filler metal.
Welding Procedure Specification (WPS)
Instructions specifying how an approved weld is to be made.
Wire rod
Rolled steel input drawn into electrode cores or welding wire.

Customers, operations and contracts

Acceptance milestone
Contract point at which a buyer approves a delivered machine or project stage.
Approved vendor
Supplier that has passed a customer's stated tests and process checks.
Bill of materials
List of parts and quantities needed to build a product or machine.
Channel inventory
Products held at dealers before an end user consumes them.
Customer concentration
Share of a supplier's sales dependent on a small number of buyers.
Dealer sell-through
Actual sales from a dealer to end users rather than shipments into the dealer's stock.
Installed base
Machines already placed at customer sites, potentially needing service and parts.
Liquidated damages
Contracted payment or deduction associated with a stated failure such as delayed delivery.
Maintenance shutdown
Planned stoppage of a plant so equipment can be inspected or repaired.
Order book
Value of accepted orders not yet recognized as completed sales; terms can differ.
Pass-through
Contract method that adjusts customer prices when input costs change.
Project provision
Expense recognized for expected cost or contract loss before final cash payment.
Repeat customer
Buyer that returns for another order; the measurement period should be stated.
Site service
Technical installation, repair or application work performed at a customer's facility.
SKU
One specific stock item or variant in a supplier's catalogue.
Utilization
Actual output divided by stated installed capacity over a defined period.
Warranty reserve
Accounting estimate for future repair or replacement obligations on sold products.

Financial statements and price evidence

Adjusted PAT
Reported profit after identified adjustments; its definition and tax treatment must be stated.
Associate
Business in which an investor has significant influence but not full control.
Cash conversion
Extent to which accounting profit is reflected in cash generated by operations.
Consolidated accounts
Statements combining a parent with controlled subsidiaries and relevant group items.
Diluted EPS
Profit per share after accounting for potential new shares from eligible instruments.
EBITDA
Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization; definition can vary in presentations.
EPS
Earnings per share, usually owner-attributable profit divided by weighted-average shares.
Equity-accounted profit
The reporting company's share of an associate's or joint venture's profit.
Exceptional item
Separately disclosed gain or charge outside ordinary recurring operations.
Free cash flow
Operating cash after capital spending under a clearly stated definition.
Gross margin
Sales less direct product costs, divided by sales, using the company's stated basis.
Minority interest
Part of a consolidated subsidiary's profit belonging to owners other than the parent.
Operating cash flow
Cash generated by business operations after working-capital movements.
Other Income
Income outside ordinary product sales, such as interest; summary sites may group other items with it.
PAT
Profit after tax, requiring a check whether it is group total or owner-attributable.
PBT
Profit before tax after the items included by the stated reporting basis.
Price-only return
Share-price change excluding dividends and other distributions.
Receivable days
Customer credit outstanding expressed as an approximate number of sales days.
Standalone accounts
Results of the parent company alone, without controlled subsidiaries.
Survivorship bias
Distortion when a history uses only firms still listed at the end of the period.
Working capital
Funds tied up in inventory and receivables after trade credit and related current funding.

22 · Test yourself

Can you separate the weld, the asset and the reported profit?

1. Why can a welding electrode have recurring demand but weak pricing power?

It is used up and reordered, but a standard grade may have several acceptable suppliers. Pricing power depends on qualification, performance, availability and the customer's cost of changing a procedure.

2. Which two of the eight roster companies are outside the price basket?

Rasandik makes mainly automotive stampings, welded assemblies and vehicles rather than welding supplies. Technocrats makes relevant cutting and welding machines but had only 20 listed price bars from 21 August to 18 September 2026.

3. What happened to GEE's June 2026 reported PAT after the property adjustment?

Reported PAT was ₹6.85 cr. The company separately recognized a ₹3.70 cr gain on selling two properties and gives adjusted PAT of about ₹3.15 cr. Operating EBITDA also improved, so the adjustment changes the scale, not the direction, of the operating recovery.

4. Why does Diffusion's summary “Other Income” near ₹9 cr need opening up?

Actual Other Income was about ₹4.10 cr, while about ₹4.46 cr came from the separately shown share of associates. The summary combines unlike items. The group PAT of ₹16.68 cr also differs from parent standalone profit.

5. Why can ESAB's FY26 September PAT not simply be annualized?

That quarter included a ₹30.91 cr exceptional land-sale gain. December also had a ₹13.65 cr exceptional labour-code-related charge. Neither item is a normal quarterly welding margin.

6. Which header compares with the rightmost June 2026 quarterly column?

June 2025, not adjacent March 2026. For Classic the rightmost reported period is March 2026 half-year, compared with March 2025 half-year, because it reports as an SME.

7. How do we know Technocrats' FY26 profit is about ₹14.94 cr rather than ₹1,493 cr?

The annual report uses thousands of rupees. Profit 149,360.06 thousand is ₹14.936 cr. Its ₹11.63 EPS times about 1.284 cr weighted shares gives about ₹14.93 cr, confirming the conversion.

8. What did Classic's FY26 sales and EBITDA growth say about leverage?

Total income rose about 18.5%, but EBITDA only 7.1%. The new flux-cored product may improve mix later, yet the reported year did not show EBITDA growing as fast as sales.

9. What makes a machine vendor's installed base economically useful?

Accepted installations that remain running and buy service, spares or compatible consumables. The number of machines sold alone does not establish repeat revenue or cash return after warranty costs.

10. How does the February–March price pattern compare with a sound trading rule?

The basket lagged the Nifty in 12 of 15 February–March windows, median −6.7 points. It is a small, changing, price-only sample with broader size and survivorship effects; it does not establish a predictable factory or share-price rule.

11. Which three Engineering-family questions are carried into this primer?

From 078: who writes the specification and does the item wear out? From 080: where is the value beyond metal weight? From 081: who bears steel cost between quote and delivery? Welding's consumable, procedure and project economics make all three relevant.

12. Why does Rasandik use welding yet remain outside this industry's return basket?

It welds vehicle parts in its own production, but the customer buys stampings, body assemblies and vehicles. The basket is intended to represent sellers of welding consumables, equipment or application services, not every manufacturer with a weld shop.
