1 · The industry in one minute

Explain it to a ten-year-old.

A factory needs more than electricity. Some machines need a strong stream of squeezed air, rather like a much more powerful bicycle pump. A compressor supplies that air. Other machines need a shaft to turn even when there is no reliable electrical connection; an engine burns fuel to make that happen. Connect the engine to a generator and it can provide electricity when the mains fails. The customer wants the factory, vehicle or computer building to keep working. The metal machine is only one part of that promise.

This primer explicitly merges two watchlists: Compressors, with three names, and Industrial Engines, with four. Seven companies are researched; six enter the historical price basket. ELGI Equipments, Ingersoll-Rand India and Kirloskar Pneumatic form the compressor side. Cummins India, Greaves Cotton and Kirloskar Oil Engines form the engine side. Scan Projects stays in the company discussion but is excluded from the basket: its filings describe machinery erection, commissioning, maintenance and trading, not industrial-engine manufacturing. Its discontinuous price history supplies an additional reason for caution. The merge is an organising choice, not a claim that the businesses have identical economics.

The practical investment distinction is between selling a machine and earning through its working life. A supplier can earn from design, manufacturing, installation, replacement parts, repairs and maintenance. It can also spend heavily on product development, overseas distribution or a new plant before those opportunities become cash. Qualification and reliability make the product important to the customer, but shareholder returns still depend on pricing, retained manufacturing value, service capture and the capital committed to deliver it. The three Engineering tests from 078, 080 and 081 are applied below at those decision points.

The one-minute client pitch.

These companies sell useful air and dependable power. Factories buying equipment, customers replacing inefficient compressors, and buildings installing backup generation can all create demand. The more attractive supplier can demonstrate performance, support the installed machine locally and collect repeat service revenue. However, June 2026 evidence shows revenue growth can coexist with weaker operating margins. Financial subsidiaries, minority losses, overseas costs and changing emission requirements also make reported profit harder to compare. Start with the exact business and the cash it produces; use the share-price calendar as context.

2 · What customers actually buy

Compressed air is a delivered service

A compressor raises the pressure of a gas. The customer specifies the quantity of air or gas, required pressure, acceptable contamination, temperature and pattern of demand. Those requirements determine the machine and the surrounding equipment. A buyer comparing only motor horsepower or the purchase price can miss a much larger difference in usable output. Pressure measured at the machine outlet is also not necessarily pressure available at the production line. Filters, dryers, piping and leaks affect the delivered result. The right unit of analysis is the complete air system at the customer's operating condition.

Reciprocating compressors use pistons. Rotary-screw compressors use intermeshing rotors. Centrifugal compressors use rotating impellers and a different pressure-building mechanism. These are engineering alternatives for particular flow, pressure and duty requirements, rather than a simple ladder from inferior to superior. A machine efficient at one operating point may be a poor choice when demand changes sharply. A supplier that understands the customer's load pattern can sometimes create more value through selection and controls than through selling a larger compressor. The industry's product catalogues should therefore be read alongside the intended application. KPCL product families · ELGI product and business discussion · Ingersoll-Rand manufacturing scope

Air quality introduces another choice. Oil-injected and oil-free designs serve different needs. The label oil-free should be interpreted within the stated test and application, together with the rest of the treatment system. It is not a blanket promise that any installation will meet every food, pharmaceutical or electronics requirement. A customer may need filtration, drying and monitoring after compression. Conversely, paying for a specification the process does not need can worsen the economic outcome. Kirloskar Pneumatic's A800 presentation specifies Class 0 oil-free air and a defined pressure and flow range; those stated conditions matter more than a broad marketing adjective. A800 specifications, slide 13

Gas compression is another business within the merged category. Refrigeration, process gas, CNG and biogas packages involve distinct fluids, safety requirements and project interfaces. An air-compressor market estimate should not be applied to the entire revenue of a company supplying these systems. Kirloskar Pneumatic's range spans these duties and also includes precision engineering. ELGI's consolidated reporting separates air compressors and automotive equipment. Ingersoll-Rand India describes installation, commissioning and maintenance alongside equipment. These differences help explain why three apparently comparable compressor shares can have different order cycles, margins and working-capital needs. An engine is not automatically a complete generator

An engine converts fuel into mechanical power. A generator set adds an alternator and the control, cooling, exhaust and other equipment needed for a specified electrical duty. The engine maker may sell the engine to a packager, supply a complete system, or participate through a distribution network. Those routes leave different amounts of value and warranty responsibility with the listed company. A data-centre backup installation also has site-level design, switching, redundancy and maintenance requirements. The engine supplier's addressable revenue is only the portion it actually supplies and supports.

Power ratings must be compared on the same basis. Mechanical kilowatts at the engine, electrical kilowatts at the output and kilovolt-amperes at a specified power factor are related but distinct measures. Standby and sustained operating duties also differ. A bigger nameplate does not by itself prove suitability for a customer's load changes, ambient conditions or permitted operating hours. For investment research, this means asking which models have been qualified, what is manufactured locally, what is imported, and which revenue the listed entity recognises. An exciting end-market forecast is not an order ledger.

Cummins' August call describes domestic power generation, distribution and industrial demand separately, with exports as another channel. KOEL's presentation divides its industrial group into Power & Energy, Fluid Dynamics and Financial Services. Greaves combines engines and engineering products with electric mobility and vehicle finance. These are not three pure generators whose entire sales can be multiplied by a common data-centre growth assumption. The product-to-entity map must come before a forecast. The useful analogy is a workshop with both an air line and a backup power room. The workshop buys the initial machines only occasionally, but filters, lubricants, inspections and repairs recur. Some recurring spend goes to the original equipment maker, some to dealers, and some to independent service providers. The analyst's job is to discover who captures it. A large installed base is the starting population of machines that might produce service revenue. It is not the same thing as a disclosed contracted revenue stream.

3 · The value chain and where value stays

The chain begins with the customer's process. A factory engineer, consultant, original-equipment manufacturer or large institutional buyer determines the required performance. The supplier selects a design and procures castings, forgings, bearings, rotors, motors, electronic controls and other inputs. It machines and assembles them, tests the package, delivers it, supports installation and remains responsible for agreed warranties. A distributor or packager may sit between the manufacturer and the final user. Each interface can create value, expense or a delay in collecting cash.

Apply the 078 specification-and-wear test at the customer interface. A qualified compressor or engine can remain on an approved list for a long period, but the replacement component may be sourced elsewhere. Ask which parts require the original maker, whether warranties depend on approved maintenance, and whether the service network can reach the installed equipment. Do not infer service exclusivity from the importance of the product. A technically critical machine can still face several qualified suppliers at the next buying decision.

Apply 080's processing-depth test inside the factory. A supplier that designs, machines and validates a core unit owns a different economic activity from one mainly assembling bought-out hardware. Both may be profitable, but their capital requirements, pricing power and supply risks differ. Greaves' discussion of extending its own engine range is relevant because retained manufacturing scope may change. It remains a proposition to test against realised margins and returns. The number of catalogue models is not a measure of the amount of value retained in each sale. Greaves on its own engine range

Apply 081's quote-to-delivery test to the order. An agreed selling price can precede the purchase of castings, copper-containing equipment, controls or imported components. Input inflation then reaches the income statement before a price increase can be accepted. A large order backlog is not protection if it contains fixed prices and variable costs. ELGI's call explicitly says price corrections should show up later than the June cost pressure. KOEL and Cummins also discuss commodity and cost pressure. These are direct examples of the family test, not reasons to repeat the fabrication primer. ELGI pricing timing · KOEL costs and mix · Cummins cost and pricing discussion

Installation creates a further division of responsibility. A well-made unit may sit idle because the site is not ready, electrical work is incomplete or a commissioning condition has not been satisfied. Who pays the supplier during that interval depends on the contract. Customer advances can fund production, while retention payments can keep part of the profit tied up long after shipment. Research should distinguish equipment dispatch, site acceptance and final collection. Treat a release mentioning successful commissioning differently from an announcement that a purchase order has merely been received.

Service changes the chain again. A replacement part sold through a dealer may be recognised before the final repair is completed. A maintenance contract may include labour, consumables and response obligations. More service contracts can make revenue steadier, but only if contract prices cover the cost of meeting those promises. KOEL's call describes initiatives to increase its service-contract base; Cummins identifies distribution sales separately. Those disclosures are more useful than assuming a fixed aftermarket percentage for every member. Neither should be extrapolated to Scan's project-maintenance activity without evidence. KOEL service initiatives · Cummins distribution

Fund-manager lens — follow the obligation.

The most valuable position is not necessarily the company with the longest product list. Look for a supplier that controls a meaningful part of the design, can recover cost changes, keeps a service relationship and gets paid after acceptable execution. June evidence shows these links can break independently: equipment revenue rises while margins soften, a group subsidiary consumes cash, or an installation business appears under the wrong industry label. Underwrite each obligation before assigning the whole company a premium for being critical infrastructure.

8 · Critical inputs, pricing and working capital

The visible machine contains several supply chains. Castings and forgings become precision-machined housings and moving parts. Bearings, seals, motors, alternators and controls make the package work. Electronic components, specialised alloys and imported subassemblies can be critical even when they are a small share of the bill. The bottleneck is the item that prevents a tested product from shipping, not necessarily the most expensive item. Earlier primers on castings, forgings, bearings and transmission cover those supplier economics.

For the equipment maker, procurement is only the first stage of cost. Machining yield, scrap recovery, assembly time, testing failures, warranty claims and field visits determine the realised contribution. A company can report stable material cost as a percentage of sales while labour or warranty expenses deteriorate. Conversely, a favourable product mix can offset raw-material inflation temporarily. Ask management to separate price, volume, mix, currency and productivity. Without that bridge, a margin improvement can be incorrectly assigned to a permanent technology advantage.

The June evidence makes the timing issue concrete. ELGI identifies raw-material costs, tariffs and mix as pressure on contribution, while expecting later pricing action. Cummins says cost inflation and employee expenses affected the quarter and discusses pricing responses. KOEL cites lower export volumes, commodity costs and investments in people and capabilities. Greaves describes both commodity pressure and purposeful capability spending. These explanations are management statements to test against the next comparable period; they are not independent proof that every cost is temporary. ELGI · Cummins · KOEL · Greaves

Inventory can protect delivery reliability while hurting cash returns. A company may hold a long-lead imported part to reduce disruption, a finished machine for a dealer, or project work awaiting an acceptance milestone. Those balances have different risks. Finished goods can become slow-moving after a specification change; imported components can carry currency exposure; project work can require further cost before it becomes billable. Management's statement that inventory supports growth is a hypothesis. Subsequent dispatch, receivable collection and inventory provisions reveal whether it was well judged.

Receivables deserve the same detail. Dealer credit is not the same as a retention receivable from a large project. A foreign subsidiary's receivable may be collectible but exposed to translation and transfer timing. A warranty holdback can be economically earned but unavailable for reinvestment. Compare receivable growth with the matching revenue base, ask about overdue balances and avoid using a single closing ratio as a complete cash-conversion diagnosis. KOEL's annual consolidated cash flow already shows why financial loans must be separated from industrial trade receivables. Cash-flow working-capital categories

Customer economics can support pricing, but the benefit has to be demonstrated. A compressor saving electricity may justify a higher purchase price; a standby engine reducing operational risk may justify a qualified premium. Neither establishes unlimited pricing power. Competitors may offer similar performance, customers may delay purchases, or the supplier may carry too much service cost. The strongest argument combines a measured customer benefit with evidence that the manufacturer retains part of that benefit after warranty and selling costs. A technically better product with an uneconomic selling price is not a shareholder moat.

9 · The operating calendar

The operating year contains several clocks. Customers approve budgets, place orders, prepare sites, accept equipment and pay invoices on different dates. A compressor delivered to an operating factory may have a short path to acceptance; a refrigeration package for a new plant may wait on other contractors. A generator ordered for a building can be supplied before the building is fully occupied. The manufacturer's financial quarter is therefore an imperfect map of the customer's capital-spending cycle.

For project-oriented businesses, the March quarter can be large because of dispatch and acceptance schedules, but the size of one March quarter does not establish a universal seasonal law. KPCL's June filing shows March 2026 consolidated operating revenue of ₹711.8 crore compared with ₹303.1 crore in June. Its March result also includes a ₹4.2 crore labour-code reversal. The difference between those quarters combines a very different volume of execution with a different exceptional-item comparison. Annualising the March margin would require a strong assumption about the next year's project mix. Quarterly statement

A customer can accelerate a purchase ahead of a regulatory change, creating a strong shipment period followed by a pause. Dealers can also build inventory ahead of a price increase. The equipment maker recognises sales according to its accounting policy, but end-user demand may arrive later. Ask for dealer stock, installation and warranty-registration evidence where available. Without it, primary dispatch growth is an incomplete demand signal. The same caution applies when a supplier reports a large new product launch but has not yet established repeated field orders.

Maintenance has its own timing. A factory may schedule work during shutdowns, a rental fleet may service machines between deployments, and an emergency-power customer may require periodic testing even with few actual outage hours. These demands can soften the cyclicality of new equipment, but their timing and profitability depend on the contract. A service business with labour, travel and parts obligations is not costless recurring revenue. The best evidence is revenue and contribution from the installed base, accompanied by renewal, collection and warranty data.

Export deliveries add transit, distributor and currency timing. A quarter of high rupee revenue can reflect exchange translation, a change in the geographic mix or a shipment concentrated near period-end. ELGI's presentation explicitly identifies an exchange contribution to growth. KOEL describes slower international demand linked to geopolitical pressures, while domestic businesses grew. Treat those disclosures as explanations of particular routes and quarters. They do not justify applying a single “exports up” or “exports down” assumption to the entire merged industry. 11 · Cycles and the reasons shares diverge

Demand, conversion and expectations are separate cycles

The first cycle is customer spending: factories expand, projects begin, equipment is replaced and backup capacity is installed. The second is the supplier's conversion cycle: capacity, procurement, labour, pricing and working capital turn that demand into profit and cash. The third is the market's expectation cycle: the share price changes as investors revise future earnings and the multiple they will pay. A company can be improving in the first cycle and deteriorating in the second. The June data for some engine groups is a current example.

Data-centre demand illustrates the distinction. Cummins describes strong high-horsepower demand and customer engagement, but also cost pressure and some supply constraints. KOEL reports an important data-centre order for its modular architecture, while its current B2B segment profit falls. Greaves discusses a larger own-engine range and strategic supply relationships, while EV losses continue. The application may be attractive, but the current economic evidence belongs to different products and reporting entities. Do not collapse them into a single artificial-intelligence earnings story. Price outliers are not automatically operating outliers

In 2025, the basket lost 7.4% while Nifty gained 10.5%. Cummins and KOEL nevertheless rose approximately 35% and 19%, while the other four fell. That is a dispersion year, rather than evidence that every compressor and engine company entered the same downturn. In 2026 all six recovered positively to the cut, but KOEL's 77.2% gain was much larger than the rest. Its latest owner profit was lower year on year. The share-price move therefore cannot be explained by simply repeating the current reported profit growth rate.

The larger historical years also need breadth checks. In 2014 every included stock rose, with Greaves and Ingersoll-Rand among the strongest. In 2021 all six rose again, with KPCL a particularly large winner. In 2024 KPCL led another strong basket year, while ELGI's full-year rise was much smaller. This supports different descriptions of leadership within a rising group. It does not establish that the same product line, macro event or valuation change caused each stock's return; that would require a separate contemporaneous earnings and valuation reconstruction.

A useful operating-cycle checklist starts with the source of the order. Replacement of a failed machine is less discretionary than capacity expansion at an uncertain project. A customer's energy-saving purchase may depend on measured payback and access to capital. A compliance-driven purchase may cluster around a rule change. A distributor's restocking order can reverse if end demand disappoints. The same revenue growth rate can therefore carry very different information about the next quarter. Ask about order type and final customer use, rather than relying on a broad industry growth adjective.

The conversion checklist then asks what has changed inside the supplier. A new plant can improve capacity but initially raise depreciation and overhead. Localisation can reduce import exposure but require testing and development expense. A richer product mix can improve contribution while stretching receivables. A price increase can support margins after a lag, but may provoke competitive discounting. Management should be able to explain these moving parts separately. Statements about long-term opportunity do not resolve a missing short-term margin bridge.

Finally, expectations require a valuation discipline. A business with visible demand can still disappoint if its share price assumes uninterrupted margin expansion, immediate new-plant utilisation and perfect cash conversion. This primer does not manufacture a target price from a generic sector multiple. It identifies the variables that a company-level valuation must model. At a minimum, distinguish organic sales, currency effects, industrial operating profit, investment income, associate earnings, minority claims, tax and reinvestment. The resulting owner cash flow is the relevant claim to value.

14 · Technology that changes the economics

Efficiency must be measured over the actual duty

The US Department of Energy's compressed-air sourcebook treats the system as a whole: pressure, flow, controls, leaks and treatment equipment affect performance together. A new compressor is one possible intervention. For an investment thesis, this suggests asking whether the supplier can document the customer's operating savings, how much service work is required to achieve them, and how much of that benefit becomes the supplier's margin. A headline efficiency claim is weaker than a measured operating result. Compressed-air system sourcebook

A variable-speed drive changes motor speed as demand changes. It can be valuable in a suitable duty, but selection still requires the demand profile and operating range. A buyer with steady base demand may require a different combination of machines and controls from one with sharp swings. The supplier's opportunity lies in choosing and supporting the appropriate arrangement. Selling the most expensive control package is not the same as solving the customer's problem. This distinction matters when valuing a manufacturer that describes itself as a solutions provider.

Oil-free technology introduces product and qualification opportunities. KPCL's A800 centrifugal model is positioned at a smaller capacity range within its Tezcatlipoca family. The presentation specifies 600–980 CFM, discharge pressure of 3–8.5 bar gauge and Class 0 air purity. Those specifications define the offered application; they are not a revenue forecast. The next milestone is a satisfactory field installation followed by repeat orders, not simply the date of launch. A800 technical range · First-installation approach

Remote monitoring can improve fault detection and maintenance planning. Economically, it can strengthen the manufacturer's connection to the installed base, but it also creates support obligations and requires usable field data. Ask whether monitoring produces paid contracts, fewer warranty visits, better renewal rates or higher parts attachment. A dashboard demonstration does not establish recurring profit. The same caution applies to digitally connected engines and generator sets: connectivity must improve a customer's result or the supplier's service economics to justify investment.

Cleaner engines add capability and cost

Engine development involves combustion, fuel systems, controls, thermal management and emissions treatment. A stricter application requirement can raise engineering and testing demands, changing which suppliers can compete. It can also add bought-out equipment and warranty complexity. Higher selling prices after a product transition therefore do not automatically mean higher margins. Large backup-power applications require more than a larger engine. 15 · Policy, certification and practical limits

The CPCB certification document refers to GSR 804(E), dated 3 November 2022, for new internal-combustion engines up to 800 kW gross mechanical power used in generator-set applications. The scope matters: a rule for a specified engine class and application is not a universal rule for every industrial engine. ARAI describes genset emissions and noise certification services. Compliance claims should be checked against the applicable product, rating, test and certificate rather than inferred from the company name. CPCB system and procedure · ARAI genset certification

Manufacturing or product certification and permission to operate at a particular site are different questions. A customer may face local environmental, noise, fuel, building or operational conditions in addition to the product standard. This primer does not turn a general product-compliance statement into a site-specific legal opinion. For demand analysis, the relevant point is that the customer must buy an appropriate system and satisfy its operating requirements. A policy headline alone does not reveal the timing or size of a purchase order.

Regulation can change competition in several ways. Engineering and certification costs can favour suppliers able to spread those costs over a larger product base. New components can increase selling prices and working capital. A changeover can alter dealer stocks and buying dates. At the same time, a smaller supplier with a suitable qualified product can remain competitive in a specific duty. The research task is to identify which mechanism appears in the company's actual sales and costs, rather than declaring every rule change an automatic margin windfall.

19 · Questions for the next earnings call

Good questions should ask for a measurable bridge and make the period and perimeter explicit. Management may not disclose every requested number. The analyst should retain that gap rather than replace it with an unsupported assumption. 20 · The debates worth having

Does essential equipment deserve a defensive valuation?

Dependable compressed air and backup power are important to customers. That importance can support qualification and service demand, but new equipment purchases still depend on budgets, replacement timing and project execution. Share prices also reflect expectations. The basket's negative years and wide company dispersion show that essential products can produce volatile equity outcomes. A defensive valuation argument needs evidence of recurring owner cash flow, not simply the cost of a customer outage.

Will data centres benefit every engine company equally?

The evidence does not support equal exposure. Cummins discusses specific high-horsepower platforms and customer relationships. KOEL describes an order for modular architecture. Greaves discusses its own engine range and larger-engine supply partnerships. These are different business positions. The useful debate concerns qualification, available capacity, retained content, service and price. Treating every industrial-engine rupee as data-centre revenue would erase the actual product mix. Cummins · KOEL · Greaves

Is a high-margin compressor automatically the best investment?

A strong current margin may reflect a good franchise, favourable mix, low investment spending or a point in the cycle. Investment returns also depend on growth, capital employed, valuation and cash allocation. A lower-margin company can improve through a proven ramp; a high-margin company can disappoint if expectations assume perpetual expansion. Compare operating definitions first. KPCL's presentation and consolidated statements show how an apparently simple margin ranking can begin with mismatched numerators and denominators.

Should Greaves and KOEL be removed because they are mixed businesses?

They have relevant engine operations, so the main basket retains them with explicit scope disclosure. The sensitivities show how results change without each. This approach permits the reader to examine the watchlist honestly while avoiding a pure-play claim. A different research question, such as valuing only industrial operations, would require a different model. The price of a listed group cannot be separated into a traded engine-only series merely by removing finance profit from a spreadsheet.

Does a split-adjusted EPS difference imply a source error?

KPCL shows why the adjustment basis must be checked first. The original result predates the August subdivision; a later historical summary can restate per-share values. Both can be internally meaningful on their own basis. ELGI presents another issue, where weighted shares differ from closing capital. Scan presents a PAT-versus-comprehensive-income narrative trap. Each apparent discrepancy requires its own diagnosis. The appropriate response is a reconciled explanation or a clearly stated unresolved difference, rather than automatic distrust or automatic acceptance.

Can management's investment explanation settle a weak quarter?

Spending on people, technology and capacity can be sensible. It can also remain expensive for longer than expected. The right response is to identify the promised output, time frame and capital required, then review subsequent evidence. Greaves and KOEL describe capability investments while margins are under pressure. Ingersoll-Rand's new facility is at an early stage. These are testable propositions. Calling every expense an investment does not make it disappear from the shareholder's return.

Is the calendar useful if it is not a reliable trade?

Yes, as a way to organise research and challenge anecdotes. The April results show the difference between a positive average and a dependable hit rate. The subgroup results show how a merge can change the story. The weak years provide counterexamples to easy rules. That is valuable context for a decision, provided the decision still rests on the particular company's earnings, cash and valuation. Historical seasonality should sharpen judgment rather than replace it.

21 · Glossary: find a term

Air, gas and compression

Compressor
A machine that raises the pressure of a gas. Its investment relevance depends on the duty, delivered flow, operating cost and service relationship, rather than the label alone.
Compressed-air system
The compressor together with treatment, storage, controls and distribution. The customer experiences the performance of this complete arrangement; a good machine cannot by itself guarantee a good installation.
Air end
The core compression assembly in a compressor. Ownership of its design and manufacturing process helps identify how much technical content the supplier retains inside the packaged machine.
Reciprocating compressor
A compressor using piston motion. Suitability depends on the required flow, pressure and duty. It should be compared with alternatives at the customer’s specified operating conditions.
Rotary-screw compressor
A compressor using rotating intermeshing rotors. Product economics depend on design, manufacture, controls, treatment and service, rather than assuming all screw packages carry an identical margin.
Centrifugal compressor
A compressor using rotating impellers and aerodynamic compression. Its applicable flow range and operating characteristics differ from positive-displacement machines, so the actual application matters in comparisons.
Positive displacement
A compression approach that traps and reduces the volume of gas. It is an engineering category, not a measure of selling price, product quality or shareholder profitability.
Oil-injected compression
A design in which oil participates in the compression process. The required downstream air quality determines the treatment system; the design name alone does not describe final delivered purity.
Oil-free compression
A design and performance claim concerning oil in the compression process or delivered air under specified conditions. Read the relevant specification and treatment requirements before making application-wide claims.
Class 0
An air-purity classification referenced in product specifications. Check the stated test, conditions and applicable standard; the phrase is not a universal guarantee for every installed air system.
CFM
Cubic feet per minute, a measure of volumetric flow. Comparisons need consistent reference conditions; the same-looking number can describe different useful output if the measurement basis differs.
Gauge pressure
Pressure measured relative to ambient atmospheric pressure. A specification in bar gauge differs from absolute pressure, so the reference basis must be retained when comparing equipment.
Discharge pressure
Pressure at the compressor’s outlet under stated conditions. The customer’s usable pressure can differ after treatment and distribution, making the full installation relevant to product selection.
Flow turndown
The ability to operate over a reduced flow range relative to a stated reference. The exact definition and performance within that range matter more than an isolated headline percentage.
Part-load operation
Running below the machine’s rated output. Efficiency and control behaviour at these conditions can be important when customer demand varies substantially through a shift or production cycle.
Variable-speed drive
An electrical control that changes motor speed. Its usefulness depends on the duty and operating range; it should be evaluated within the proposed system and customer demand profile.
Air receiver
A vessel used to store compressed air within a system. Its function should be considered alongside controls and demand patterns when understanding the supplier’s complete package.
Air dryer
Equipment that removes moisture from compressed air to meet the specified duty. It adds to the system’s capital, operating and maintenance requirements and can create additional supplier scope.
Pressure drop
The reduction in pressure as gas passes through components or piping. It affects the relationship between the compressor specification and the useful condition delivered to the customer.
Process-gas compressor
A compressor designed for a particular gas and process duty. Fluid properties, safety and customer qualification can make its economics different from a standard industrial-air application.
Refrigeration package
A system supplied for a cooling duty, potentially including compression and related equipment. Package dispatch and acceptance can be lumpy, so revenue should be read with execution schedules.
Total cost of ownership
The purchase, energy, maintenance and other relevant costs over a defined operating life. For research, check the assumptions and whether the supplier can retain part of the customer benefit.

Engines, power and product qualification

Industrial engine
An engine supplying mechanical power for an industrial application. The relevant market depends on duty and customer, rather than treating every engine as a vehicle engine or generator.
Generator set
An engine-driven electrical generating package with the required associated systems. Revenue retained by the engine maker depends on whether it supplies the engine, package, distribution or service.
Alternator
The electrical machine converting mechanical input into electrical output in a generating set. A supplier buying this component retains different manufacturing scope from one producing it internally.
Mechanical kW
A measure of mechanical power, relevant to engine ratings and specified regulatory scopes. It should not be substituted automatically for the electrical output rating of the complete generator.
Electrical kW
A measure of real electrical power delivered to the load. It describes a different point in the energy-conversion chain from engine mechanical output and requires consistent operating assumptions.
kVA
Kilovolt-amperes, a measure of apparent electrical power. Comparison with real power requires the relevant power factor; a kVA headline alone does not specify all aspects of a duty.
Standby duty
A specified backup operating duty. The permitted performance and operating conditions should be checked in the product rating rather than assuming the unit is interchangeable with continuous generation.
Load acceptance
The ability of a generating system to respond to an applied electrical load within specified performance limits. Qualification depends on the customer’s actual requirement, not simply the engine size.
Redundancy
Additional capacity or equipment intended to maintain service when part of a system is unavailable. Its design affects project scope, but project capacity is not automatically one supplier’s revenue.
Modular power architecture
An arrangement using multiple coordinated units or modules. Its commercial value needs evidence of qualified applications, orders and delivered economics; the architecture name is not an earnings forecast.
High horsepower
A broad industry description for larger engine applications. The exact product range and customer duty should be stated before drawing conclusions about margins or data-centre exposure.
Backup power
Power available when the normal supply is unavailable or unsuitable. Customer spending depends on reliability needs, system design and operating requirements, rather than only the frequency of outages.
Aftertreatment
Equipment used to treat engine exhaust after combustion. It can add engineering, components and service requirements, so a higher compliant-product price does not automatically imply a higher margin.
Emissions certification
Evidence that a defined product meets specified requirements under the applicable process. Check scope, model and conditions; it does not itself establish permission for every installation to operate.
CPCB
Central Pollution Control Board. The cited genset procedure defines a particular product and application scope; analysts should retain that scope when linking regulation to company demand.
ARAI
Automotive Research Association of India. Its certification services include specified genset tests; a reference to the institution is not a substitute for the actual product certificate and coverage.
Type approval
Approval concerning a defined product type under a specified framework. It should be distinguished from a customer’s procurement approval and from the supplier’s general management-system certification.
Conformity of production
The requirement that continuing production remains consistent with the applicable approved specification. It creates an ongoing quality responsibility rather than a one-time marketing credential.
Reference installation
A working deployment that can demonstrate performance to later customers. Early installations can support adoption but also reveal service and warranty costs that a launch presentation cannot settle.
OEM
Original equipment manufacturer. Identify whether the listed company is the final machine maker, a component supplier or a customer-facing packager before assigning it the economics of the full system.
Packager
A business assembling equipment and associated components into a usable system. It may create meaningful integration value while retaining less core manufacturing content than the engine or compressor designer.
Warranty provision
An accounting estimate of future costs under warranty obligations. Compare later claims and releases with the estimate to assess whether current margins fairly reflect expected field-support costs.

Orders, operations and commercial economics

Order board
A company’s disclosed stock of orders awaiting execution. Definitions vary; check cancellations, scope, timing, advances and price terms before converting it into a revenue forecast.
Order intake
Orders booked during a stated period. It differs from revenue executed and from closing backlog, and should be analysed with cancellations and the opening order position.
Enquiry
An expression of possible customer interest. It can indicate an opportunity, but should remain separate from an accepted purchase order and from recognised sales in a forecast.
Dispatch
Shipment of equipment from the supplier. Depending on the contract and accounting policy, dispatch, revenue recognition, customer acceptance and final cash collection may occur at different times.
Commissioning
The process of making an installation operational and confirming specified performance. It can be a contractual milestone with implications for acceptance, warranty commencement and release of payment.
Retention money
A portion of payment withheld under contract until specified conditions are met. It can leave accounting profit tied up in receivables beyond the main delivery or commissioning date.
Customer advance
Cash collected before the associated revenue is fully recognised. It can fund production, but also creates an obligation to deliver the agreed equipment or service.
Installed base
The population of machines already in service. It creates potential replacement and maintenance demand; only some of that demand may become paid revenue for the original supplier.
Aftermarket
Parts, repairs, maintenance and related activity after the original equipment sale. Profitability depends on customer access, competition, contract scope and the cost of providing service.
Service attachment
The extent to which equipment customers buy service from the supplier or its network. A large machine population without paid attachment is a weaker recurring-revenue proposition.
Channel inventory
Stock held by distributors or dealers. Higher manufacturer dispatches can raise this inventory before final customer sales, so primary sales and end-user demand should be distinguished.
Localisation
Moving specified manufacturing or sourcing into a local location. Its economic benefit depends on quality, scale, development costs and retained value, rather than the label alone.
Capacity utilisation
Output relative to an appropriately defined available capacity. The denominator and product mix matter, especially for diverse machines whose production requirements are not interchangeable.
Fixed-cost absorption
The spread of fixed operating costs over output. A new plant may initially absorb these costs poorly; later volume can help only if contribution per unit remains adequate.
Product mix
The composition of sales across products, customers or applications. Changes can affect margins even when volume and price within each individual product remain unchanged.
Price-reset lag
The time between a cost change and recovery through selling prices. It is central to assessing margins on previously quoted or contracted equipment orders.
Natural currency hedge
An offset between revenues and costs in the same currency. Its effectiveness depends on the actual timing and amounts, and cannot be inferred solely from export sales.
Working capital
Operating money tied up in inventory and receivables, net of relevant operating liabilities. Financial-services loans require separate interpretation within a mixed industrial group.
Cash conversion
The relationship between reported earnings and cash generated over an appropriate period. Read classification, working-capital movements and group structure before judging a simple ratio.
Maintenance capex
Investment needed to preserve existing operating capability. It may not be separately disclosed, so avoid treating all reported asset spending as optional growth expenditure.
Growth capex
Investment intended to expand capacity, products or capability. Its return depends on future utilisation and contribution; spending the money is not evidence that the return has been earned.
Capital allocation
Management’s decisions about reinvestment, acquisitions, subsidiaries, debt and distributions. A strong operating franchise can still produce weak owner returns if incremental money is deployed poorly.

Accounts, share prices and research controls

Ordinary Other Income
The statutory non-operating-income line before separately disclosed exceptions or associate contributions. A financial website’s expanded summary may combine several of these categories, requiring a source bridge.
Other operating income
Income classified within operating revenue, distinct from ordinary Other Income. Cummins presents it separately from its main sales line, so the chosen revenue denominator must be explicit.
Operating EBITDA bridge
An explicitly defined calculation from pre-exception operating profit, adding interest and depreciation and removing non-operating income. Always state exclusions and avoid applying an industrial formula to a lender.
Exceptional item
A separately disclosed material item under the company’s reporting. Separation helps analysis, but does not prove that similar costs can never recur or that the item has no cash effect.
PAT
Profit after tax. In consolidated accounts, distinguish total group profit from the amount attributable to the listed company’s owners before comparing it with earnings per share.
Owner profit
The portion of consolidated profit attributable to owners of the parent. This is generally the numerator relevant to the listed parent’s EPS, subject to the specific EPS-note adjustments.
Non-controlling interest
The claim of other shareholders in consolidated subsidiaries. Their share of losses can make parent-owner profit higher than total group profit, as the Greaves example demonstrates.
Associate contribution
The investor’s recognised share of an associate’s result under the applicable accounting treatment. It is distinct from ordinary operating revenue and from dividend cash received during the year.
Standalone accounts
Financial statements of the individual listed legal entity. They can exclude subsidiary operations and include dividends that are treated differently in the consolidated reporting group.
Consolidated accounts
Financial statements presenting the parent and controlled businesses together under the applicable accounting rules. The group may still contain several very different economic activities.
Reporting perimeter
The entities and operations included in a financial comparison. Acquisitions, disposals and internal transfers can change it, making restated or continuing-operation comparisons necessary.
Discontinued operations
Operations presented separately under the applicable accounting criteria. Keep their profit and cash effects distinct from continuing business when evaluating recurring growth or margin.
OCI
Other comprehensive income, reported outside the ordinary profit figure. Adding it to PAT produces comprehensive income, which should not be substituted casually for the profit used in EPS.
Basic EPS
Profit attributable to ordinary shares divided by the weighted average share count on the relevant basis. It should be checked against the actual profit and denominator in the filing.
Diluted EPS
A per-share measure reflecting applicable potential dilution. Adjustments can affect both the denominator and, in cases such as KOEL’s subsidiary options, the numerator.
Weighted average shares
Shares outstanding over the reporting period, weighted for timing and applicable adjustments. Closing issued capital can differ and may produce an incorrect EPS cross-check.
Share subdivision
A change dividing existing shares into smaller-face-value shares. Comparable price, share count and EPS must be adjusted consistently; the event does not itself create operating profit.
Net cash
Cash or defined liquid resources less specified debt. Company definitions vary, so check included investments, restrictions and reporting perimeter before comparing net-cash figures.
Equal-weight basket
A model assigning the same weight to each included stock at its reset date. It differs from a market-cap-weighted industry index and can give smaller names substantial influence.
Relative return
The difference between basket and benchmark returns over the same period. A positive relative result can coexist with a negative absolute return and should be labelled in percentage points.
Hit rate
The number of periods beating the benchmark divided by the observed periods. It measures frequency, not size of gain, and is sensitive to sample length and constituent choice.
Survivor selection
The bias introduced when current companies are used to study the past without representing all former participants. The resulting history describes the selected group, not the full historical industry.

22 · The investment test

Start with the physical job. Identify the air, gas or power requirement and the product the company supplies. Then identify who chooses the specification and who is responsible when performance is inadequate. A customer relying on a machine gives the supplier an opportunity to earn trust; the contract and competitive position determine whether that trust becomes attractive economics. This is where the Engineering family's qualification, processing-depth and quoted-input tests become practical rather than decorative.

Next, follow one unit of revenue through the business. What proportion is manufactured internally? What is bought out? What engineering, selling and field-support expense is required? Does the company retain the service customer after installation? Can it recover input inflation? How long does it wait for acceptance and payment? A company-level investment model becomes more credible when these operating questions explain the margin and working-capital assumptions. The product's importance cannot substitute for them.

Then locate the earnings claim. The shareholder in Greaves owns the parent claim after minority allocations, but must still evaluate the cash going into subsidiaries. The shareholder in KOEL owns a group containing industrial and financial activities. Cummins' shareholder participates in associate/JV earnings as well as the operating business. ELGI's weighted shares and KPCL's subdivision affect per-share comparisons. These are central to valuation because they determine what the owner actually receives, rather than being footnotes to be checked after the conclusion is written.

The cash test follows. Reconcile operating cash to profit, identify working-capital movements and read investing receipts and expenditure. Ask whether the next rupee of capital is maintaining a proven franchise, expanding a validated product, entering a new market or funding an activity with a different risk profile. The return requirement should reflect that distinction. A group can report net cash and still face a difficult allocation choice if several businesses need funding at the same time.

Finally, compare the evidence with the expectations embedded in the price. The basket's strong 2026 performance means the market has already recognised something favourable. A prospective investment needs a reason why future owner cash flow can exceed what that price requires, together with a clear account of what would falsify the thesis. Examples here include failed price recovery, slow new-plant absorption, weak order conversion, persistent subsidiary losses or cash generation dependent on loan-book shrinkage. The thesis should survive a neutral calendar assumption.

The central judgment:

Reliable air and power can support valuable franchises, especially when design, customer qualification and service remain with the supplier. June 2026 evidence also shows that these advantages do not remove margin pressure or capital-allocation risk. Select the company whose next conversion step is demonstrable, whose accounting bridge is understandable and whose price leaves room for execution uncertainty. The merged industry is a useful research map; the final investment decision still belongs at the level of the business and the shareholder claim.
