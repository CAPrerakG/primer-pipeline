The industry in 60 seconds

1 · The industry in 60 seconds

Explain it to a 10-year-old

A machine tool is a robot carpenter for metal. The factory tells it where to move, how fast to cut and which tool to pick up. Making the first good part is only the beginning. It must make the next part the same size after the motor has warmed up, hold the metal without shaking, and keep working when a tool becomes blunt. A cheap machine that makes bad parts can cost its owner more than an expensive machine that makes good parts all day. The company selling the machine earns its reputation by proving that difference, then keeping the customer’s factory running.

Say it to a client in one minute

Machine tools turn investment budgets into production capability. Their builders sell future factory capacity; cutting-tool makers sell repeat consumption inside existing factories. Those two demand streams react differently when customers postpone capital spending. This watchlist also contains component suppliers, a demerged investment-heavy company and businesses with almost no current production. Read the roster before treating the heading as one industry.

  • Jyoti and Macpower are expanding CNC capability, but their backlog, accounting perimeter and cash conversion need separate examination.
  • Kennametal’s cutting tools and WIDMA machinery are an adjacent exposure with a June financial year-end.
  • Lokesh combines machines, components and defence exposure; its financing cost absorbs much of its operating earnings.
  • HMT’s parent profit and group loss tell opposite stories. Keeping it in the basket prevents a profitable-company-only history.
  • The eight-stock basket gained 45.6% in 2026 while the Nifty fell 10.7%. That is a price result, not proof that all eight companies improved.

The central investment question is whether engineering skill produces accepted machines, repeatable customer output and collected cash. A large order book answers only part of that question. A catalogue with many models does not establish customer economics, and a domestic manufacturing address does not establish ownership of the controller or spindle technology. The best evidence connects the machine’s tested duty to the customer’s purchase decision and then to the builder’s working capital.

Apply the family’s existing tests by cross-reference: specification and replacement value in 078 — Castings, retained processing depth in 080 — Forgings, and the quoted-input exposure in 081 — Fabrication. Here they lead to a further practical question: who remains responsible when the installed machine stops making acceptable parts? Service responsibility can support repeat business, but it can also create warranty costs. The contract and demonstrated support capacity decide which outcome is more plausible.

Why the product name is insufficient

A turning centre for repetitive, uncomplicated components and a multi-axis system for complex aerospace parts share the CNC label. Their customers can nevertheless require different mechanical stiffness, controller functions, proof of machining performance and service support. Do not assign a premium valuation merely because the brochure says five-axis. Establish whether the axes are used simultaneously, which actual components have passed acceptance, and how much of the delivered package is proprietary. Product breadth has value when it lets a supplier solve more customer problems with a support network it can afford.

The chain in one breath

The customer’s drawing becomes an acceptance specification. The machine builder turns that specification into a design, buys or makes the assemblies, integrates motion and cutting, and proves the result on actual work. Delivery transfers the machine to the customer’s factory, but completion may still require installation and acceptance. After that, the customer wants output rather than demonstrations. The supplier has to keep spares, technicians and useful documentation available. Every stage creates a different financial exposure: design cost, bought-out inventory, unfinished machines, customer balances or warranty obligations.

For a child, imagine selling a remote-controlled pencil that must draw the same tiny circle every time. You can buy its motor and computer, but someone must make the frame steady, fit the gears properly, and show that the circle stays round after an afternoon’s use. If the buyer refuses to pay until the pencil works at school, having finished it at your workshop does not mean you have collected your money. If it later shakes, the buyer calls the name printed on the box. The listed machine company is responsible for the whole promise even when important components came from elsewhere.

Fund-manager lens

The value sits at the customer problem that is difficult to solve repeatedly. Before crediting an integration story, trace one delivered machine through process specification, bought-outs, acceptance and payment. A builder that funds inventory for an ambitious expansion can be technologically promising while still producing weak current cash conversion. That combination appears in the filings here and should remain visible in the investment case.

Common definition: the operating EBITDA proxy is profit before tax plus finance cost plus depreciation/amortisation minus other income. It is a transparent calculation from the filed income statement, not a company-adjusted EBITDA claim. Exceptional items and associate effects can require additional separation; HMT’s tiny associate loss is immaterial at the displayed precision. PAT is total group profit where consolidated, with owner attribution separately disclosed. The source links are in the roster above. No company’s half-year total is labelled a quarter.

On this basis Jyoti’s June consolidated operating EBITDA is ₹108.83 crore, about 21.4% of operations; Macpower’s is ₹15.43 crore, about 16.2%; and Lokesh’s is ₹9.60 crore, about 17.3%. Lokesh then pays ₹4.65 crore of finance cost and ₹3.86 crore of depreciation before reaching ₹1.01 crore PAT. The operating margin alone would obscure the size of the shareholder claim left after those charges. Jyoti’s higher margin also does not settle its cash funding requirement. These are different mechanisms that should be examined rather than collapsed into one ranking.

Kennametal’s June operating proxy is ₹129.20 crore on ₹477.60 crore operations, approximately 27.1%. Its quarterly finance charge is small; the more material issue is the inventory and receivable absorption shown in the year-end cash-flow statement. The high current profit does not remove the need to fund carbide products and other stock. It also does not make Kennametal comparable to a pure CNC OEM: the mixture of cutting-tool consumption, machinery, product mix and customer industries changes what an incremental rupee of sales means.

Emkay makes the other-income test unavoidable. In the March half-year, operations are ₹0.46 crore while other income is ₹8.36 crore. Reported PAT of ₹5.78 crore cannot be interpreted as a margin on toolmaking sales. Its filed result describes trading and wind generation, and the annual report explains the transferred undertaking. HMT is a separate example: the parent earns positive profit with other income far above its operations, while the consolidated group loses ₹29.21 crore in June. Choosing the parent headline would change the apparent industry health.

Rapicut’s acceleration is substantial: operations rise from ₹10.38 crore to ₹82.04 crore and PAT from a loss to ₹8.17 crore. Other income is only ₹0.02 crore, so the recent profit is not explained by investment income. However, the statement includes a large inventory movement, and the annual history starts from a weak base. The filing says there is one segment, tungsten-carbide products. The honest read

The current evidence supports demand and execution at several companies, alongside substantial funding demands and weak operators. Margin improvements should earn greater confidence when they survive a full year, exclude other income, reconcile to the reported perimeter and produce cash after inventory and receivables. The strongest margin in a single table is insufficient evidence of the strongest shareholder outcome.

The shareholder profit and column checks

Three questions precede every profit comparison: what is the reporting period, what is the consolidation perimeter, and how much other income sits above profit? Only then does the EPS check help. EPS is profit attributable to equity shareholders divided by weighted-average shares. Paid-up capital divided by face value establishes closing shares; it establishes the weighted denominator only when shares did not change during the period. Share splits, bonuses, warrants and fresh issues need their own timing treatment.

The following table checks the latest reported profit against the printed basic EPS. It deliberately shows closing shares where the independently available quarterly denominator is limited and labels the cases that remain unresolved. A mathematically implied denominator is a way to locate a problem, not independent verification of the same profit. Minority losses can make owners' profit higher than total group profit. Ignoring that distinction can produce a superficially matching EPS check on the wrong shareholder numerator.

The discrepancies are evidence, not hidden corrections

Batliboi’s June result prints total group PAT of ₹0.4910 crore, owners' PAT of ₹1.0410 crore and minority loss of ₹0.5500 crore. Its printed EPS of ₹0.10 approximately follows group PAT. A screen’s ₹0.22 follows owner-basis arithmetic with closing shares. Both figures therefore have identifiable numerators, but they are not interchangeable. This primer uses the filed profit attribution and discloses the EPS inconsistency; it does not certify that the screen has formally corrected the issuer’s statement. The same issue is visible in the March annual attribution.

Sunita’s consolidated statement prints March-half total PAT of ₹3.2611 crore and owners' PAT of ₹3.4040 crore. Its annual EPS note uses ₹6.1891 crore group profit with 61,78,071 weighted shares; the result’s owners' profit is ₹6.3320 crore. The annual EPS calculation is arithmetically reproducible on the group numerator, but that does not establish an owner-basis EPS. This matters whenever a valuation is presented as a claim on the listed shareholders' earnings.

Sharp’s printed half-year EPS cannot be made to fit the published capital history simply by using closing shares. Its annual note does disclose a weighted denominator that approximately reconciles annual PAT. In addition, its printed September and March half-year PAT rows, ₹5.6404 crore and ₹2.6390 crore, sum to ₹8.2794 crore rather than annual ₹9.1901 crore. The difference is ₹0.9107 crore; the annual other-income line also exceeds the sum of printed half-year other income by approximately that amount. Without an explicit recast bridge, treating this as a clean quarter-on-quarter comparison would overstate what has been established.

Sunita’s two printed half-year PAT rows total ₹6.1397 crore against annual ₹6.1891 crore, a ₹0.0494 crore difference associated with the difference between summed half-year and annual tax charges. These are source-row discrepancies, not amounts repaired in this page. Annual figures and period figures retain their labels; no growth rate or earnings multiple is built from the unreconciled pair. Hittco’s annual EPS notes also carry unit/denominator inconsistencies, while its latest-quarter loss check is consistent within rounding.

Lokesh issued 13 lakh shares during June’s quarter and converted another five lakh warrants in July. The post-June shares cannot be inserted into June’s basic EPS check. Its filed ₹0.49 differs from a current screen’s ₹0.47, which resembles a closing-share calculation. That is a weighted-denominator issue to take to management, not permission to call ₹0.49 verified using a quotient reverse-engineered from PAT.

April is useful context, with identifiable failures

April’s average relative return is +7.1 points, positive in 13 of 15 observations. It failed in 2019 at −2.5 points and 2025 at −3.1 points. The result is a tendency in this selected price history. It does not prove that customers systematically place enough orders in April to generate the observed share-price change. April overlaps with the broader small-cap pattern already documented in this series. Treat the fiscal-budget explanation as a possible mechanism requiring independent evidence, not a causal finding from monthly prices.

The wider April–July interval fails in 2013, 2016, 2017, 2018 and 2019. April’s usual strength therefore cannot be stretched into an unconditional summer trade. In 2017, for example, April beats the Nifty while May’s relative return is −16.7 points. The return path matters: a single positive month can be overwhelmed by later declines. The 2014 April–July spread of +48.7 points and 2024’s +37.0 points also show why average-window performance can be more dependent on large winners than a hit-rate summary suggests.

February–March is the weaker interval, with a current exception

February has beaten the Nifty in only 2 of 15 years, with an average −4.8-point relative result. The February–March window is positive in only 2 of 15 years and has a median −5.6-point spread. Its positive years are 2024 and 2026. The current year’s +16.8-point window is exactly the sort of exception that prevents the historical tendency becoming a mechanical sell rule. January–March, by contrast, is positive in 7 of 15 years; February’s weakness does not justify attaching the same hit rate to the entire first quarter.

October–December is positive in 11 of 14 complete years, with a median +8.7-point spread. August–September is positive in only 3 of 14 complete years, median −4.0 points. Use the calendar to decide which management explanations deserve follow-up, not to override the operating evidence. Technology: prove the part, not just the brochure

CNC control tells a machine how to move; the complete system must still cut accurately under real conditions. Positioning accuracy is closeness to the instructed position. Repeatability is consistency when returning to a position. Neither alone proves the quality of a finished complex part under cutting load. Mechanical stiffness, heat, workholding, tool condition, programming and motion synchronisation all contribute. A buyer’s process trial is consequently more informative than an isolated catalogue specification.

ISO 230-2:2014, positioning accuracy and repeatability provides the framework for axis positioning and repeatability tests. ISO 230-12:2022, accuracy of finished test pieces addresses finished test pieces, including effects of geometric errors and coordinated-axis behaviour. They establish different tests; success on one should not be passed off as success on all. The exact customer’s acceptance contract still determines which part features, operating conditions and measurements matter.

The useful current split is between growing capacity that converts into cash and growing capacity that still requires financing. Jyoti, Macpower, Kennametal and Lokesh each have an explicit cash-flow test in the evidence. Treating them as one capex rally misses those differences; treating the weakest companies as absent would miss the downside already present in the roster.

Questions for the management call

How much of the book is customer-advanced and how old is it?

Request opening book, new awards, cancellations, accepted deliveries and closing book by age. Separate submitted bids. An advance reduces funding exposure only if it is collectible, usable and tied to a firm executable order.

What triggers revenue and the final payment?

Obtain the dispatch, installation and acceptance milestones in representative contracts. Ask which test parts and measures govern acceptance, and what happens if the customer delays site readiness.

Which part of the technology is actually owned?

Separate bought-out controllers, licensed software, internal applications and manufactured critical assemblies. Ask for accepted customer processes and evidence of repeat business rather than a model count alone.

How does expansion change cash needs?

Bridge inventory, receivables, advances, payables, capex and borrowing to the ramp plan. Ask which stock is common across models and which would be stranded by cancellation.

Why is reported EBITDA different from the common operating proxy?

Request a bridge from filed PBT through finance cost, depreciation, other income, exceptional items and management adjustments. The comparison should use the same consolidated or standalone perimeter.

How much service value is captured after sale?

Ask for paid service and spare revenue, repeat customers, warranty obligations and technician coverage. Installed-machine counts alone do not establish an annuity.

What explains the profit/EPS discrepancies?

Batliboi and Sunita need an owner-versus-group numerator bridge; Sharp needs half-year/annual and EPS reconciliation. Lokesh needs the weighted-share calculation, with June issues separated from July conversions.

What fraction of the book is legally deliverable for export?

Request product, end-use, counterparty and licence status within the relevant reporting perimeter. Investigation or access-improvement disclosures should be connected to actual shipment and collection.

Is the carbide growth price, volume or mix?

For Rapicut and the tool names, reconcile sales to product mix, material cost, inventory and cash. Do not infer the operating mechanism solely from a large percentage change.

What would show that the new premium platform is accepted?

For Macpower and other OEMs, ask about repeat orders, completed process trials, output, commissioning and support. A richer pending book is promising but requires a delivery bridge.

What funding remains after current equity issues?

At Lokesh and the changing-capital SMEs, separate issued shares, unconverted warrants and weighted shares. Growth in company profit can leave a smaller gain per shareholder if the denominator rises.

What proves recovery at a weak operator?

Require operating sales, usable production assets, cash collections and a credible financial perimeter. A new promoter, quoted share-price rise or insolvency-plan headline does not by itself establish normal machinery earnings.

Glossary: find a term

Machines and the cutting process

Machine tool
Equipment that shapes material through a controlled machining or forming process; here predominantly metal-cutting machinery.
CNC
Computer numerical control: programmed commands coordinate machine movement and the machining sequence.
Turning
Removing material while the workpiece rotates against a cutting tool; commonly used for rotational parts.
Milling
Removing material with a rotating cutter while the workpiece is located and supported.
VMC
Vertical machining centre; a machining platform with a vertical spindle arrangement.
HMC
Horizontal machining centre; spindle arrangement and workholding permit particular multi-face production configurations.
Five-axis
A machine with five controlled axes; positioning and simultaneous contouring capability must be distinguished.
Contouring
Coordinated movement to follow a defined path or surface; accuracy depends on the complete operating system.
Spindle
The rotating assembly that drives a tool or workpiece; speed, stiffness and reliability affect cutting performance.
Feedrate
Speed at which the tool and workpiece advance relative to each other during a machining operation.
Tool change
Switching the active cutting tool; reliability and time influence the production cycle.
Workholding
Chucks, fixtures and other arrangements that locate and grip the workpiece for machining.
Runout
Deviation in rotation or alignment that can affect where a tool or workpiece actually cuts.
Centreless grinding
Grinding with a regulating-wheel/support arrangement, rather than holding the part conventionally between centres.
Special-purpose machine
Equipment engineered around a defined operation or component program rather than a broad standard application.
Pallet
A workholding carrier that permits prepared workpieces to be moved or exchanged within a machining system.

Capability, inputs and support

Positioning accuracy
Closeness of actual axis position to commanded position under defined test conditions.
Repeatability
Consistency in returning to a position; repeatable error can still be inaccurate.
Tolerance
Permitted variation in a specified part feature; the actual acceptance requirement matters.
Thermal drift
Geometry or position changes as a machine and its environment heat or cool.
Compensation
Control correction based on measured error behaviour; effectiveness depends on calibration and operating conditions.
Rigidity
Resistance to deformation under load; inadequate stiffness can impair the machined result.
Chatter
Unwanted vibration during cutting that can damage finish, accuracy or the cutting edge.
Carbide
Hard tool material used in cutting applications; composition and duty affect performance and cost.
Tool life
Useful service of an edge under a specified duty; compare accepted output, not an unsupported universal duration.
PLC
Programmable logic controller; coordinates machine functions and interlocks alongside the numerical-control system.
Digital twin
A model of machine/control behaviour used to test programs or processes before physical operation.
Adaptive feed
Feed adjustment in response to operating conditions; benefits must be tested on the actual application.
Commissioning
Preparing an installed machine to operate at the customer’s site, including setup, checks and training.
Acceptance trial
Agreed test demonstrating the delivered machine meets specified customer requirements.
Installed base
Machines already placed with customers; an opportunity for repeat business rather than automatic service profit.
Backward integration
Moving a previously purchased process or component into internal production, with associated capital and quality obligations.

Orders, earnings and the shareholder claim

Order book
Customer commitments remaining for execution under the company’s definition and stated measurement date.
Bid pipeline
Business being pursued; submissions and tenders are distinct from awarded orders.
Order intake
New accepted orders in a period; reconcile with opening book, execution and cancellations.
Book bridge
Reconciliation of opening backlog to new orders, deliveries, cancellations and closing backlog.
Customer advance
Payment before delivery; terms determine whether it reduces usable financing needs or remains refundable.
Working capital
Funds tied to inventory and customer balances, partly offset by suppliers and customer advances.
Inventory absorption
Cash committed to stock growth; a negative inventory expense movement does not establish a cash inflow.
Receivable
Amount owed by a customer; booked revenue can exist before cash is received.
Operating cash flow
Cash generated or used by operations after working-capital and tax effects under the reported statement.
Operating EBITDA proxy
This page’s PBT plus finance cost and depreciation minus other income; separate material exceptional effects.
Other income
Income outside the operations line; assess its nature before interpreting reported profit.
PAT
Profit after tax; consolidated total group PAT can differ from profit attributable to listed equity owners.
Minority interest
The share of a subsidiary’s result belonging to shareholders outside the consolidating parent.
Basic EPS
Profit attributable to equity holders divided by weighted-average equity shares for the period.
Weighted shares
Share denominator accounting for time outstanding; closing capital alone may not reproduce it.
Warrant
Instrument potentially convertible into equity; conversion timing and dilution affect the shareholder denominator.
Balancing period
Final quarter or half-year obtained from annual results less previously published periods; adjustments can concentrate here.
Consolidation perimeter
Entities included in group accounts; acquisitions, disposals and subsidiaries can change comparability.

Policy, industry data and the statistical basket

Apparent consumption
Production plus imports minus exports within a defined dataset; not measured machine utilisation.
Import substitution
Replacing imported purchases with domestic supply through demonstrated customer acceptance and economics.
SCOMET
India’s controlled-export list; specific technical characteristics and end-use conditions determine coverage.
Dual-use
Equipment or technology with civilian and sensitive uses; applicable export controls require product-specific examination.
OTR
Omnibus Technical Regulation; the cited 2024 machinery safety notification was rescinded in January 2026.
CIRP
Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process; legal resolution exposure should not be equated with normal operating equity.
Equal weighting
Assigning equal weight to available company returns at each rebalance, regardless of market capitalisation.
Return clipping
Limiting individual monthly returns to ±50% in this pipeline before calculating the monthly basket.
Relative return
Basket return minus benchmark return in percentage points over the same interval.
Hit rate
Number of positive relative observations divided by total eligible observations; always retain the denominator.
Partial year
A calendar-year result that stops at the fixed project date rather than December.
Stale endpoint
Last valid quote before the fixed data date; it is not filled forward to imply a later trade.
Corporate-action discontinuity
A price step caused or complicated by changes in capital/business ownership; may not represent an investor’s total return.
Survivorship bias
Historical study of names still present today; missing delisted or failed names can distort inferred industry outcomes.
Window drawdown
Distance from the highest observed close in the stated prior window; distinct from maximum historical drawdown.
Liquidity sensitivity
Testing whether thin or structurally different members drive a result; it does not prove tradeable execution.

Sources and method

Primary result filings are the authority for financial periods, other income, profit and capital. Investor decks and calls provide order definitions and management forecasts, identified as such. Raw Screener tables are locators and annual-reconciliation diagnostics, not substitutes for unread primary columns. Local audit files preserve all sixteen dates, the price repairs, EPS arithmetic, annual locator comparisons and sensitivity calculations.

Financial evidence is restricted to the fixed-date information set used here; price statistics stop at 18 September 2026. ₹lakh values are divided by 100 to obtain ₹crore; ₹million values by ten. Quarterly or half-year EPS is not annualised. Company profit differences left unresolved by the available statements remain explicitly disclosed. No invented profit, recast quarter or synthetic closing price is introduced to force a reconciliation.
