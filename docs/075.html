Industry primer · 75 of 278 · Metals family, part 12

Specialty, Non-ferrous & Precious Metals

This primer covers four small watchlist sections at once — specialty alloys, non-ferrous metals, precious metals and diversified metals — because they share one organising idea that none of them states. Everything in the previous eleven primers was a commodity: a tonne of steel, aluminium or copper is interchangeable with any other tonne and sells at a world price. Nothing here is. A superalloy for a fighter-engine turbine is certified to a specific engine; a gold deposit has to be found before it can be mined; and the residual Vedanta is whatever was left after a conglomerate broke itself into pieces. These are metals businesses where the asset is knowledge, a licence or an orebody — not tonnage.

1 · The industry in 60 seconds

Metals where the tonnage is beside the point

Explain it to a 10-year-old

Most metal is sold by weight, like potatoes — a kilo is a kilo. But some metal is more like medicine. The inside of a jet engine gets so hot that ordinary metal would melt, so a few factories in the world make special mixtures that do not. You cannot buy that at a market price; you buy it from whoever is allowed to make it. And gold is different again: before anyone can sell it, somebody has to walk over rocks for twenty years and find it.

Say it to a client in one minute

Three unrelated businesses filed under one heading.

  • Superalloys: Mishra Dhatu Nigam, the state-owned specialty alloy plant. Q1 FY27 revenue up 40% to ₹239.5 cr and profit up 27% to ₹16.3 cr, but EBITDA margin down to 15.3% on raw materials and a near-doubling of LPG cost. Order book ₹2,300 cr, two-thirds defence.
  • Gold exploration: Deccan Gold Mines, India's only listed gold explorer, now valued at ₹4,435 cr with commercial production still ahead of it.
  • The residual conglomerate: Vedanta Ltd, the entity left after four businesses were demerged out of it in 2026, still holding copper, zinc international, ferro chrome, nickel and its stake in Hindustan Zinc.
  • The calendar: April has worked in 12 of 15 years, the strongest month in the project; February–March in 4 of 15.
  • The warning: seven of the eleven names in these sections are micro-caps or recent listings, several 84–96% below old highs.

What a superalloy actually is

Inside a jet engine, turbine blades sit in gas hotter than the melting point of the metal they are made from, spinning at enormous speed. They survive because of nickel-based superalloys: mixtures engineered so that microscopic particles inside the metal hold its structure together at temperature, combined with internal cooling channels and ceramic coatings. Titanium alloys do a different job — half the density of steel at similar strength — which is why they dominate airframes and rocket structures.

The commercial point is that you cannot buy these at a price. Each alloy is qualified for a specific application by the customer over years of testing, and once qualified, the supplier is effectively locked in for the life of the programme. Mishra Dhatu Nigam's announcement this quarter that it has isothermally forged a nickel superalloy for a fighter engine, rolled a 7000-series aluminium alloy, and obtained S400 certification from GE in the United States for testing services is exactly the kind of milestone that matters here — each one is a door that opens and does not easily close again.

These two chains fail in opposite ways. A specialty alloy business fails slowly and visibly — margins erode on input costs, as Midhani's did this quarter when LPG nearly doubled, and you can see it in the numbers quarter by quarter. An exploration business fails suddenly and invisibly: nothing happens for years, and then either a lease is granted or it is not. Position sizing should reflect that difference. The first is an industrial you can model; the second is an option with an unknown expiry.

The audit

Vedanta is excluded, and the reason is a data trap worth knowing. Four businesses — aluminium, iron and steel, oil and gas, and power — were demerged out of Vedanta Ltd during 2026, with a record date of 1 May and listing on 15 June. Logically the parent's share price should show a sharp step down when it went ex-demerger. It does not. The series runs ₹271.6 on 30 April to ₹294.6 on 4 May and on up to ₹352.6 by late May, with no discontinuity anywhere. Either the series has been back-adjusted for the demerger or the adjustment is handled some other way — but in either case, a long-run Vedanta chart is not a like-for-like record of today's company, and we will not build a fifteen-year basket on it. Anyone making a level claim about Vedanta's history should verify the adjustment first. This is the same caution we applied to NMDC in 064 and to HEG in 071.

Four of these eleven names are not metals businesses on any sensible reading. Krishna Defence belongs with defence engineering; JTL Defence's price history plainly belongs to a predecessor business; Panth Infinity and Naksh Precious Metals are trading entities whose charts have lost 94–96% from old highs. The basket is therefore Deccan Gold Mines, Mishra Dhatu Nigam and Hisar Metal — and before 2018 it is essentially Deccan Gold alone, which we flag rather than hide.

Why Midhani's quarter is more interesting than it looks

Turnover rose 40.5% but value of production rose only 7.9%. That gap is the whole story: the company shipped a great deal of inventory it had already made, which flatters revenue growth without telling you much about underlying activity. Meanwhile EBITDA rose 12.9% against a 40.5% revenue rise, so margin fell to 15.3% — management attributes about 13 percentage points of pressure to adverse raw-material price variance, plus an LPG cost that nearly doubled on Middle East supply disruption.

For a business selling qualified products under long contracts, input inflation is a genuine problem, because prices cannot be reset quickly without reopening a qualification. The useful number to hold onto is the order book: ₹2,300 crore at 1 July 2026, with 66% defence and 21% space and aero. That is roughly two and a half years of revenue at the current run rate, and it is the reason to look through a bad margin quarter — provided the margin does normalise by the third quarter as management expects.

How to think about an explorer

Deccan Gold has been listed for over two decades and has never produced gold commercially. That is not a criticism — it is what exploration is. The correct way to value it is as a portfolio of options: each project is worth the probability of a mining lease, times the probability of financing, times the value of the deposit at a long-run gold price, discounted for the years until first production. At a ₹4,435 crore market value with the Ganajur lease still awaited, the market is assigning a high probability to a sequence of events that has not happened yet, in a year when gold has more than quadrupled from its 2015 low. Own it if you want that option and can size it as one; do not own it as a way to be long gold, for which there are far cleaner instruments.

5b · India's gold paradox

The world's biggest buyer, and almost no mines

India buys more gold than almost any country on earth and digs up almost none of it. That gap — hundreds of tonnes imported each year against a domestic output measured in a couple of tonnes — is one of the more striking facts in Indian resources, and it is the entire reason a single pre-production explorer can be worth ₹4,435 crore.

The explanation is not geology. India sits on Archaean greenstone belts of exactly the kind that host major goldfields in Australia, Canada and West Africa, and the Kolar fields produced gold for a century before closing. The explanation is process: until the exploration rules were opened up, private companies could not economically search, and even now the step from a proven deposit to an operating mine runs through a state mining lease that can take years. Deccan Gold has been listed since 2003 and is still waiting on Ganajur.

  • What an investor is actually buying is a position in that bureaucratic queue, not an ounce of gold. The correct comparison set is junior explorers on the Toronto or Sydney venture boards, which trade on resource ounces, drill results and permitting milestones — not Indian metals companies.
  • The gold price matters less than it seems. A deposit's value scales with the gold price, but the probability of ever mining it does not, and in 2025 that distinction cost holders dearly: gold rose 64% and this basket fell.
  • The optionality is real, though. If leases are granted and financing follows, a domestic producer would sell into the world's deepest physical gold market with no freight or import duty disadvantage — which is a genuinely good place to end up. The question is only ever when.

The same logic, incidentally, explains why every announcement from a company like this moves the price sharply: with no revenue to anchor a valuation, news about leases, drilling and financing is the fundamental.

7 · The business calendar

Two calendars in one basket

  • January–March: defence and government customers push deliveries to close the fiscal year, but the market has treated this window badly — 4 wins in 15 years.
  • April: new budgets, fresh order announcements and annual results; 12 wins in 15 years, averaging +11.4 points.
  • April–July: order intake season for defence suppliers; 10 wins in 15.
  • August–September: mixed; exploration news flows irregularly and has no season.
  • October–December: the weakest of the second-half windows at 7 of 14, with a median of exactly zero.

Basket: 3 names (Hisar Metal Industries Limited, Mishra Dhatu Nigam Ltd., Deccan Gold Mines Limited), equal-weighted, monthly from Jul 2011, compared with the Nifty 50.

  • Strongest months vs the Nifty: Apr +11.4 pts (beat in 12 of 15 years); Jul +5.3 pts (beat in 6 of 16 years); Jun +4.3 pts (beat in 8 of 15 years).
  • Weakest months: Mar -6.4 pts (beat in 2 of 15 years); Feb -3.1 pts (beat in 4 of 15 years); May -0.5 pts (beat in 5 of 15 years).
  • Windows: Feb–Mar beat the Nifty in 4 of 15 years (median -8.6 pts); Apr–Jul beat the Nifty in 10 of 15 years (median +11.6 pts); Aug–Sep beat the Nifty in 8 of 14 years (median +1.4 pts); Oct–Dec beat the Nifty in 7 of 14 years (median 0.0 pts); Sep–Nov beat the Nifty in 7 of 14 years (median +0.6 pts); Jan–Mar beat the Nifty in 4 of 15 years (median -9.6 pts).

Control: small caps as a whole (Nifty Smallcap 100) beat the Nifty in April in 11 of 15 years, by an average of 3.0 points, and lag in January–February. Part of any April strength is this market-wide effect. After removing it, this industry's own April edge is about +8.4 points.

Reading it: three companies with usable histories — Deccan Gold Mines (spliced to its BSE record back to 2006), Mishra Dhatu Nigam (from 2018) and Hisar Metal (from 2017). Before 2018 the basket is effectively one gold explorer, so read the early years accordingly. April has beaten the Nifty in 12 of 15 years, averaging +11.4 points — the strongest single month anywhere in this project. April–July has worked in 10 of 15 with a median of +11.6. Against that, February–March has worked 4 times in 15 (median −8.6), January–March 4 of 15 (median −9.6), and March alone twice in fifteen.

A tendency, not a rule: small samples, survivorship bias (only names still listed) and equal weighting. Use it for timing context, not as a signal.

The exceptions, and why

  • April's only losses were 2019 (−3.7), 2012 (−3.4) and 2017 (−1.1) — all small. Its best were 2026 (+30.7), 2015 (+28.1) and 2018 (+23.8).
  • April–July's mean of +21.8 is twice its median of +11.6, because 2015 (+95.9) and 2016 (+74.4) dominate. Use the median.
  • February–March has been negative in eleven of fifteen years, and its two best years barely cleared zero (+1.7 and +0.6).
How to use this — carefully

April's 12-of-15 record is the strongest monthly number in this project, but the basket is three small companies and, before 2018, one of them. The mechanism is plausible for the defence side — new budgets and order announcements land in April — and non-existent for the gold explorer. What this table is really telling you is that small Indian industrial and speculative stocks are sold into the fiscal year end and bought into the new one. That pattern shows up across the whole Metals family; here it is simply at its most extreme because the companies are at their smallest.

9 · What moves margins

Eight drivers

  1. Defence and space order flow. Two-thirds of Midhani's book.
  2. Nickel, cobalt and titanium prices. The input costs that cannot be passed on quickly.
  3. Energy and gas costs. LPG nearly doubling cost a full margin point cluster this quarter.
  4. Qualification milestones. Each new approved alloy is permanent revenue.
  5. Execution and delivery. The gap between value of production and turnover.
  6. The gold price. For the explorer, it sets the value of anything found.
  7. Mining lease decisions. The binary event in exploration.
  8. Import substitution policy. The reason a domestic superalloy plant exists at all.

The anatomy of the cycles

  1. The gold bear market (2012–2015): gold fell from $1,676 to $1,060 and exploration became almost unfundable; the one exception was a speculative drilling re-rating in 2014.
  2. The defence listings (2017–2019): Midhani listed in 2018 and Hisar began trading on the NSE, turning a one-stock basket into three; the first full year was poor.
  3. The order-book years (2020–2023): defence indigenisation lifted Midhani, and the small-cap boom lifted everything; 2023 returned 126%.
  4. Divergence (2024–2026): two poor years on execution and margins, then a 45% year driven almost entirely by gold.

Patterns that repeat

  • These are not gold proxies. In 2025 gold rose from $2,641 to $4,341 and the basket fell 9.4%.
  • Margin, not revenue, decides Midhani's year — 2024 fell 19% on a rising order book.
  • The exploration name moves on news, not on price, which is why its best year (2023, +224%) came with gold almost flat.

Worth noting how unusual India's position is. For specialty alloys, a country that builds fighter aircraft and launch vehicles essentially cannot buy the critical inputs on an open market, because the suppliers are concentrated in a few allied jurisdictions and subject to export control — which is the entire strategic rationale for a state-owned plant. For gold, India is the world's largest consumer and among its smallest producers, which is a structural oddity that has persisted for decades and explains why a single listed explorer can command ₹4,435 crore.

12 · Where technology is going

Six shifts

  1. Additive manufacturing. Printing turbine parts from superalloy powder, which changes what a mill sells — powder rather than billet.
  2. Single-crystal blades. Casting a blade as one crystal to resist creep; a capability gap India is still closing.
  3. Titanium for space. Launch-vehicle demand growing faster than aerospace generally.
  4. Ceramic matrix composites. A long-run substitute for nickel superalloys in the hottest sections.
  5. Exploration technology. Airborne geophysics and machine learning applied to old survey data; the cheapest way to find deposits under cover.
  6. Domestic testing accreditation. Midhani's S400 certification from GE turns a cost centre into a service business.

What is priced in

For Midhani, a recovery that management has explicitly guided to: margins normalising by the third quarter as raw-material and gas costs subside. The stock is 21% below its July 2024 high and up 22% this year, which looks like the market extending partial credit — buying the order book while discounting the margin. If the ₹2,300 crore book converts at a normalised margin, the current price is undemanding; if input costs stay where they are, it is not. The single number to watch is value of production, not turnover, because that is what strips out inventory movement.

For Deccan Gold, a great deal. ₹4,435 crore for a company that has not produced gold commercially in twenty-three years of listing, in the same year that gold reached about $4,425 an ounce, is the market paying for a sequence of approvals rather than for cash flows. That can be entirely rational as an option — but it should be sized as an option, and the two things that would change its value are the Ganajur mining lease and a financing decision, neither of which follows a schedule.

The honest read

These four watchlist sections should not be traded as a group; they are a filing convenience, not an industry. Midhani is a defence industrial with a genuine moat in qualification and a real, current cost problem — judge it against the defence names in 057–062, not against metals. Deccan Gold is a single-asset exploration option in a country that consumes enormous quantities of gold and produces almost none. And the remaining nine names are either misclassified or uninvestable. The useful output of this primer is knowing which of the three to look at, and on what terms.

17 · Questions for management

Concall checklist

  1. What is the order book, its sectoral split, and its expected execution period?
  2. What was value of production, and why does it differ from turnover?
  3. What drove the margin change, and what is the path back to normal?
  4. How are raw-material and energy costs passed through in long contracts?
  5. Which new alloys or grades were qualified this year, and for whom?
  6. What is capacity utilisation, and what is being added?
  7. What revenue does the testing and certification business generate?
  8. For an explorer: what is the resource estimate, to what standard, and at what gold price?
  9. What is the status of each lease application, and the expected timeline?
  10. How will development capital be raised, and what dilution should shareholders expect?

19 · Glossary

Every term you'll meet

Specialty alloys

Superalloy
A nickel- or cobalt-based alloy that keeps its strength near its melting point.
Titanium alloy
Half the density of steel at similar strength; the airframe and rocket metal.
Titanium sponge
The porous raw form of titanium metal before melting.
Maraging steel
An ultra-high-strength steel used in rocket motor cases and centrifuges.
Vacuum induction melting
Melting under vacuum to exclude oxygen and impurities.
Vacuum arc remelting
A second remelt to refine structure for aerospace grades.
Isothermal forging
Forging at a constant high temperature to shape difficult alloys.
Near-net shape
Forming a part close to its final dimensions to save costly machining.
Single-crystal casting
Growing a turbine blade as one crystal to resist creep.
Creep
Slow deformation of metal under stress at high temperature.
7000-series aluminium
High-strength zinc-bearing aluminium alloys used in aerospace.
Qualification
The multi-year customer approval that makes a supplier hard to replace.
Value of production
Output made in the period, whether or not it was sold — the real activity measure.
Order book
Contracted future revenue; ₹2,300 cr here at 1 July 2026.
Price variance
The profit effect of input prices differing from those assumed in a contract.

Exploration and precious metals

Exploration
Searching for an economic mineral deposit — mostly unsuccessful, by design.
Greenstone belt
An ancient volcanic-sedimentary belt that often hosts gold.
Dharwar Craton
The ancient crustal block in Karnataka that hosts India's gold belts.
Open-pittable
A deposit shallow enough to mine from the surface.
JORC code

20 · Test yourself

Ten questions; answers hidden

1. What unites these four watchlist sections?

None of them is a commodity: value comes from qualification, a licence or an orebody rather than tonnage.

2. What is a superalloy?

A nickel- or cobalt-based alloy engineered to keep its strength at temperatures near its own melting point — the material of turbine blades.

3. Why is qualification the moat?

Each alloy is approved for a specific engine or airframe over years of testing, after which the supplier is effectively locked in for the programme's life.

4. Why look at value of production rather than turnover?

Midhani's turnover rose 40.5% but value of production only 7.9% — much of the revenue was inventory shipped rather than new activity.

5. Why did Midhani's margin fall?

Adverse raw-material price variance of about 13% plus LPG costs that nearly doubled on Middle East disruption.

6. What is Deccan Gold Mines?

India's only listed gold exploration company, working Karnataka's Dharwar Craton since 2003, still awaiting the Ganajur mining lease.

7. How should an explorer be valued?

As an option: the probability of a lease, times the probability of financing, times the deposit's value, discounted for the years to first production.

8. Are these stocks gold proxies?

No. In 2025 gold rose from $2,641 to $4,341 and the basket fell 9.4%.

9. Why is Vedanta excluded from the basket?

Four businesses were demerged in 2026, yet the price series shows no clean step at the record date, so the long-run chart is not like-for-like.

10. What does April's 12-of-15 record really measure?

Small-cap behaviour around the Indian fiscal year end — selling into March, buying into April — rather than anything specific to these industries.
