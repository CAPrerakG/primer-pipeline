Graphite Electrodes & Carbon Materials

To melt a pile of scrap steel you need to pass an enormous electric current through it. The thing that carries that current is a graphite electrode — a machined column of pure carbon, up to 800 mm across and three metres long, that burns itself away as it works. Its cousins are the carbon products that let an aluminium smelter run at all. These are consumables like refractories (070), but with one crucial difference: their raw materials are by-products of the oil and steel industries, which makes their prices violently cyclical. In 2017 this basket returned 596% in a single calendar year. By 2019 it had given almost all of it back. This primer explains why.

1 · The industry in 60 seconds

Carbon consumables with commodity prices

Explain it to a 10-year-old

Think of a giant pencil, as thick as a tree trunk. You plug it into a socket so powerful that lightning jumps from its tip and melts a pile of old cars into liquid metal. The pencil wears down as it works, so the factory buys another. The trick is that this pencil is made from a special leftover of oil refining that only a few factories in the world can make — so when everyone wants pencils at once, the price goes crazy.

Say it to a client in one minute

A cyclical consumable business with two live stories.

  • The cycle: electrode prices went vertical in 2017–18 when China shut capacity and needle coke ran short; the basket rose 596% and then fell 27% and 46%.
  • Now: no price spike — just volume. Graphite India is at 97% utilisation with revenue up 27% and profit up 28%, and Rain Industries' EBITDA rose 61%.
  • The new direction: Himadri is spending ₹1,300 cr on battery materials — anodes, lithium iron phosphate cathodes, carbon nanotubes — because the same molecules feed cells.
  • The risk: the US made a preliminary affirmative countervailing-duty finding against Indian electrodes in July 2026, and Brazil put a preliminary 57.3% dumping margin on them.
  • A data warning: HEG's electrode business was demerged on 1 September 2026 and has not yet relisted; the listed stub was renamed and its price history is gone.

The unifying idea is that all of these begin as a by-product — of refining oil, of making coke for blast furnaces, of distilling tar — and are purified into something a metal producer cannot operate without. That is a good position in an upcycle and a dangerous one in a downcycle, because by-product supply does not respond to demand.

The chain in one breath

  • Refineries and coke ovens throw off heavy carbon residues.
  • Specialist plants turn some of it into needle coke — the bottleneck.
  • Electrode makers bake and graphitise it over several months.
  • Steel mills burn the electrodes away melting scrap.
  • Smelters burn carbon anodes away making aluminium.
Fund-manager lens

The four-to-six month production cycle is the reason this industry behaves the way it does. When demand rises, no producer can respond for half a year, so price does all the adjusting — violently. When demand falls, half-finished inventory keeps arriving, so price collapses just as violently. Any model that treats electrodes as a steady consumable like refractories will be wrong at both turns. The critical variable is not demand; it is how long supply takes to answer.

The audit: a price history that vanished this month

Anyone running a screen on this sector in September 2026 will get the wrong answer, so it is worth setting out precisely what happened.

  • 1 September 2026: HEG Limited's scheme of arrangement became effective. The graphite electrode business was demerged into HEG Graphite Limited, and Bhilwara Energy was merged into the remaining company.
  • 2 September 2026: the Registrar of Companies issued a fresh certificate of incorporation renaming HEG Limited as HEG Advanced Materials Limited — a green-energy and advanced-carbon platform holding hydro and wind, energy storage through RePlus, and the advanced carbon business through TACC.
  • 7 September 2026: record date. Shareholders receive one ₹2 share of HEG Graphite for every ₹2 share held.
  • Today: the ticker NSE:HEG no longer resolves. The successor, NSE:HEGAM, has a price series that begins on 22 September 2026 at about ₹233. HEG Graphite — the company that actually owns the electrode plants — has not yet listed.

Consequence: the ₹4,176 cr market value shown against "HEG" in any classification file is the post-demerger stub, not the electrode business, and there is no continuous electrode price history to test. We therefore run the basket on Graphite India, Himadri and Rain Industries, and treat HEG's history as unavailable rather than quietly splicing two different companies together. As of 18 September 2026 there is exactly one listed pure-play Indian graphite electrode maker, and a second is waiting in the wings.

5 · How money is made

A spread business with a six-month lag

Strip away the detail and an electrode maker earns the gap between the electrode price and the needle-coke price, multiplied by tonnes shipped. Both halves of that gap are set in world markets, neither is under the producer's control, and the production cycle means today's shipment was made from coke bought months ago.

PhaseWhat happens to the spreadWhat it does to profits
Demand acceleratesElectrode prices rise immediately; coke is already boughtExplosive margin expansion — 2017
PeakCoke prices catch up; new capacity is announcedMargins top out — 2018
Demand slowsElectrode prices fall while expensive coke is still in inventoryMargins collapse and inventory is written down — 2019
TroughBoth prices low; utilisation fallsLosses, but cash survives if there is no debt — 2020
RecoveryUtilisation rises before price doesOperating leverage; profits rise on flat prices — 2026

The current quarter is a textbook fifth row. Graphite India's revenue rose 27% and its profit 28%, but electrode prices contributed only marginally; the gain came from running the plant at 97% instead of 82%. In a business with this much fixed cost, the utilisation number is the profit number.

Why the balance sheet is the strategy

Graphite India holds ₹3,939 cr of net cash. That is not idleness — it is the correct response to a business that can lose money for two years at a time. The companies that died in the 2019 bust were the leveraged ones; the ones that survived bought assets cheaply and are now enjoying the upswing. Rain Industries, which carries meaningful debt from its global acquisitions, fell 64% in 2018 and is still 55% below its 2018 high despite a 61% rise in EBITDA this quarter. Same industry, different balance sheets, permanently different outcomes.

5b · Anatomy of the 2017 supercycle

How a consumable returned 596% in a year

The 2017–18 graphite electrode episode is the single most instructive event in this primer, because every ingredient of a commodity squeeze arrived at once and in the right order. Worth walking through slowly.

  1. Supply was cut. China's environmental campaign closed a large amount of small, polluting electrode and needle-coke capacity, and the industry had already been shrinking after years of losses — Western producers had shut plants outright.
  2. Demand jumped. China simultaneously banned induction-furnace steel made from scrap, pushing that volume into electric-arc furnaces — which use electrodes and induction furnaces do not. Global scrap-based steel making grew sharply at the same moment.
  3. The input was already tight. Needle coke is made by very few plants, and a growing share was being diverted to lithium-ion battery anodes. The electrode makers found themselves bidding against the battery industry for the same molecule.
  4. Supply could not answer. Graphitisation takes months and a new plant takes years. Prices had to clear the market, and they rose by a multiple, not a percentage.
  5. The market re-rated on peak earnings. Graphite India rose 872% in 2017 and Rain Industries 575% — not because anyone believed the prices were permanent, but because the earnings arrived faster than the scepticism.
  6. Then it reversed. Contracts reset lower, Chinese capacity restarted, coke costs stayed high into the downturn, and inventory written at boom prices had to be sold at bust prices. The basket fell 27% in 2018 and 46% in 2019.
The lesson, stated plainly

A consumable with a long production cycle and a by-product input is not a compounder; it is an option on a shortage. The right question is never "what are earnings?" but "what phase are we in, and what does the balance sheet look like if I am wrong about the phase?" In 2017 the answer was euphoria on a trailing multiple of two; in 2019 it was despair on an infinite one. Today, with utilisation recovering and prices flat, we are in the least glamorous and historically most rewarding phase — provided nothing breaks the export route, which is exactly what the trade cases threaten.

7 · The business calendar

Annual contracts, then the melting season

  • January–March: annual electrode contracts are negotiated and Chinese New Year slows Asian demand; the weakest window, 6 wins in 15 years.
  • April–July: the new contract year begins and mills run hard; the strongest stretch, 10 of 15 years and a median of +9.6 points.
  • July–September: aluminium smelters and EAFs run through the monsoon, which does not affect them; September has beaten the Nifty in 10 of 15 years.
  • October–December: mixed, 6 of 14, as the year's contracts are already priced in.
  • Through the cycle: orders are booked months ahead, so a quarter's results describe decisions made two quarters ago.

8 · What the stocks do

Seasonality test on TradingView prices

The exceptions, and why

  • April–July's two great years were 2017 (+55.0) and 2026 (+51.3) — the supercycle and this year's utilisation recovery.
  • Its failures were 2018 (−20.9), 2019 (−19.6) and 2013 (−14.9) — every one a downcycle, not a seasonal effect.
  • January–March's worst years were 2019 (−32.2), 2015 (−29.3), 2020 (−21.6) and 2025 (−20.5), which tells you the contract-negotiation window is where bad news lands.
How to use this

Be honest about what this table can and cannot do. With a basket this volatile — one year at +596% and another at −46% — the seasonal averages are dominated by the cycle, not the calendar. The usable signal is the direction: the first calendar quarter is when annual contracts reset and disappointments surface, and April onward is when the year's volumes are actually shipped. Use it to time a decision you have already made on the cycle; do not use it to make one.

9 · What moves margins

Eight drivers

  1. Electric-arc furnace steel output. The direct demand for electrodes.
  2. Needle coke price and availability. The input, and the squeeze mechanism.
  3. Aluminium smelter output. Drives calcined coke and pitch.
  4. Chinese capacity policy. The swing factor in both supply and demand.
  5. Utilisation. In a fixed-cost plant, the profit driver at flat prices.
  6. Trade remedies. Duties in export markets, and India's own duties on imports.
  7. Power tariffs. Graphitisation is among the most electricity-intensive processes in manufacturing.
  8. Battery-material demand. A growing competing claim on the same carbon feedstocks.

The anatomy of the cycles

  1. The long winter (2012–2016): global overcapacity, weak steel, and a basket that lost to the index in four of five years.
  2. The squeeze (2017–2018): Chinese closures, an induction-furnace ban and a needle-coke shortage collided; one year returned more than the previous decade.
  3. The hangover (2019–2020): contracts reset, expensive inventory was sold cheap, and the basket lost 46% and then 4% in a rising market.
  4. Divergence (2021–2026): Himadri re-rated on batteries while the electrode names waited; this year, utilisation finally delivered.

Patterns that repeat

  • The stocks move on the second derivative of steel, not its level.
  • Balance sheets decide who is still here at the next peak.
  • Every upswing arrives with a new story attached — in 2017 it was Chinese closures; today it is batteries.

The trade cases, which matter more than the quarter

In February 2026, Resonac Graphite America and Tokai Carbon GE petitioned for antidumping and countervailing duties on large-diameter graphite electrodes from China and India. On 27 July 2026 the US Department of Commerce announced preliminary affirmative countervailing-duty determinations. Separately, Brazil's DECOM opened its own investigation in March 2026, with preliminary dumping margins of 54.9% for China and 57.3% for India. For companies that export most of what they make, this is a larger swing factor than any quarterly number on this page, and it deserves to be the first question on the next results call.

12 · Where technology is going

Six shifts

  1. Decarbonising steel favours electrodes. Every tonne of steel that moves from blast furnace to electric arc adds electrode demand; see 066.
  2. ...and starves pitch. The same shift shrinks coke ovens, the source of coal tar — a real long-term problem for pitch producers.
  3. Battery anodes compete for needle coke. Synthetic graphite anodes use the same premium feedstock.
  4. Lower specific consumption. Better electrodes and better furnace practice keep cutting kilograms per tonne of steel.
  5. Carbon nanotubes and conductive additives. Himadri's ₹70 cr project targets a small, high-value cell input.
  6. Inert anodes in aluminium. Still experimental, but a genuine long-run threat to carbon anode demand; see 073.

What is priced in

Very little of a cycle, and quite a lot of a story. Graphite India is valued at roughly ₹11,800 cr excluding its net cash, for a business earning ₹171 cr a quarter at 97% utilisation — that is neither cheap nor demanding, and it implicitly assumes electrode prices stay flat. Rain Industries, at 55% below its 2018 high with EBITDA up 61%, is priced for its leverage rather than its franchise. Himadri, by contrast, carries a market value near ₹33,000 cr against roughly ₹5,700 cr of annual revenue, on the strength of a battery-materials plan whose commercial cathode plant does not start until the third quarter of this financial year.

The honest read

Two different investments are sitting in one watchlist section. The electrode and carbon names are cyclical, cash-rich and unfashionable, with an identifiable upside trigger — any supply disruption, given six-month lead times — and an identifiable downside trigger in the trade cases. Himadri is a growth story about lithium-ion materials that happens to be classified under carbon, and it should be judged against battery-material peers, not against Graphite India. Do not let the watchlist heading persuade you that they are the same trade. On the ₹30,000 cr battery-revenue ambition: treat it as a statement of intent with capital behind it (₹1,125 cr committed to LFP), not as a forecast — the number is roughly five times the company's current total revenue.

17 · Questions for management

Concall checklist

  1. What proportion of volume goes to the US and Brazil, and what is the plan if duties are confirmed?
  2. What is current utilisation, and what is the realistic ceiling?
  3. How is needle coke contracted — spot, annual, or integrated supply?
  4. What is the inventory holding period, and at what average cost?
  5. How much of the order book is priced annually versus quarterly?
  6. What is the specific consumption trend at your major EAF customers?
  7. What return is expected on the battery-materials capex, and by when?
  8. What is net cash or net debt, and what is the capital allocation policy for it?
  9. How exposed is the pitch business to the long-term decline of coke ovens?
  10. For the demerged entities: what is the listing timetable, and how are debt and cash being split?

20 · Test yourself

Ten questions; answers hidden

1. What does a graphite electrode do?

It carries an enormous electric current into a furnace to strike an arc that melts scrap steel, burning itself away in the process.

2. Why did prices explode in 2017?

China shut electrode and needle-coke capacity and banned induction-furnace steel at the same time, pushing demand into electrode-using furnaces while supply could not respond for months.

3. Why can't supply respond quickly?

Baking, impregnation and graphitisation take months, and a new plant takes years, so price does all the adjusting.

4. What is needle coke, and who competes for it?

A premium coke with an aligned structure, made by few plants; battery makers now bid for the same material to make synthetic graphite anodes.

5. What drove Graphite India's Q1 FY27 profit?

Utilisation rising from 82% to 97%, not electrode prices, which gained only marginally.

6. What is the trade risk in 2026?

A US preliminary affirmative countervailing-duty determination in July and a Brazilian investigation with a 57.3% preliminary dumping margin on India.

7. What happened to HEG?

Its electrode business was demerged into HEG Graphite on 1 September 2026; the remaining company was renamed HEG Advanced Materials, and the electrode entity has not yet listed.

8. Why does decarbonising steel cut both ways?

More electric-arc steel means more electrodes, but fewer coke ovens mean less coal tar and so less pitch.

9. Why does the balance sheet matter so much here?

The business can lose money for two years at a time; Graphite India's ₹3,939 cr of net cash is what lets it wait, while leveraged peers were forced to sell.

10. Are Himadri and Graphite India the same trade?

No. Himadri is becoming a lithium-ion materials company and should be judged against battery peers, not against an electrode cycle.
