1 · The industry in 60 seconds

Chemistry from two simple alcohols

Explain it to a 10-year-old

There are two simple alcohols. Ethanol comes from sugarcane or grain; methanol mostly comes from natural gas or coal, and India buys almost all of it from abroad. Chemical makers turn ethanol into nail-polish-remover-type solvents (ethyl acetate) and vinegar acid (acetic acid). They turn methanol into formaldehyde, which becomes the glue that holds plywood together and the resin in paint. The trouble: cars now run on ethanol mixed into petrol, so chemical makers must compete with oil companies for it. And the methanol has to cross the sea, so a war in the Gulf can make it suddenly expensive. In 2026 both things happened, and the companies that could pass the higher cost on made a lot of money.

Say it to a client in one minute

Alco-chemicals are conversion businesses priced off two volatile feedstocks. Ethanol costs are set largely by India's fuel-blending programme: E20 made oil companies the biggest buyer at administered prices (1,059 crore litres contracted for the 2025–26 supply year). Methanol is ~98% imported, so Gulf supply and freight decide it; the 2026 war pushed Indian methanol up ~22% between January and March, and Chinese methanol futures reached ¥2,999/t in September. Products (ethyl acetate, acetic acid, formaldehyde, pentaerythritol) are commodities competing with Chinese and Middle Eastern imports, so margins are spreads. Q1 FY27 was a spread windfall: ethyl acetate hit a 12-year-high spread (Laxmi's profit tripled), GNFC's acetic acid rose to ₹63,000/t, and Kanoria swung to a standalone profit. Kanoria Chemicals is the only listed name left in this bucket after the audit, and it is reinventing itself by selling its loss-making car-electronics arm (APAG). The bigger acetyls names (Laxmi Organic, Jubilant Ingrevia, GNFC) sit in other watchlists.

2 · What the products are

Two trees of products

Ethanol tree (alco-chemicals)

  • Acetaldehyde → acetic acid, pyridines, pentaerythritol (with formaldehyde).
  • Acetic acid → PTA (polyester), vinyl acetate, acetic anhydride (pharma, cellulose acetate), esters. Also made from methanol and CO (the dominant global route).
  • Ethyl acetate (ethanol + acetic acid) → solvent for inks, coatings, adhesives, flexible packaging and pharma.
  • Butyl acetate and other esters → paints and coatings.
  • Diketene derivatives (Laxmi) → pharma and agro intermediates.
  • Bio-MEG and bio-ethylene from ethanol (India Glycols) → "green" polyester.

Methanol tree

  • Formaldehyde (37% formalin) → urea-, phenol- and melamine-formaldehyde resins for plywood, laminates, MDF and adhesives.
  • Pentaerythritol (formaldehyde + acetaldehyde) → alkyd resins in paints, synthetic lubricants, explosives (PETN).
  • Hexamine (formaldehyde + ammonia) → resins, rubber curing, fuel tablets, explosives.
  • Paraformaldehyde → agrochem (glyphosate) and resins.
  • Methanol also goes to acetic acid (GNFC), MTBE, solvents, pharma and formic acid directly.
Everyday examples

The plywood of your wardrobe is held together with formaldehyde resin; the quick-drying printing ink on a chips packet uses ethyl acetate; the oil paint on a steel gate has alkyd resin made with pentaerythritol; the "solid fuel" tablets campers light are hexamine; the paracetamol tablet in your cupboard was made using acetic anhydride.

Each stage for a 10-year-old

  • Feedstock: ethanol is like fruit juice fermented from sugarcane or maize; methanol is made in giant gas plants far away.
  • Alcohol supply: imagine a shop with limited juice where the government says car fuel gets first pick; chemical makers get what's left, at a higher price.
  • Conversion: heating the alcohol with a catalyst (a helper chemical) to change it into a new chemical, like baking dough into bread.
  • Derivatives: combining two of these chemicals to make something more special, like pentaerythritol, which paint makers pay more for.

Fund-manager lens

These are feedstock-spread businesses without a feedstock advantage: most Indian producers buy ethanol and methanol at market prices. Edge comes from integration (making both acetaldehyde and formaldehyde for pentaerythritol), location next to customers (formaldehyde is shipped as a liquid, 37% strength, so it travels badly), and specialty derivatives with approvals. In shortage years (2021, 2026) spreads explode because Chinese supply dries up; in normal years they're thin.

Fund-manager lens: how to think about Kanoria now

For a decade Kanoria's consolidated numbers mixed two unrelated businesses: a profitable-in-good-years formaldehyde and alco-chemicals franchise, and a loss-making European car-electronics group that made up about half of income. Once APAG is deconsolidated, the company should be judged like any other Indian formaldehyde and pentaerythritol maker: capacity utilisation of the new 345 TPD formaldehyde and hexamine plants, the spread over methanol, and the share of specialty derivatives (pentaerythritol, resins). The residual 14.3% APAG stake (to be transferred in FY2029–30) is a small option, not a driver. The risks are the usual ones for spread businesses: the 2026 windfall came with expensive methanol and scarce Chinese supply, and both can reverse. The stock at ₹190 is still below its 2021 peak, so the market is pricing a better company, not a new super-cycle.

5 · How money is made

Spreads over alcohol

About 0.5 t of methanol makes 1 t of 37% formalin, so formaldehyde margins are a methanol spread plus freight savings (it's made near customers). Ethyl acetate needs ethanol and acetic acid; when OMCs pay more for fuel ethanol, the industrial price follows. Pentaerythritol uses both formaldehyde and acetaldehyde and sells into paints and lubricants, the highest-value outlet in this family.

Where the formaldehyde goes

Most Indian formaldehyde ends up as urea- or phenol-formaldehyde resin inside plywood, block boards, particle boards, MDF and decorative laminates. India's plywood industry is clustered: Yamunanagar in Haryana (the largest hub), Perumbavoor in Kerala, the Gandhidham–Kandla belt in Gujarat (built around imported timber), and clusters around Kolkata and in Punjab and Uttar Pradesh. Large panel makers run their own resin kitchens and buy formaldehyde or methanol; thousands of small plywood units buy ready resin. So formaldehyde demand tracks home-building, office interiors and furniture, and it dips in the monsoon. Because formalin is mostly water, a plant earns best selling within a few hundred kilometres, which is why formaldehyde makers put plants next to plywood clusters and why national market share matters less than local share.

Analogy

A formaldehyde plant is like a neighbourhood ice factory: ice (formalin, mostly water) is too heavy to ship far, so each town has its own. When the cost of the electricity (methanol) jumps, every factory raises its price together, and the one with the newest, most efficient machines earns the most.

6 · The business calendar

Sugar season decides ethanol, construction decides formaldehyde

  • Sugar crushing (November–April) produces molasses and cane ethanol; grain distilleries run year-round. The ethanol supply year for fuel blending runs November–October, with OMC tenders and allocations set before it starts.
  • Monsoon (June–September) slows construction, and with it plywood, laminates and formaldehyde demand; post-monsoon and festive interiors lift it.
  • Paint season: pre-Diwali repainting (September–November) lifts pentaerythritol and solvent demand.
  • Chinese New Year affects import prices of acetic acid and ethyl acetate.
  • Methanol: Iranian gas curtailments in winter (December–February) often cut methanol exports and raise prices.

8 · What moves demand and margins

Policy on one side, the Gulf on the other

  • Ethanol blending policy: India reached E20 in 2025; OMCs buy at administered prices, so industrial ethanol competes for the same supply. Rules on grain and cane diversion change the pool every season. In the first half of the 2025–26 supply year, grain distilleries supplied ~333 of 515 crore litres, and maize alone ~182 crore litres, the largest single feedstock. That shift ties ethanol costs to maize prices and the monsoon as much as to sugar.
  • Methanol imports: ~98% imported. After the war began, India cut its Middle East share from ~88% to ~51% (March–May 2026) and bought more from China. Indian spot methanol rose from ~₹28.7/kg (January) to ~₹35.1/kg (March 2026).
  • Chinese export prices of acetic acid and ethyl acetate; Chinese plant closures created the 2026 ethyl acetate spread.
  • Gas prices: for GNFC's methanol and acetic acid, gas costs decide whether plants run at all.
  • Construction and interiors demand for formaldehyde resins (plywood market ~₹248 bn in 2025, growing ~5% a year).
  • Paint and coatings demand for pentaerythritol and solvents.
  • Company-specific: for Kanoria, the APAG exit removes the biggest source of losses and volatility.

The anatomy of an alco-chemicals cycle

  1. Feedstock shock or Chinese supply cut (2021 energy curbs; 2026 Hormuz) → product prices jump faster than costs.
  2. Spread windfall → record quarters (Laxmi, Jubilant, GNFC and Kanoria in Q1 FY27).
  3. New capacity and Chinese restarts → spreads compress (2022–24).
  4. Feedstock squeeze (high methanol, E20 ethanol) with weak product prices → losses and shutdowns (GNFC shut its acetic acid plant in Q1 FY27 on cost).

Patterns that repeat

  • Cheap methanol helps formaldehyde makers, but only if product prices hold. 2015 and 2020 (falling methanol) were good; 2018 and 2022 (high methanol) were bad.
  • Diversifications away from the core destroyed value. Kanoria's 2014 APAG purchase dragged the stock for a decade; its exit sparked the 2026 re-rating.
  • The biggest years come in shortages (2021: +225%; 2026: +138%), and they fade when supply returns.

Country leadership shift: Europe and the US invented these chemistries; China now sets the price for most of them. India's route to relevance is bio-based ethanol chemistry, since it produces ethanol from cane and grain at scale.

Methanol trade after the war: the Gulf's cheap gas made it India's natural supplier (~88% of imports before the conflict). In March–May 2026 that fell to ~51% as buyers switched to Chinese and South-East Asian cargoes, and Iran's share dropped below 3%. Chinese coal-based methanol costs more than Gulf gas-based methanol, so a lasting shift raises India's structural feedstock cost for formaldehyde, acetic acid and amines.

11 · Where technology is going

Five shifts

  • Bio-based chemicals: ethanol → bio-ethylene, bio-MEG and bio-acetic acid for brand owners seeking lower carbon; Laxmi's ethyl acetate earned a USDA biobased label.
  • Green methanol (from biomass or hydrogen plus CO₂) for shipping fuel, which could compete for methanol supply.
  • Low-emission resins: E0/E1 formaldehyde-emission standards for plywood and furniture, and formaldehyde-free binders.
  • Membrane and process efficiency in pentaerythritol and formaldehyde (silver vs metal-oxide catalysts).
  • Grain ethanol: maize and rice distilleries make more ethanol available beyond sugar seasons.

What would break the story

  • Hormuz reopens and Gulf methanol returns: methanol falls, and formaldehyde prices fall with it; the spread may hold, but revenue and inventory gains shrink.

16 · Questions for management

Concall checklist

  1. Where do you source methanol and ethanol, on what contract terms, and how many days of stock do you hold?
  2. What is the spread per tonne over methanol and ethanol vs the five-year average?
  3. What share of volume is specialty derivatives (pentaerythritol, hexamine) vs bulk formaldehyde?
  4. What is the demand split by end market (plywood, paints, pharma)?
  5. What is utilisation of the new capacity?
  6. (Kanoria) What are the remaining obligations to APAG, and when does it leave the consolidated accounts?

Red flags

  • Margins that depend on cheap feedstock inventory.
  • Capacity added in bulk products facing Chinese exports.
  • Heavy dependence on one resin customer.
  • Diversification into unrelated businesses (APAG's lesson).

19 · Test yourself

Eleven questions; answers hidden

1. Why did E20 hurt alco-chemical makers?

Oil companies became the dominant ethanol buyer at administered prices, tightening supply and raising costs for industrial users.

2. Why did the Iran war matter for formaldehyde?

India imports ~98% of its methanol, much of it from the Gulf; supply disruption raised methanol prices sharply.

3. Why is formaldehyde made near its customers?

It's shipped as a 37% water solution, so freight per useful tonne is high.

4. Which end market links this industry to housing?

Plywood, laminates and MDF, through formaldehyde resins.

5. What was Vijay Solvex, and why did it move?

An edible-oil company: "Solvex" refers to solvent extraction of oilseeds, not solvent chemicals.

6. What is APAG, and why does its exit matter for Kanoria?

A Swiss-German car-electronics group Kanoria owned from 2014; it made ~half of consolidated income but lost money. Selling it leaves Kanoria as a pure chemicals company.

7. What drove Laxmi Organic's profit to triple in Q1 FY27?

Ethyl acetate spreads at a 12-year high ($215/t) plus new capacity at Lote.

8. Why did GNFC shut its acetic acid plant in Q1 FY27?

Gas and input costs made production uneconomic; it restarted in August.

9. When have Kanoria's best years come?

In shortage years with falling or manageable methanol costs and rising product prices: 2015, 2021 and 2026.

10. Why do formaldehyde makers put plants next to plywood clusters such as Yamunanagar?

Formalin is mostly water, so freight eats the margin beyond a few hundred kilometres; local share matters more than national share.

11. Which feedstock supplied the most fuel ethanol in the first half of the 2025–26 supply year?

Maize (~182 of 515 crore litres); grain overall supplied ~333 crore litres.
