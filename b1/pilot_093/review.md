## What the industry actually contains

A factory control system is a loop. A sensor measures temperature, pressure, position or current. A controller compares that measurement with the desired state. An actuator changes something: a motor moves, a valve opens, a heater draws power. Software coordinates the sequence and records what happened. The systems integrator makes these devices work together and proves the completed installation meets the customer's requirements. Maintenance, calibration, replacement sensors and software changes can then create further sales.

The commercial chain is less uniform than that description. A measurement-instrument maker owns a product specification and calibration capability. A robotics integrator can buy the robot, design fixtures, program the line and accept the customer's production-performance risk. An electrical contractor can sell a complete installed package while buying most of its expensive components. A distributor makes money from assortment, sourcing and availability. An IT services company can call software delivery automation without participating in this factory control loop. Mixing these companies into one industry margin or valuation would hide the question the primer should explain: **what capability does the customer repeatedly pay this particular listed company to retain?**

The roster proves this problem rather than merely suggesting it. Honeywell Automation is an anchor for process/building integration, but its Indian listed perimeter is narrower than Honeywell's global businesses. Affordable and Patil are production-line/robotics integrators. Tempsens manufactures temperature sensing, heating and cables. Aplab supplies professional electronic equipment and instrumentation. Rishabh combines electrical measurement products with aluminium die casting. Marine combines marine electrical/automation with industrial power-distribution systems. These are relevant businesses, but they have different replacement cycles and cash demands.

The names on the edge are analytically useful precisely because they should not silently enter the same basket. Macfos's Robu business is electronics distribution with an emerging proprietary-products initiative. Canarys has real water-resource automation, but its FY26 consolidated presentation identifies 93% IT solutions and only 7% water-resource management after acquisition-led expansion. Power & Instrumentation is primarily electrical EPC. Rexnord is instrument cooling fans and motors, not the unrelated global Regal Rexnord business. Continental Controls now explicitly reports **Advisory Services** as its only segment. Universal Office Automation has nil revenue and working-capital constraints. Axis is relevant today, but its current automation business arrived through a merger into the legacy Asya Infosoft listed entity.

## Carry the Engineering tests forward

**See 078, Castings: who writes the specification, and does the part wear out?** In automation, replacement demand is not identical to wear. A thermocouple may fail or drift; calibration may be required; a controller can become obsolete while still functioning; production software can need changes when the customer's product changes. Qualification and the cost of stopping a process can protect a supplier. But an installed base is only a potential service opportunity until the listed entity owns the relationship and invoices the service. A robot integrator that purchases the robot does not automatically capture the robot maker's spare-parts margin. The full primer should distinguish field-device replacement, calibration, engineering changes, software support and one-off commissioning.

**See 080, Forgings: retained processing and complexity matter more than physical output.** Here the analogous measure is the engineering, testing and intellectual property retained inside the company. Counting robots installed or panels shipped says little about value added. A larger invoice can contain more purchased hardware and less retained margin. Compare the cost of traded goods, technical manpower, own manufactured content and third-party licences before attributing margin differences to superior technology. Macfos's June filing, for example, has stock-in-trade purchases overwhelmingly larger than materials consumed; that is evidence of distribution economics. Patil's custom line design and commissioning should be assessed through acceptance milestones, rework and cash retention, not just equipment throughput.

**See 081, Metal Fabrication: who carries input cost between quotation and delivery?** For this industry, extend the input list beyond steel. Imported controls, semiconductors, exchange rates, software licences, engineering wages and purchased electrical equipment may be committed before the customer accepts the project. A fixed-price automation order can lose money even during a strong factory investment cycle. The risk is amplified where a commissioning delay holds up billing or customers retain a final payment until performance guarantees are met. The relevant bridge is orders to engineering/procurement, then installation, accepted revenue, receivable collection and operating cash. Do not describe an order book as cash already earned.

The additional question for093 is whether the supplier controls the installed technology and subsequent upgrade path, or only carries the installation risk. This can explain why a well-known global brand, a local integrator and a low-cost distributor have different returns even while all sell into industrial investment.

## What the financial audit found

Five prospective basket companies now have primary FY26 quarterly bridges: **Honeywell, Aplab, Affordable, Marine and Rishabh**. Original June, September and December2025 filings were examined, with March2026 quarter/full-year figures from the subsequent primary June2026 filing. Revenue, other income and reported group/standalone profit add to the annual figures within displayed rounding. Marine and Rishabh owner-profit bridges also reconcile. Affordable's quarterly statement does not disclose a separate owner/minority allocation. These are arithmetic/source checks, not an independent audit of the underlying accounting, and the March quarter is explicitly a balancing figure.

Patil's reported H1 and H2 FY26 revenue, other income, group profit and owner profit add correctly to the annual statement. There is no prior-year consolidated comparator: subsidiary acquisitions began in September2025. A standalone comparison must be labelled as a different perimeter. Its annual EPS reconciles to owner profit using the disclosed weighted share count; the half-year denominator remains a separate task.

Several other cases require disclosure rather than a fabricated clean result:

* **Axis:** June EPS0.66 aligns approximately with group profit, whereas owner profit is larger because minorities bear losses. More seriously, annual EPS6.12 multiplied by47,260,280 disclosed weighted shares aligns with group **total comprehensive income**, not owner profit. The annual EPS note uses2893.48lakh, while the June comparative reports group PAT2884.10lakh and owners2941.66lakh. See [Axis annual report p227](https://www.bseindia.com/xml-data/corpfiling/AttachHis/f08c7d5b-c206-41b5-9606-37d2e42cf5c9.pdf). Preserve all three numerators.
* **Canarys:** the two displayed half-year owner-profit rows do not add to the annual owner amount. Annual EPS does reconcile after deducting the preference dividend from owner earnings; this cannot validate the inconsistent half-year attribution rows. Acquisition scope and preference shares both matter.
* **Continental:** the annual EPS note appears to confuse the paid-up capital figure with the number of shares, giving a tenfold discrepancy relative to the result statement's annual EPS. The June-quarter profit is advisory income, not a revival of industrial controls manufacturing.
* **Power & Instrumentation:** the June locator returned the same May 30 FY26 annual-result PDF as the March locator. The page 14 header is March 31, not June 30. June 2026 primary results remain unresolved, so only 13 of 14 current primary result sets were examined. March financials are retained separately. The annual report EPS note and annual-result EPS also disagree; group versus owner profit adds another mismatch. This is not a verified annual EPS reconciliation.
* **Macfos:** the June filing restates FY26 for Ind AS, with PAT/EPS differing from the annual report. Quarter EPS uses an explicitly disclosed share denominator and reconciles; that does not resolve the accounting-transition annual bridge.
* **Tempsens:** the June-quarter share base precedes the IPO fresh issue. The prospectus restates earlier shares for a split and bonus. The latest filing says the June2025/March2026 comparatives were management-approved but were not audited or reviewed. Both statements must accompany any year-on-year comparison.

Other-income-first review also changes the interpretation. Aplab's December other-income line includes a121.80lakh property-sale profit. Affordable's annual other-income line includes GST/provision reversal and accrued interest on an overseas loan. Honeywell's treasury income is substantial. Rexnord includes an industrial-promotion subsidy inside operating revenue; simply subtracting the other-income line would not isolate product sales. Statutory line placement is not a statement that an item recurs.

Cash adds a further filter. The checked FY26 consolidated statements show negative operating cash at Axis, Marine, Macfos, Power & Instrumentation and Patil, despite their accounting profits. Affordable's parent operating cash differs sharply from group cash. Canarys converts only part of group profit into operating cash. These findings are starting points for receivable, inventory, customer-advance, related-party and acquisition bridges. A positive CFO figure funded by payables or tax refunds is also not automatically recurring customer cash.

Every numeric block in `company_reviews.json` carries the source URL and PDF page. EPS results distinguish an independently disclosed weighted denominator from a closing-share proxy. A denominator implied by dividing PAT by EPS is not independent verification.

The main feasibility lesson is that parallel retrieval can assemble filings quickly, but classification, scanned-table inspection and ownership/EPS reconciliation remain substantial analyst work. Counting downloads or drafted words as completed sections would overstate progress. The pack is useful because it preserves the checks that passed and the reasons the remaining checks have not passed.
