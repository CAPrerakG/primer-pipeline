1 · The industry in 60 seconds

Cooking with molecules

Explain it to a 10-year-old

Imagine a giant kitchen. Oil companies deliver basic ingredients, like flour and sugar (benzene, toluene, methanol, ammonia). Specialty chemical companies are the chefs: they mix, heat and react those ingredients step by step to make something special, like a sauce a medicine maker needs, or the "secret spice" that makes shampoo foam. Some chefs cook big pots of one popular dish cheaply (platform companies). Some cook a secret recipe for one famous restaurant under contract (custom synthesis). Some sell ready-to-use sauces with advice on how to use them (performance chemicals). China has the biggest kitchen in the world, and when it cooks too much, everyone else's prices fall. From 2022 to 2025 China cooked far too much; in 2026 Indian kitchens got busy again.

Say it to a client in one minute

Specialty chemicals are sold for what they do, not just what they are. India's listed universe spans five models: integrated chemistry platforms (Aarti, Deepak, Atul, Vinati, the amine makers), custom synthesis and CDMO for agro and pharma innovators (Anupam, Aether, Tatva, Neogen), performance and formulation chemicals (Fineotex, Rossari, Galaxy, Foseco), oleo and bio-based chemicals (Fine Organic, Godrej Industries' chemicals, Jayant Agro), and MNC subsidiaries (BASF India). The sector boomed in 2020–21 on China+1 and supply shortages, then spent 2022–25 in a bust: Chinese overcapacity, agrochemical destocking, European weakness and, in 2025, US tariffs of 50%. In 2026 volumes and profits are recovering (Q1 FY27: Deepak's profit tripled, Aarti's rose 260%, Alkyl's and Balaji's doubled), tariffs have fallen to about 10%, but energy costs from the Hormuz disruption squeezed some (Chemplast, Camlin, Thirumalai made losses). The stocks are split between winners at new highs and former leaders still 50–70% down.

Analogy

A commodity is rice sold by the kilo; any shop's rice works. A specialty chemical is a restaurant dish: the recipe, consistency and trust matter more than the ingredients. Custom synthesis is a caterer cooking a client's secret recipe under contract, which is sticky while the client's dish sells well.

In practice many "specialty" companies in India sell intermediates that are closer to commodities (a chlorinated benzene is a chlorinated benzene). This is why Chinese oversupply hurt them so badly in 2022–25. The true test is whether margins hold when Chinese prices fall.

Everyday examples

The ibuprofen tablet started with Vinati's IBB; the shampoo's lather comes from Galaxy's surfactants; the crisp packet's food antioxidant may be Camlin's; the car tyre's rubber chemicals may be Yasho's; the polycarbonate in a phone case will one day come from Deepak's new plant; the zeolite catalyst in a diesel truck's exhaust needs Tatva Chintan's structure-directing agents.

Each stage for a 10-year-old

  • Feedstocks: raw ingredients bought from refineries or imported.
  • Unit processes: the cooking techniques (like frying, boiling, baking); some are dangerous, so only licensed, careful kitchens can use them.
  • Intermediates: half-cooked dishes sold to other chefs.
  • Advanced intermediates: a nearly finished dish cooked to a client's exact recipe.
  • Formulations: ready-to-use products sold with instructions, like a spice mix with a recipe card.
Fund-manager lens

Money is made by combining hazardous chemistry India can do safely at scale with approvals that take years. The trap is assuming every step is specialty: stages 02–03 get priced off Chinese exports; stages 04–05 hold pricing if the customer relationship is real. Check each company's mix before paying a specialty multiple.

The capex treadmill

Specialty chemical returns depend on filling new plants. A ₹500 cr plant at 1x asset turn needs ~₹500 cr of sales to earn its keep; if utilisation stays at a third (Clean Science's HALS sold ~3,300 t against ~10,000 t of capacity in FY26), returns collapse even if the chemistry is excellent. Sector capex of roughly ₹18,000 cr in the last cycle is only now being filled; Deepak's ₹11,500 cr polycarbonate project is the next big test.

Analogy

A new chemical plant is like a new restaurant: the kitchen costs the same whether it seats 20 or 200. It only makes money once the tables fill up, and the owner has to survive the empty months first.

The business calendar

  • Agrochemical season: global innovators place orders for intermediates ahead of the northern spring (October–February) and India's kharif (February–April).
  • Pharma and CDMO: order flow follows customers' launch timelines, not seasons.
  • Chinese New Year (January–February): Chinese supply pauses; Indian export prices can firm.
  • Monsoon: slows construction chemicals and some textile chemicals.
  • Year-end results (April–May) and Q1 results (July–August) set the tone for the stocks.

7 · What the stocks do

Seasonality test on TradingView prices

The exceptions, and why

  • The only April miss (2019: −1.7 pts). The small-cap bear market and polarisation to quality; near-misses were 2025 (+0.2, tariff fears) and 2013 (+0.6, rupee crisis). The best Aprils were 2026 (+17.9), 2021 (+17.6) and 2015 (+12.6).
  • April–July failures (2013: −5.2; 2018: −8.4; 2019: −9.3; 2022: −1.2). Each was a broad small-cap or rupee shock. The biggest April–July runs were 2021 (+53.0) and 2014 (+40.0).
  • August–September misses (2015, 2021, 2025). 2021: the sector topped as valuations peaked; 2025: US tariffs of 50% from August.
  • October–December collapses (2022: −15.0; 2025: −13.3). Both came at the start of a leg down: Chinese restocking ending in 2022, tariffs and dumping in 2025.
  • February–March lagged in 12 of 15 years; the only big exception was 2021 (+13.4), the boom year.
How to use this

For these stocks, April–July is the reliable window and the fourth quarter the risky one. But the annual result is set by Chinese pricing and the capex cycle, and dispersion inside the basket is enormous: in 2026 the best stock rose 182% and the worst fell 34%. Use the calendar for timing, company-level results for selection.

8 · What moves demand and margins

Eight drivers

  1. Chinese export prices: China's chemical capacity surge of 2021–24 is the single biggest reason for the bust; Chinese prices set the ceiling for Indian intermediates.
  2. Agrochemical and pharma inventory cycles: global agrochemical destocking in 2023–24 cut orders for intermediates; restocking since 2025 is helping.
  3. Europe's cost disadvantage: high gas prices since 2022 closed European plants, shifting some business to India.
  4. US trade policy: 50% tariffs in August 2025; an 18% deal in February 2026; about 10% after the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs on 20 February 2026 (the replacement Section 122 tariff is limited to 150 days unless Congress extends it, so check the current rate).
  5. Energy costs: the Hormuz disruption in 2026 raised coal, gas and freight costs; Chemplast, Camlin and Thirumalai made Q1 losses.
  6. New plants filling up: utilisation of last cycle's capex decides operating leverage (Clean's HALS, Aether's Site 5, Deepak's phenol expansions).
  7. New demand pools: battery chemicals (Neogen, Tatva, Anupam–Basquevolt), electronic chemicals, emission-control catalysts, polycarbonate import substitution (Deepak), oilfield chemicals (Fineotex).
  8. Currency and inventory: a weaker rupee (~₹96/$ in September 2026) and inventory gains flattered some Q1 FY27 results (Aarti cited both).

The anatomy of a specialty chemicals cycle

  1. China shrinks (environmental closures 2016–18, energy curbs 2021) → Indian producers win orders and pricing; stocks re-rate.
  2. Euphoria → valuations of 50–80× earnings, an IPO wave and a capex wave (2021).
  3. China expands and customers destock → prices fall 30–50%, new plants run half-empty, and stocks fall 50–70% (2022–25).
  4. Recovery → volumes return first, then margins; winners are those with new chemistry or contracts (2026).

Patterns that repeat

  • IPO waves mark peaks. The 2021 listings (Anupam, Laxmi, Clean Science, Tatva, Chemplast) mostly peaked within a year.
  • Profits recover before stocks. Deepak, Alkyl, Aarti and Balaji posted strong Q1 FY27 profits but remain far below their peaks.
  • Micro caps swing wildly in both directions (Sadhana: +830% in 2018, −82% in 2025).
  • Exposure to new demand pools gets the premium: in 2026, battery, oilfield and CDMO names (Neogen, Fineotex, Aether, Yasho) led.

11 · Where technology is going

Six shifts

  • Flow chemistry and continuous processing: safer, cleaner hazardous reactions (nitration, fluorination).
  • Catalytic, low-waste routes (Clean Science's model): cost advantage that China can't copy easily.
  • Battery chemicals: electrolyte salts (LiPF₆, LiFSI), solvents and additives (Neogen, Tatva, Gujarat Fluorochemicals).
  • Electronic chemicals: high-purity solvents, acids and precursors for India's new fabs.
  • Import substitution of big polymers: Deepak's polycarbonate chain will replace imports of BPA and PC.
  • Bio-based and green chemicals: customer demand for lower-carbon inputs (India Glycols, Fine Organic, Godrej).

16 · Questions for management

Concall checklist

  1. What share of revenue competes directly with Chinese exports, and what's the price gap today?
  2. What is utilisation of each new plant, and when do you reach payback?
  3. What share of revenue is under contracts with volume commitments?
  4. How many molecules are in validation, and at what phase for pharma customers?
  5. How exposed are you to US tariffs and to energy costs?
  6. How much of this quarter's margin came from inventory or forex gains?

Red flags

  • Capex announced on the back of one customer's forecast.
  • "Specialty" labels on products with many Chinese suppliers.
  • Rising working capital and receivables from related parties.
  • Plant accidents or pollution-board closure notices.
  • Promoter selling after a rally (Sadhana, March 2026).

17 · Myths and live debates

What you'll hear, and the evidence

ClaimEvidence
"China+1 guarantees Indian growth."2022–25 showed Chinese pricing still dominates; India wins where chemistry is hard or customers want diversification.
"The worst is over."Volumes and profits are recovering, but part of Q1 FY27 was inventory, forex and one-offs, and energy costs are high.
"CDMO is always better than platforms."CDMO revenue is lumpy and depends on the molecule's success; platforms like Deepak and Alkyl earned 21–25% margins in Q1.
"April strength is about chemicals."Partly small-cap behaviour: about 3 points of the April edge is common to all small caps.
"Deepak's polycarbonate bet will re-rate it."Only if it's delivered on time and at cost; ₹11,500 cr is several years of profit, and PC is a global commodity.

19 · Test yourself

Ten questions; answers hidden

1. Why did Indian specialty chemical stocks fall 50–70% after 2021?

Chinese overcapacity and dumping, agrochemical destocking and weak Europe cut prices and volumes just as valuations peaked at 50–80x earnings and new plants came on stream.

2. What separates a custom synthesis company from a platform company?

Custom synthesis makes a client's patented molecule under contract; a platform makes intermediates that many buyers use and competes on cost.

3. Why do asset turns matter so much here?

Returns depend on filling new plants; low utilisation (like HALS at a third of capacity) destroys returns even with good chemistry.

4. Which month has the most reliable edge, and how much is sector-specific?

April (14 of 15 years); about 3 points is the general small-cap April effect, leaving about 5–6 points.

5. Why is Godrej Industries an imperfect specialty play?

Most of its value is its stakes in Godrej Consumer, Godrej Properties, Godrej Agrovet and Godrej Capital, not its chemicals business.

6. What is Deepak Nitrite building, and why does it matter?

A ₹11,500 cr propylene → phenol → BPA → polycarbonate chain (plus a ₹2,500 cr BPA plant), replacing imports and moving Deepak into polymers.

7. Why did some companies lose money in Q1 FY27 while others posted records?

Energy and input costs from the Hormuz crisis hit those without pricing power (Chemplast, Camlin, Thirumalai), while integrated or scarce-product makers (Deepak, Alkyl, Balaji) gained.

8. What drove Fineotex's 175% revenue jump?

The integration of CrudeChem Technologies, a US oilfield-chemicals business, and a larger Texas plant.

9. Which new demand pools did 2026's winners share?

Batteries (Neogen), oilfield chemicals (Fineotex), contract manufacturing (Aether) and exports of rubber and food chemicals (Yasho).

10. Why did US tariffs fall from 50% to about 10% in 2026?

A February 2026 deal cut them to 18%, and the US Supreme Court then struck down the IEEPA tariffs, leaving a temporary ~10% Section 122 tariff.
