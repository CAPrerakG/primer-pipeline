Chlor-Alkali & Soda Ash

Salt and electricity in; caustic soda, chlorine, hydrogen and soda ash out. These are the most basic chemicals of all, used by nearly every other industry. The twin products can't be made separately, so one of them is always a problem.

1 · The industry in 60 seconds

Salt water, electricity, and a twin you can't get rid of

Explain it to a 10-year-old

Pass electricity through salty water and it splits into three things: caustic soda (a strong cleaner that dissolves grease, and which factories need to make aluminium, cloth, paper and soap), chlorine gas (used to make PVC pipes and to clean drinking water) and hydrogen. You always get the same amount of each, like a shop that can only sell shoes in pairs. If people want lots of caustic but little chlorine, you still have to find somewhere to put the chlorine, and sometimes you pay people to take it. Soda ash is a cousin, made from salt and limestone or dug out of the ground, and it goes mostly into glass and detergent. China has built so much soda ash capacity that its price has more than halved since 2022.

Say it to a client in one minute

Chlor-alkali is a power-intensive co-product business. Every tonne of chlorine comes with 1.1 tonnes of caustic soda, so the economics are measured per ECU (electrochemical unit): caustic price × 1.1 plus chlorine price, and chlorine is often negative. Power is the biggest cost, so captive or cheap power and chlorine integration (PVC, chloromethanes, hydrogen peroxide) decide who wins. India is adding caustic capacity at about twice the rate of demand, so it exports more and ECUs are weak. Soda ash is a separate cycle, dominated by China's huge natural and synthetic additions: Chinese futures have fallen from ¥2,656/t (2022 average) to about ¥1,000. Q1 FY27 showed the split: Gujarat Alkalies posted a record quarter on better caustic and exports, Tata Chemicals' Indian soda ash earned a 28% margin behind trade protection while its US business, exposed to world prices, earned almost nothing, and DCW lost money on PVC. India protects soda ash with a minimum import price while an anti-dumping case runs.

2 · What the products are

Four products, dozens of customers

Caustic soda (NaOH)

Sold as lye (48–50% liquid) or flakes and prills. Indian demand split: textiles ~17.5%, alumina ~14.7% (the Bayer process uses caustic to extract alumina from bauxite), inorganic chemicals ~13%, pulp and paper ~5.8%, plus soaps and detergents, water treatment, organics, pharma and dyes. Indicative Indian prices in 2026: lye ₹35–50/kg, flakes ₹50–60/kg; up ~12% in Q2 2026.

Chlorine (Cl₂)

Hard to store or transport (toxic gas), so it must be used on site or sold nearby. Outlets: EDC/VCM → PVC, hydrochloric acid, chloromethanes (refrigerants, pharma solvents), chlorinated paraffin wax, stable bleaching powder and hypochlorite (water treatment), epichlorohydrin, chlorotoluenes and many agrochem and pharma intermediates.

Hydrogen (H₂)

A by-product: burnt for fuel, used in hydrogenation, bottled, or turned into hydrogen peroxide (GACL). It is "free" low-carbon hydrogen, a small green-hydrogen angle.

Soda ash (Na₂CO₃)

Made synthetically via the Solvay process (salt + limestone + ammonia) or its dual-process variant (co-producing ammonium chloride fertiliser, as at Tuticorin), or mined as natural trona (US, Turkey, China's Inner Mongolia). Uses: glass (flat, container and solar) is the largest, then detergents and washing soda, sodium silicate, chemicals, and lithium carbonate for batteries.

Everyday examples

The aluminium in your window frame needed caustic soda to separate it from bauxite; the cotton in your shirt was cleaned with caustic; the chlorine that made your tap water safe and the PVC pipe that carried it came from the same cell; the glass of your phone screen, the window pane and the solar panel on the roof started with soda ash; the "washing soda" in a detergent is soda ash too.

Each stage for a 10-year-old

  • Salt: the same salt you eat, but by the truckload; 1.6 kg of it makes 1 kg of caustic.
  • Power: the cell is like a giant battery charger running backwards; a plant making 500 tonnes a day uses as much electricity as a small city.
  • Electrolysis: a thin plastic membrane lets only sodium through, so caustic collects on one side and chlorine bubbles off the other.
  • Chlorine derivatives: a way to turn the unwanted twin into something people will pay for.
  • Soda ash: a different recipe that heats salt water with limestone and ammonia, recycling the ammonia each time, like reusing a teabag.

Fund-manager lens

Three things make a chlor-alkali producer structurally better than a peer: cheap captive or renewable power, a chlorine sink it controls (PVC, chloromethanes, ECH, hydrogen peroxide), and location next to a large caustic customer such as an alumina refinery (hence the GACL–NALCO Dahej joint venture). For soda ash, the edge is natural trona or protected domestic pricing: Tata's Indian soda ash earned a 28% EBITDA margin in Q1 FY27 while its US trona business, selling into world markets, earned almost nothing.

5 · How money is made

The ECU, power, and the chlorine problem

ECU realisation = (caustic price × 1.1) + chlorine price, per tonne of chlorine produced. When chlorine demand is weak, producers sell it at zero or pay to dispose of it ("negative chlorine"). Then caustic must carry the whole ECU, and producers cut runs if caustic can't. The reverse also happens: strong PVC demand with weak caustic demand leaves caustic in surplus.

Cost / value lineShare or driver
PowerThe largest cost; roughly 2,300–2,600 kWh per tonne of caustic. Captive coal or renewable power decides the cost rank. New electrolysers cut this (Chemfab's 2026 upgrade).
SaltAbout 1.6 t of salt per tonne of caustic; freight matters.
Chlorine realisationFrom positive (strong derivative demand) to negative (disposal cost); CARE notes negative chlorine persisted into 2026.
Soda ash costEnergy (coal or gas for steam), limestone, ammonia recovery; natural (trona) producers have a large cost edge.

Typical through-cycle EBITDA: integrated chlor-alkali players 12–25%; standalone caustic 8–20%; soda ash 15–30% at the top of the cycle, low teens or worse in gluts. These ranges are indicative. In Q1 FY27, Tata's Indian soda ash margin was 28% and GACL's ~19%.

Analogy

A chlor-alkali plant is like a dairy that can only sell milk if it also sells the cream. When nobody wants cream, the dairy has to pay to get rid of it, and the milk price must cover everything. A dairy that owns an ice-cream shop next door (a chlorine sink) never has that problem.

6 · The business calendar

Mostly steady demand, with a few seasonal pulls

  • Caustic: steady industrial demand. Alumina expansions add lumpy step-ups; textile demand follows export orders; prices soften in the monsoon and improve from September.
  • Chlorine: PVC demand dips in the monsoon (construction slows), so chlorine gets harder to place from June to September. Water-treatment demand for bleaching powder peaks in the monsoon.
  • Soda ash: glass demand is steady; detergent demand rises ahead of the festive season.
  • Power: summer grid demand (April–June) raises exchange power prices for producers without captive plants.
  • Pricing: caustic contracts reset monthly or quarterly; soda ash resets quarterly or half-yearly with glass makers.
  • Policy dates: DGTR findings and the soda ash minimum import price extensions arrive through the year and move the soda ash names.

7 · What the stocks do

Seasonality test on TradingView prices

The exceptions, and why

  • April misses (2013: −10.2 pts; 2019: −0.3). 2013 was the rupee crisis building; 2019 was the caustic price collapse. Every other April beat the Nifty.
  • Biggest Aprils (2026: +23.2; 2020: +22.0; 2021: +17.6). Each followed a crash or shock: the Hormuz sell-off in March 2026, the COVID crash in March 2020, and the start of the 2021 energy-crisis boom.
  • April–July failures (2013: −14.5; 2018: −23.8; 2019: −15.6). 2013: rupee and demand slump; 2018: small-cap crash; 2019: caustic prices falling.
  • Biggest April–July runs (2021: +41.9; 2015: +38.3). 2021: scarcity pricing; 2015: falling energy costs.
  • November and the fourth quarter. The worst Novembers were 2022 (−15.4, as soda ash and caustic prices rolled over from their peaks), 2014 (−14.8) and 2018 (−11.9, the small-cap sell-off). October–December lost 24 points in 2022 and 19 in 2025, when soda ash was sliding.
How to use this

For these stocks, April–July has been the reliable window and the fourth quarter the risky one, especially when soda ash or caustic prices are already falling. Use the Chinese soda ash futures (in the table below) as the real-time check: the soda ash names have followed that price's direction far more closely than the calendar.

8 · What moves demand and margins

Capacity waves, power and chlorine

Caustic and chlorine

  • Domestic capacity additions: ~1.2 mt added in FY24 on a ~6.2 mt base, more than 1 mt more over FY25–26, while demand grows ~4–5% a year. New plants due: Indian Peroxide (Dahej, around October 2026), Valor (Mundra, 2026–27).
  • Export prices: surplus must be exported at export parity (August 2026 FOB Gulf: flakes $780–800/t, liquid $270–340/t); high ECU costs and weak chlorine netbacks make India less competitive.
  • Alumina capacity: each new alumina refinery absorbs large caustic volumes (~0.9 mt of caustic for the ~3 mtpa alumina addition in FY25).
  • PVC demand and PVC margins: set the value of chlorine; DCW lost ₹50–55 cr on PVC in Q1 FY27.
  • Power and coal prices; renewable power adoption (GACL).

Soda ash

  • Chinese capacity: the Berun natural soda ash project (5 mt) plus more than 5.5 mt of synthetic additions in 2024. Chinese futures averaged ¥1,301/t in 2025 and ¥1,141 so far in 2026.
  • US trona exports: low-cost US producers compete for Asian markets, which crushed Tata's US margins in Q1 FY27.
  • Trade protection: India's minimum import price, and the anti-dumping petition (GHCL and others).
  • Glass demand: construction, autos, and solar glass for India's module build-out.
  • Energy costs for synthetic plants (coal, gas), higher in 2026.
  • Lithium carbonate demand (battery supply chain).

The anatomy of a chlor-alkali cycle

  1. Energy or environmental shock (China's power curbs, Europe's gas crisis) removes high-cost supply → caustic and soda ash prices jump (2016–17, 2021–22).
  2. Record ECUs → Indian producers announce expansions; small caps multiply (Primo, Tuticorin, Lords).
  3. Capacity arrives together (India's 2023–26 caustic wave; China's soda ash wave) → exports at export parity, chlorine turns negative, prices halve.
  4. Protection and closures (minimum import prices, anti-dumping petitions, idled European plants) slowly rebalance the market.

Patterns that repeat

  • The small caps move three to five times as much as the large ones. Primo and Tuticorin rose more than 400% in 2021; Tuticorin fell 68–70% in 2013 and 2018.
  • Soda ash prices lead the soda ash stocks. Tata Chemicals peaked in March 2024 and GHCL in January 2025 as Chinese futures kept falling.
  • Integration saves you in the downturn. GACL's derivatives and Tata's protected Indian business held up in 2026; standalone caustic and PVC-exposed players suffered.
  • Policy is a lever, not a floor. The soda ash minimum import price has not stopped cheaper imports; stocks respond more to Chinese prices than to Indian policy news.

  • China holds ~45% of world soda ash capacity and swings between caustic exporter and importer depending on its PVC demand.
  • Country leadership shift: Europe (Solvay invented the process in the 1860s) lost ground to US trona in the 20th century and to China in the 21st. Natural ash keeps pushing the synthetic cost curve down.
  • India is short in soda ash, long in caustic, and structurally dependent on China's PVC and glass cycles.

11 · Where technology is going

Five shifts

  • Renewable power for electrolysis: the largest cost lever; producers are signing solar and wind PPAs (GACL credits renewables for its record quarter).
  • New-generation electrolysers: zero-gap membrane cells cut power per tonne (Chemfab's 2026 INEOS upgrade).
  • By-product hydrogen used as fuel, for hydrogen peroxide, or as a low-cost green-hydrogen stepping stone; electrode makers (De Nora) are also chasing green-hydrogen electrolysers.
  • Chlorine integration into higher-value derivatives (ECH for epoxy, chloromethanes, CPVC, specialty chlorinated intermediates for pharma and agrochem).
  • New soda ash demand from solar glass and lithium carbonate; natural ash keeps pushing the synthetic cost curve down.

15 · Adjacent industries and proxies

Up and down the chain

  • Upstream: Salt producers (015); power companies and renewable developers selling PPAs.
  • Chlorine sinks: PVC (DCW, Chemplast, Finolex, DCM Shriram), fluorochemicals (chloromethanes feed refrigerants, 007), specialty chemicals using chlorination (agrochem and pharma intermediates).
  • Caustic users: aluminium (Hindalco, NALCO, Vedanta), textiles and viscose (Grasim), paper, FMCG.
  • Soda ash users: glass (Asahi India, Borosil Renewables in solar glass), detergents (HUL, Nirma), sodium silicate makers, lithium refiners.
  • Electrolyser suppliers: De Nora India; the green-hydrogen theme.
  • Global read-through: WE Soda, Genesis, Solvay/Syensqo, Chinese soda ash futures (Zhengzhou exchange).

16 · Questions for management

Concall checklist

  1. What was ECU realisation this quarter, and the chlorine netback?
  2. What share of power is captive or renewable, and the cost per kWh?
  3. What share of chlorine goes to your own derivatives vs merchant sales?
  4. What are the caustic export share and export netback vs domestic?
  5. Soda ash: realisation vs landed Chinese import; contract share with glass makers?
  6. What are utilisation and the capex pipeline; expected payback at current ECU?
  7. (Tata) How do US soda ash margins recover if Asian prices stay low?

Red flags

  • Capacity expansion without a chlorine sink.
  • Grid power dependence in a high-tariff state.
  • Soda ash margins that rely on policy that hasn't been notified.
  • Rising inventory of flakes (unsold caustic).
  • Profits that depend on one-offs (tax credits, settlements).

17 · Myths and live debates

What you'll hear, and the evidence

ClaimEvidence
"Caustic demand from alumina will absorb the new capacity."It helps (about 0.9 mt from FY25 alumina additions), but capacity is still growing about twice as fast as demand.
"Solar glass will fix soda ash."It is a real demand driver, but Chinese additions of more than 10 mt dwarf any demand increment; futures are at series lows.
"Chlorine is a by-product, so ignore it."Chlorine often decides whether plants run; negative chlorine can wipe out caustic gains, and DCW's PVC loss did in Q1 FY27.
"The minimum import price protects soda ash producers."Producers themselves describe it as a "small benefit"; imports below it continue.
"Tata Chemicals is an India soda ash play."Its Indian business is doing well; the US and UK businesses, which earn world prices, decide the consolidated result.

19 · Test yourself

Eleven questions; answers hidden

1. Why can't a producer make only caustic soda?

Electrolysis gives chlorine and caustic in a fixed ratio (1 : 1.1), so you can't make one without the other.

2. What is ECU realisation and why can it fall when caustic prices are stable?

Caustic × 1.1 + chlorine. If chlorine turns negative, the ECU falls even with caustic unchanged.

3. What three things make a chlor-alkali plant advantaged?

Cheap captive or renewable power, an owned chlorine sink, and proximity to a large caustic buyer.

4. Why is India exporting more caustic?

Capacity is growing ~9% a year vs demand ~4%, so the surplus goes abroad at export parity.

5. Why are natural soda ash producers cheaper?

They mine trona instead of synthesising soda ash from salt, limestone and energy.

6. What made 2021–22 a boom?

The global energy crisis and Chinese power curbs cut supply of caustic and soda ash, driving prices to records.

7. How does the monsoon affect chlorine?

Construction slows, so PVC demand dips and chlorine becomes harder to place.

8. What is the policy catalyst for soda ash?

A final DGTR anti-dumping finding and its notification; the minimum import price alone hasn't stopped imports below it.

9. Why did Tata Chemicals report a consolidated loss in Q1 FY27 despite a strong India business?

US soda ash EBITDA fell from ₹188 cr to ₹3 cr on weak Asian export prices, and the UK lost money.

10. Which quarter has been the risky one for these stocks, and why?

October–December, especially November, when soda ash or caustic prices were already rolling over (2014, 2022, 2025).

11. Why is De Nora India flagged?

It supplies electrodes and electrolysers to chlor-alkali plants; it doesn't make caustic or chlorine.
