1 · In 60 seconds

For a 10-year-old

Fluorine is the "grabbiest" element: once it sticks to something it never lets go. That makes pans non-stick, carries heat inside ACs and makes medicines last longer. It's also dangerous (its acid eats glass), so only a few companies can handle it. It starts as a rock, fluorspar. Some fluorine gases warm the planet, so countries cap how much can be made, and the few makers with permits earn more.

For a client

Fluorspar → hydrofluoric acid (HF) → refrigerants, fluoropolymers, battery chemicals and fluorinated intermediates. Refrigerants are a quota business: China froze HFC output in 2024, pushing R32 up ~80%. India freezes in 2028 on a 2024–26 baseline and stops clearing new HFC plants after 2027, so everyone is adding capacity now (Tanfac's R32, SRF's Odisha complex). All four listed players had strong Q1 FY27 results. The durable value is in fluoropolymers, battery chemicals, HFOs and contract manufacturing; the risks are refrigerant normalisation and EU PFAS rules (not before 2029).

Fund-manager lens

R22 → PTFE is the key link: R22 is banned as a refrigerant but allowed as feedstock, so integrated makers get cheap PTFE. Refrigerants are the profit swing; fluoropolymers, batteries and CDMO are the durable growth. India's 2024–26 baseline is a one-time land grab for production rights.

4 · How money is made

  • Refrigerants: commodity pricing, but supply capped by law; spreads explode when demand outgrows quotas (2025–26).
  • Fluoropolymers: grade approvals customer by customer; 25–35% margins in good years.
  • CDMO: multi-year contracts; value tied to the molecule's success.
Analogy

A refrigerant quota is a taxi licence: cap the taxis, demand rises, licence holders earn more. Fluoropolymers are a specialist surgeon: slow to win trust, steady fees after.

History: until 2012–13 SRF and Navin earned carbon credits for destroying HFC-23; that income vanished when CER prices collapsed.

5 · Calendar and what the stocks do

AC and refrigerant stocking October–February; peak demand March–August (India March–June, US/Europe May–August); China sets next year's quota in Q4. Method: see "How to read these primers".

Basket: 4 names (Srf Limited, Gujarat Fluorochemicals Ltd., Navin Fluorine International Limited, TANFAC Industries Limited), equal-weighted, monthly from Jul 2011, compared with the Nifty 50.

  • Strongest months vs the Nifty: Apr +7.0 pts (beat in 12 of 15 years); Aug +5.6 pts (beat in 10 of 16 years); Jul +5.4 pts (beat in 13 of 16 years).
  • Weakest months: May -2.9 pts (beat in 8 of 15 years); Feb -0.8 pts (beat in 7 of 15 years); Nov -0.8 pts (beat in 6 of 15 years).
  • Windows: Feb–Mar beat the Nifty in 7 of 15 years (median -1.3 pts); Apr–Jul beat the Nifty in 8 of 15 years (median +4.2 pts); Aug–Sep beat the Nifty in 11 of 14 years (median +9.6 pts); Oct–Dec beat the Nifty in 10 of 14 years (median +3.9 pts); Sep–Nov beat the Nifty in 9 of 14 years (median +3.6 pts); Jan–Mar beat the Nifty in 8 of 15 years (median +3.3 pts).

Control: small caps as a whole (Nifty Smallcap 100) beat the Nifty in April in 11 of 15 years, by an average of 3.0 points, and lag in January–February. Part of any April strength is this market-wide effect. After removing it, this industry's own April edge is about +4.0 points.

Reading it: a real mid-year edge. July (13 of 16) is as reliable as April (12 of 15), and August–September beat the Nifty in 11 of 14 years: gas prices firm into the northern summer and Q1 results (July–August) show it. May is the soft month.

A tendency, not a rule: small samples, survivorship bias (only names still listed) and equal weighting. Use it for timing context, not as a signal.

  • Exceptions: August–September missed only in 2012 (carbon credits), 2024 and 2025 (both marginal); April missed in 2013, 2019 and 2025; July in 2023 (agro destocking).
  • Biggest windows: April–July 2021 (+61.9) and 2014 (+58.7).

7 · Drivers, world, technology, policy

  • China's HFC quota (six producers hold 90%; Juhua ~38%) is the biggest price driver.
  • India's baseline race before the end-2027 clearance cut-off: watch for oversupply after 2028.
  • AC penetration (~10% in India), heatwaves, and data-centre cooling.
  • Batteries: LiPF₆ and PVDF demand from non-Chinese cell makers.
  • Refrigerant generations: CFC (banned) → HCFC R22 (phasing out) → HFC R32 (Kigali phase-down: −10% 2032 … −85% 2047) → HFO/natural.
  • Western majors (Chemours, Honeywell, Daikin, Arkema) hold HFO patents; China has the volume; India is the non-Chinese alternative.
  • EU PFAS restriction: Commission decision not before Q3 2027; application from 2029, with a conditional route for fluoropolymers.
  • US AIM Act (−40% HFC from 2024) supports US prices.

Stocks: the basket is +29.2% this year vs -10.7% for the Nifty (to 18 Sep 2026). Navin and Tanfac trade above their 2020–25 peaks; SRF is 24% below its peak despite a record Q1, on its own normalisation warning and Chinese pressure on specialty chemicals.

Track: Chinese R32/R125 prices (weekly) and next year's quota (Q4); Indian AC sales; fluorspar and sulphur; new Indian HFC capacity filings before end-2027; GFL battery orders; ECHA PFAS milestones.

Ask management: refrigerant share of profit at "normal" prices; capacity counted in the baseline; HF integration; PFAS exposure. Red flags: extrapolating peak prices; battery capex without offtake; a crowded R32 build-out.

Debate: "Pricing is structurally higher" — partly, but India is adding capacity and SRF expects normalisation. "PFAS will kill fluoropolymers" — unlikely; key uses have no substitutes.

10 · Test yourself (5) and sources

1. Why are refrigerant prices at decade highs?

China froze HFC output in 2024 while demand grew.

2. Why is R22 still made?

It's allowed as PTFE feedstock.

3. Why does 2024–26 output matter in India?

It sets the baseline, the cap from 2028.

4. Why did SRF fall despite a record quarter?

Its normalisation warning, Chinese pressure on specialty chemicals, and lost Middle East exports.

5. What income did SRF and Navin lose in 2012–13?

Carbon credits from destroying HFC-23.
