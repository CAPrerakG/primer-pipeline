1 · The industry in sixty seconds

Explain it to a ten-year-old

Imagine a bicycle, a lift and a workshop press. The bicycle needs to turn fast pedalling into useful movement. The lift must move a heavy load at a controlled speed. The press must push hard without losing control. Gears and belts carry turning power between shafts. Hydraulic equipment uses pressurised liquid to create force and movement. Lubrication keeps moving surfaces from damaging each other. These products work together inside machines, but the companies making them earn money in different ways.

Say it to a client in one minute

This primer merges Industrial Gears & Transmission and Hydraulics & Motion Control: fourteen watchlist names, nine companies in the historical price basket. It covers gearboxes, belts, fluid couplings, hydraulic components, presses, lubrication systems and repair. Five companies remain in the discussion but are excluded from the basket for business classification or price-history reasons.

The nine-stock basket gained 21.1% in 2026 through 18 September against Nifty −10.7%. That price recovery is broader than any one product, but the operating recovery is uneven. Elecon has growing orders and distorted comparisons; PIX has improving belt economics plus a large investment gain; Shanthi has a rising share price alongside weaker revenue and profit. A hydraulic label also conceals investment income at AG Ventures and a tools business at KPT.

The investment question is which supplier gets paid repeatedly for keeping a customer's machine productive, and how much cash it must commit to earn that payment. An expensive gearbox can create a service opportunity for years, but service revenue must actually appear. A belt is a wear item, but competition and distributor inventory can absorb its apparent advantage. A hydraulic component may be difficult to qualify without giving its maker control of the complete machine's price.

Read this primer in three passes. Identify the product and legal entity first. Reconcile operating earnings, investment income, tax and the share count second. Then compare the stock's behaviour with the evidence from orders, margins and cash. Reversing that order encourages attractive share-price charts to become explanations of demand that the company has never supplied.

The fixed price date is 18 September 2026. All fourteen member series were checked at that date before the basket was calculated. Financial reporting periods and release dates are identified separately: a June result describes April–June operations, while an annual cash-flow statement describes the preceding full financial year. Neither should be presented as a live September factory reading.

2 · What the products actually do

A gearbox changes the relationship between input speed and output torque. The motor may run most efficiently at a speed different from that required by a conveyor, mixer, mill or process machine. Gear teeth transmit force between rotating elements, while the housing, bearings, seals and lubrication keep the assembly aligned and protected. The engineering problem is to deliver the required duty reliably, including starts, shocks and temperature changes. Elecon's annual report and Shanthi's report describe the businesses behind the two most obvious gear names in this roster.

A helical gearbox uses angled teeth; a bevel arrangement can change the direction of the shaft; a planetary arrangement distributes load through multiple gear engagements around a central element. These categories do not establish a universal ranking of quality. The appropriate design depends on load, space, required ratio, duty and maintenance. A large housing photographed at a factory is not evidence of a superior margin unless the company can explain the engineering content, production yield and selling terms of the finished assembly.

A belt drive connects pulleys using a flexible belt. Frictional V-belts and toothed synchronous belts solve different problems. The compound, tensile cords, profile and dimensional consistency affect performance. Belt selection also depends on the pulleys, tension, alignment and operating environment. PIX's annual report identifies its industrial rubber-products business. A belt supplier is exposed both to new equipment and to replacement demand, but it is not insulated from distributors reducing stock or customers delaying maintenance.

A fluid coupling transfers rotating power through moving fluid between an input impeller and an output runner. It can soften starting and influence the transmission of load. This is a hydrodynamic device, distinct from a positive-displacement hydraulic pump feeding a cylinder. Fluidomat belongs in this merged primer because it connects a motor to a driven machine, not because all equipment containing oil is economically identical. Its company presentation is useful for the product range, applications and expansion plan.

Hydraulic pumps supply flow; resistance to that flow creates pressure within the system. Valves direct or control the fluid, cylinders turn fluid power into linear force, and hydraulic motors turn it into rotation. The reservoir, filters, pipework, seals and controls are part of the complete circuit. A component maker can sell to an equipment builder, a system integrator or a maintenance user. The buyer's requirements differ across those channels even if the component's outside appearance looks similar.

Yuken and Veljan give this roster exposure to hydraulic products rather than only complete project machinery. Their annual and company reports should be read for the manufacturing boundary, subsidiaries and end uses. Bemco's hydraulic presses and related machines add an equipment-project layer. Hy-Tech supplies hydraulic fittings, placing it closer to the connection points in the circuit. A fitting's small share of the machine's cost does not make sealing, pressure integrity or dimensional consistency unimportant.

Lubrication equipment serves a different purpose from a hydraulic actuator. It delivers the required lubricant to moving surfaces rather than using fluid primarily to move a load. Cenlub's report describes this lubrication-system position. The commercial opportunity is tied to equipment reliability and maintenance, but the company's revenue is not a direct measure of gear manufacturing or hydraulic pump demand. Including it in a motion-system basket is a disclosed analytical choice.

United Van Der Horst adds repair and reconditioning as well as manufacturing. Restoring a worn cylinder, marine-engine component or other engineering surface can extend the customer's asset life. That requires inspection, machining, surface treatment, testing and a judgement about what can safely be recovered. Its annual report matters because the business cannot be understood from the word “hydraulics” alone. The company is excluded from the return basket for price-data reasons, not because the operating activity is fictitious.

ITL combines manufacturing of machines with trading of hydraulic and other engineering products. It therefore links machine-tool demand and distribution economics. Buying a component and reselling it has a different gross-margin and working-capital pattern from designing and making a complete machine. The annual report makes that mixed activity visible. It is useful coverage, but it must not be described as a pure hydraulic-component manufacturer.

3 · The value chain and who keeps the money

Think of a restaurant kitchen. One supplier delivers ingredients, another makes the oven, another installs the kitchen and another maintains it. All benefit when the restaurant expands, but their invoices arrive at different times and their bargaining power differs. Industrial motion has a similar chain: materials and purchased parts become precision components, components become assemblies, assemblies enter machines, and the installed machines later need spares, inspection and repair. The investor must locate the listed company at a particular step.

The chain starts with suitable metals, elastomers, castings, forgings, bearings and seals. Material specification is a starting condition rather than a complete product. Heat treatment, tooth cutting, grinding, bore finish, surface treatment, compound mixing and testing convert the input into a part that can perform its intended duty. Yield losses or rework can destroy the margin on an apparently premium order. A supplier with an impressive catalogue may still be constrained by one heat-treatment furnace or one testing station.

Gearbox makers such as Elecon and Shanthi integrate multiple manufactured and purchased elements. Their value includes application selection, gear design, assembly and testing, delivery reliability and field support. Elecon also has material-handling equipment, so its consolidated orders cannot all be interpreted as identical gear units. Elecon's presentation separates the business discussion; Shanthi's release reports one gearbox-and-gear-products business and gives its order book.

PIX occupies the belt-manufacturing and distribution chain. A wide range of sizes and specifications helps serve diverse applications, but requires inventory discipline. Availability can be a competitive advantage when a customer's machine is stopped. It can also tie up cash in slow-moving stock if demand forecasts are poor. The investor should ask whether stock is held at the factory, overseas subsidiary or independent distributor, because a shipment between those points may not represent final consumption.

Fluidomat's coupling sits at the interface between the driver and the driven machine. Selection and commissioning matter because the unit must suit the motor, load and duty. Its expansion presentation includes production capacity and testing capabilities. A capacity increase only becomes economically useful if the company can win appropriate orders, make the required range and complete acceptance without a disproportionate increase in inventory or receivables.

Hydraulic-component makers sit upstream of system builders and equipment manufacturers. Yuken's group also contains a foundry segment, creating internal transactions that must be eliminated before arriving at consolidated revenue. Veljan's group includes its wholly owned UK Adan Holdings and Adan operating subsidiary. Its consolidated result therefore includes more than the Indian parent factory; the overseas boundary must remain consistent when comparing margins. Bemco sells hydraulic machinery and therefore carries project scheduling and acceptance risk in addition to component economics. Bemco's report should be read as an equipment-business document, not as a proxy for recurring valve replacement alone.

Hy-Tech's fittings are connective components. Cenlub supplies lubrication systems; ITL combines machine manufacture and engineering distribution; United Van Der Horst works in manufacturing and restoration. AG Ventures participates indirectly through its controlling holding in Duncan Engineering, but its own consolidated earnings also include investment and trading activity. KPT and the renamed Indian Link Chain do not belong at the hydraulic-manufacturing step simply because a source watchlist put them there.

Apply the Engineering tests by cross-reference. see 078 for specification and wear: here the question becomes whether a customer's approved gearbox, belt or valve design leads to repeat orders for the same supplier. see 080 for processing depth: a bought-out fitting, precision pump element and tested gear assembly retain different amounts of value. see 081 for quote-to-delivery input risk: the project supplier must explain how material and purchased-component costs reset during a long delivery schedule. These are applications of the earlier tests, not a reason to assume every engineering company has the same economics.

Fund-manager lens

The attractive position is an approved product with repeat demand, controlled delivery risk and cash collection that keeps pace with reported profit. None of those conditions follows automatically from the word “precision”. ITL's trading exposure, Yuken's intersegment sales and Bemco's project deliveries show why comparing one consolidated margin across the roster can be misleading. Start with the invoice and the responsibility accepted with it: component performance, complete-machine acceptance, installation or restoration. That tells you what must go right before the shareholder is paid.

4 · Fourteen names, nine basket constituents

The merge is explicit: six names from Industrial Gears & Transmission and eight from Hydraulics & Motion Control. All fourteen are covered. The price basket contains Bemco, ITL, Veljan, Yuken, Cenlub, Elecon, Fluidomat, PIX and Shanthi. Equal weighting makes a small specialist as influential as a larger manufacturer in each available monthly observation; this is a research basket, not the value-weighted Indian industry.

Elecon is the larger integrated industrial-gear and material-handling exposure. Shanthi provides another gear-products business with a different customer and cost mix. PIX provides belts and industrial rubber transmission products. Fluidomat provides fluid couplings. These four should not be reduced to interchangeable “transmission” stocks: one can benefit from a new capital project while another benefits from running and maintaining existing equipment.

Yuken and Veljan provide hydraulic-product exposure. Bemco adds hydraulic machinery. Cenlub's lubrication systems are an adjacent reliability activity, and ITL's hydraulics trading sits beside its machine-manufacturing business. The latter two are deliberately described as mixed or adjacent exposure. Removing ITL changes the April hit rate, so the classification is consequential and the sensitivity is shown later.

AG Ventures, formerly Oriental Carbon & Chemicals, is a particularly important boundary case. Its annual report records the changed business after the chemicals demerger and its 50.01% holding in Duncan Engineering. Duncan's engineering activity is real; see 023 for that earlier coverage. However, the parent's June segment results show investment/trading profit dominating the group. The listed holding-company share is therefore not used as another pure hydraulic operating company in this basket.

KPT's June segment statement shows power tools, blowers, wind and electric vehicles. Its inclusion in the original hydraulics watchlist is a classification problem. Power-tool economics belong beside the discussion in 082; that cross-reference does not retroactively change the twelve-company roster of 082. KPT remains visible here so that the source-file correction can be made without pretending the name disappeared.

Indian Link Chain is now presented in its exchange filing as RRP Electronics India. The filing reports no operating revenue. The old name is not evidence of an active chain-manufacturing business. It must also not be confused with another private or listed company carrying similar RRP branding. The retained watchlist symbol and ISIN identify this legal entity, while its current filing identifies the actual reporting name and activity boundary.

Hy-Tech Engineers listed on 1 September 2026 and has thirteen price observations by the cutoff. It supplies relevant hydraulic fittings, but a few weeks of prices cannot support a fifteen-year seasonal inference. Its June results were released on the evening of 18 September, after the stock-market close. They are described separately and are not treated as information that explains that day's closing price. The later September presentation and transcript are outside this primer's information cut.

United Van Der Horst is an operating engineering company with manufacturing and job-work/reconditioning revenue. Its price record has a 1,622-day gap between December 2013 and June 2018 and an unresolved two-day adjustment around March 2024. Those are sufficient reasons to leave it outside the return basket while retaining a full business and cash-flow discussion. Exclusion is a statement about the suitability of a series for this calculation, not a negative judgement on every aspect of the company.

5 · Revenue, margins and the profit that belongs to owners

Every amount in this section is in crore rupees unless stated otherwise. June means the quarter ended 30 June 2026; the comparison is June 2025. Other Income is read before profit. Group profit, profit attributable to owners, operating EBITDA and total comprehensive income are different measures. A financial summary can put exceptional items or associate earnings into an expanded “other income” line, so the primary statement controls the classification.

Elecon reported revenue of ₹520.56 crore and ordinary Other Income of ₹21.91 crore. PAT was ₹70.35 crore; EPS ₹3.14 multiplied by about 22.44 crore shares gives ₹70.46 crore, consistent with the rounding of the printed EPS. The primary release is the basis. A mechanical comparison with prior-year PAT of ₹175.44 crore would miss the ₹80.47 crore net exceptional gain from reclassifying the Eimco investment and the arbitration settlement embedded in the earlier quarter. Prior EPS ₹7.82 on the same shares gives about ₹175.48 crore.

The earlier Elecon quarter contained ₹25.29 crore arbitration-related revenue and ₹9.75 crore in Other Income. Removing the identified revenue item takes the comparison base from ₹490.57 crore to ₹465.28 crore; against that base, the new quarter's ₹520.56 crore is about 11.9% higher rather than the reported revenue growth of about 6.1%. This is a revenue adjustment, not a licence to subtract pre-tax items directly from after-tax profit. Management's adjusted comparison uses its own profit bridge and must be labelled accordingly.

For a consistent operating calculation, Elecon's June PBT ₹93.16 crore plus finance cost ₹8.11 crore plus depreciation ₹29.76 crore less ordinary Other Income ₹21.91 crore gives ₹109.12 crore operating EBITDA, about 21.0% of revenue. Prior-year operating EBITDA on the same broad bridge was ₹130.30 crore before removing the identified arbitration revenue; after that removal it is about ₹105.01 crore. Thus an underlying improvement can coexist with a large fall in statutory PAT. The conclusion depends on the disclosed adjustment, not an invented normalised tax rate.

PIX reported revenue ₹149.2476 crore and Other Income ₹27.4226 crore. That other-income line includes ₹25.2949 crore of investment fair-value gain through profit and loss. PAT ₹52.7477 crore approximately checks against printed EPS ₹38.71 times 1.36241 crore shares, about ₹52.74 crore. Exact division gives about ₹38.7165, conventionally ₹38.72; the filing prints ₹38.71. This small precision difference remains disclosed, with statutory PAT retained. The June notes make the investment contribution explicit. The operating bridge, ₹66.6391 crore PBT plus ₹0.6059 crore finance cost plus ₹5.8331 crore depreciation less ₹27.4226 crore Other Income, gives ₹45.6555 crore, or about 30.6% of revenue.

That is a substantial operating contribution, but it is smaller than the statutory profit headline might suggest. The prior year's June PAT was ₹27.8598 crore, checking against EPS ₹20.45 on the same share base. Investors should distinguish improvement in the belt operation from a favourable market value movement in investments. A higher fair value is an accounting gain; it does not prove that a customer ordered more belts, accepted a price increase or paid an overdue invoice.

Shanthi presents the opposite price-and-profit combination. Revenue fell from ₹134.89 crore to ₹115.49 crore. Ordinary Other Income was ₹4.16 crore, compared with ₹3.77 crore. PAT fell from ₹22.69 crore to ₹9.85 crore. EPS ₹1.28 times roughly 7.67 crore shares gives ₹9.82 crore, within printed rounding; prior EPS ₹2.96 gives about ₹22.70 crore. The company release reports an unexecuted order book of ₹378 crore and quarterly free cash flow of ₹16.2 crore, but neither number cancels the decline in current earnings.

Shanthi's operating EBITDA on the same PBT-plus-finance-plus-depreciation-less-other-income bridge is ₹15.18 crore, about 13.1% of revenue. The prior quarter-year comparison was about ₹30.51 crore, or 22.6%. This is not merely an exceptional-item comparison: the latest quarter has weaker operating conversion. The FY26 labour-code charge belongs to the annual comparison and should not be used to explain away an unrelated June operating-margin decline.

Yuken's consolidated revenue was ₹134.3268 crore. Ordinary Other Income was ₹1.0688 crore; a ₹0.3034 crore associate loss was reported separately. Group PAT ₹5.2074 crore differs from owner profit ₹5.2180 crore because the minority interest is a small loss. EPS ₹3.84 times 1.3584 crore shares gives ₹5.2163 crore, confirming the owner-profit basis. The results also show why adding hydraulic and foundry segment sales without eliminating intersegment transactions overstates group revenue.

Veljan reported revenue ₹43.4752 crore, Other Income ₹1.1219 crore and PAT ₹7.6779 crore. EPS ₹17.06 times 0.45 crore shares gives ₹7.677 crore. Prior-year PAT ₹7.3337 crore checks against EPS ₹16.30 on that basis. These are comparatively straightforward June figures in the primary statement, but a clean EPS reconciliation does not itself answer questions about order mix, customer concentration or the future return on manufacturing assets.

Bemco's revenue was ₹15.5840 crore, against ₹11.7055 crore a year earlier. Other Income rose to ₹1.3730 crore from ₹0.5218 crore. PAT ₹2.7240 crore checks against EPS ₹0.62 times about 4.3801 crore shares, around ₹2.716 crore. The consolidated release therefore supports improving operating activity alongside a meaningful non-operating contribution. Its roughly ₹3.2821 crore operating EBITDA is a more useful starting point for machine economics than assuming all profit growth came from press deliveries.

ITL's current standalone revenue was ₹52.1872 crore, Other Income ₹0.5065 crore and PAT ₹3.0002 crore. EPS ₹9.36 times 0.32043 crore shares gives ₹2.9992 crore. The filing explains the change in reporting perimeter after the sale of its MM Metals holding in March 2026. An old consolidated screen that stops at March is not the latest quarterly result. The current statement must be selected deliberately.

Cenlub's revenue was ₹17.0871 crore, Other Income ₹0.4048 crore and PAT ₹1.6706 crore. EPS ₹3.58 times 0.46629 crore shares gives ₹1.6693 crore. Fluidomat reported revenue ₹11.8477 crore, Other Income ₹0.8246 crore and PAT ₹2.2251 crore; EPS ₹4.52 times 0.4927 crore shares gives ₹2.2270 crore. Their respective primary statements show modest absolute profit pools whose tax, treasury and delivery timing deserve attention rather than an automatic small-company premium.

Across these names, the correct comparison is not simply which reported the largest percentage increase in PAT. First remove the known accounting differences without inventing an adjustment for every inconvenient expense. Then ask whether the operating change came from revenue, mix, price, utilisation or lower input cost. Finally test whether the resulting profit was converted into cash. A large order book can be valuable and still require advance spending before it produces distributable earnings.

The honest read

PIX's quarter contains both genuine operating strength and a large investment gain. Elecon's statutory decline overstates the deterioration in its underlying comparison. Shanthi's weaker operating margin is real even though its shares have risen. These observations argue against buying or rejecting the entire group on one earnings-growth screen. The useful ranking is by the durability and cash conversion of the operating bridge, followed by the valuation placed on that bridge.

6 · The audit: classifications, prices and reporting boundaries

The audit

Five names are outside the return basket. AG Ventures has a changed corporate perimeter and investment-dominated earnings. KPT's disclosed segments are tools, blowers, wind and electric vehicles. Indian Link Chain is now RRP Electronics India with nil operating revenue. Hy-Tech has only thirteen listed observations. United Van Der Horst has a long missing-price interval and an unresolved adjustment. Each exclusion is retained in the discussion so the reader can see what was removed and why.

Veljan's 22 May 2024 observation initially showed a fall of about 51.5%, followed by a rise of about 96.5%. BSE's official daily files give unadjusted closes of ₹3,955.50 on 21 May, ₹3,837.65 on 22 May and ₹3,770.30 on 23 May. The neighbouring cached observations used a consistent adjustment scale, while the middle observation had been halved again. That one duplicated adjustment was removed. The official 29 May close of ₹1,933.70 against the previous ₹3,872.35 also confirms the later bonus-related price step. The correction does not change a month-end return.

The price repair is supported by the 22 May BSE daily file, the following day's file and the 29 May file. The saved audit records the original and corrected observation. No unexplained jump was smoothed merely to make a chart more attractive.

Bemco has a 110-day historical gap between 2 September and 21 December 2011, as well as an earlier gap outside the backtest. Missing months are not invented. Its exclusion sensitivity leaves April at 12/15 and changes the 2026 basket return from 21.1% to 22.6%. The existence of a price on the fixed endpoint is necessary, but it does not guarantee uninterrupted historical liquidity or the ability to execute a monthly equal-weight strategy.

AG's June consolidated owner profit is ₹20.7389 crore after minority interest of ₹0.4662 crore is removed from group PAT ₹21.2051 crore. EPS ₹20.76 times 0.99901 crore shares gives about ₹20.7394 crore. Its investment/trading segment reports ₹24.5534 crore PBIT while general engineering reports ₹0.5096 crore. The segment statement supports exclusion from the operating basket while preserving the fact that Duncan Engineering is a real subsidiary. A low ordinary Other Income line does not make investment activity disappear when it sits in a revenue segment.

KPT's June PAT ₹2.8841 crore reconciles to EPS ₹8.48 times 0.34 crore shares, about ₹2.8832 crore. Its primary segment statement, not the old watchlist label, controls classification. RRP's June loss ₹0.0699 crore checks against EPS −₹0.26 times 0.2675 crore shares, about −₹0.06955 crore. Its annual EPS uses a different weighted-share basis from the closing-share shortcut shown on a summary page; the exact annual denominator has not been independently resolved here, so no precise annual per-share valuation is claimed.

Hy-Tech's June PAT ₹4.598 crore checks against EPS ₹0.55 times 8.3532 crore pre-IPO shares, about ₹4.5943 crore. The new shares issued in the IPO cannot be inserted retrospectively into that quarter's EPS calculation. The filing's annual EPS of ₹2.70 differs slightly from the simple arithmetic of annual PAT ₹22.605 crore divided by the June share base, about ₹2.706. This minor denominator/precision issue is disclosed rather than silently replacing the printed figure. The filing also identifies management-compiled historical quarterly comparatives.

United Van Der Horst's June group PAT ₹1.1313 crore checks approximately against EPS ₹0.16 times 6.8944 crore shares, ₹1.1031 crore; low printed EPS precision explains the broad rounding band. Its FY26 PAT ₹5.2303 crore is consistent with EPS ₹0.76 on that share base. The consolidated quarterly history is incomplete before the associate investment, so a four-quarter group reconciliation cannot be claimed where four comparable statements are absent. The primary filing preserves that boundary.

7 · Annual reconciliation and cash conversion

The quarterly table's rightmost date was explicitly checked as June 2026, with June 2025 as the year-earlier comparison where available. The four FY26 quarters were then compared with the annual row. This matters because an apparently plausible shifted column can reverse an earnings conclusion. Some annual summary rows are rounded to whole crore rupees; their difference from the sum of two-decimal quarters is not automatically an accounting error. Primary annual figures provide the more precise benchmark.

ITL's four displayed quarterly revenue figures total ₹203.03 crore, against primary annual revenue ₹203.0322 crore. Displayed PAT totals ₹9.50 crore against primary ₹9.5005 crore; annual EPS ₹29.65 times 0.32043 crore shares gives ₹9.5007 crore. Bemco's quarterly PAT totals ₹14.87 crore against primary ₹14.8676 crore, and EPS ₹3.40 times its roughly 4.3801 crore share base gives ₹14.89 crore within rounding. Their annual statements support these checks.

Cenlub's displayed quarterly PAT totals ₹7.92 crore against primary annual ₹7.9313 crore, within the combined rounding of four quarters. EPS ₹17.01 times 0.46629 crore shares gives ₹7.9316 crore. The March quarter benefits from tax credits, so an annualised March profit is particularly unsafe. The annual statement should be read alongside cash flow: the summary's approximately negative ₹1.21 crore operating cash flow warns that accounting profit did not translate into a comparable inflow during FY26.

Shanthi's four quarterly PAT figures add exactly to ₹76.66 crore, and annual EPS ₹9.99 times approximately 7.67 crore shares gives about ₹76.62 crore. Ordinary annual Other Income was ₹19.63 crore; the ₹4.78 crore exceptional labour-code charge explains why an expanded summary other-income line can read only about ₹14.85 crore. That summary line is not the statutory caption. The annual release and June comparative column establish the correct annual profit and classification.

Yuken's annual owner profit of ₹14.4731 crore must be checked using weighted average shares of approximately 1.33888 crore and EPS ₹10.81. Using the closing share count instead gives a different EPS and is the source of the approximately ₹10.65 summary figure. The annual EPS note resolves the denominator. Group PAT ₹14.3903 crore is also different from owner profit because of minority interests. These are small numerical differences with large consequences if the analyst assumes every profit and EPS line refers to the same numerator.

Veljan's annual PAT ₹25.8370 crore checks against EPS ₹57.42 times 0.45 crore shares, about ₹25.839 crore. Fluidomat's annual PAT ₹20.0618 crore checks against EPS ₹40.72 times 0.4927 crore shares, about ₹20.063 crore. Their annual results provide the anchors. Fluidomat's March PAT ₹10.2677 crore, which checks against EPS ₹20.84 on the same shares, is more than half the annual figure, making delivery timing critical. A strong March is not a recurring quarterly run rate until subsequent deliveries demonstrate it.

PIX's annual PAT ₹106.8872 crore checks against EPS ₹78.45 times 1.36241 crore shares, about ₹106.88 crore. Its annual revenue is ₹582.3147 crore. However, the displayed quarterly Other Income figures do not add to annual Other Income ₹17.4597 crore on an unchanged classification basis. The annual notes put an ₹8.5901 crore annual net investment fair-value loss in Other Expenses and a ₹22.5229 crore loss in the March quarter. Earlier quarterly gains and annual net presentation therefore require a classification bridge. The disagreement is retained rather than treating the summed summary line as an audited annual amount.

PIX's consolidated cash-flow statement reports FY26 cash from operations of ₹171.0816 crore. That is useful evidence of cash generation, but the analyst should still distinguish working-capital release from a permanent increase in operating margin. A company can release inventory in one year and need to rebuild it in the next. The annual cash-flow and inventory notes are the appropriate place to test that distinction. Cash flow should be analysed over a cycle, not awarded a quality premium solely because it exceeds PAT once.

United Van Der Horst illustrates the opposite risk. Its FY26 consolidated cash-flow statement reports only ₹0.075 crore net operating cash flow against ₹5.2303 crore PAT. Inventory absorbed ₹3.6095 crore, receivables ₹3.0383 crore and other current assets ₹4.4706 crore before other movements and tax. Those outflows do not prove the work is unprofitable; they show that growth and execution required funding. The investor needs evidence of collection and completion rather than treating the accounting margin as available cash.

Fluidomat's presentation states FY26 EBITDA ₹25.67 crore and a 35% margin. The straightforward statutory operating bridge from PBT, depreciation and ordinary Other Income gives a different amount. The deck does not provide a sufficient reconciliation here to make those definitions interchangeable. This primer therefore quotes the company's label as a company metric and uses the primary profit statement for audited PAT. It does not quietly force a match by adding or removing an unidentified expense.

Cash conversion has a practical sequence. The supplier buys material, performs work, dispatches equipment, obtains acceptance, raises or finalises the invoice and collects. Advances can finance part of that sequence; retention or warranty terms can delay part of it. A good-looking order intake number can therefore increase borrowing before it increases cash. Ask which stage currently holds the money and what evidence would release it. That is more informative than a generic statement that industrial demand is strong.

Cash is the second test

United Van Der Horst illustrates why an engineering margin cannot be valued before examining working capital. Its small operating cash inflow alongside profitable reported operations means the investor is financing inventory and collection as well as technical capability. PIX offers a different cash outcome, but even there a year of working-capital release must not be assumed permanent. The practical comparison is cash earned across a delivery cycle after the assets required to support it. A product can be valuable to the customer while its supplier still needs more capital than the profit headline suggests.

8 · Critical inputs and the cost-reset problem

The critical inputs differ by product. Gear manufacture depends on suitable steels, cast or fabricated housings, bearings, seals, lubricants, energy and machining capacity. Hydraulic components depend on metals, precision surfaces, seals, cleanliness and testing. Belts depend on elastomers, reinforcement, processing consistency and energy. Treating all three as a simple steel-price trade loses the mechanism through which their costs enter the income statement.

A quoted gearbox can carry material risk between order acceptance and production. If the supplier purchases the steel immediately, it may protect price but tie up cash. If it waits, it may face a higher cost later. Customer escalation clauses can help, but their timing, eligible inputs and documentation matter. The application of the 081 test is to inspect the contract and the buying policy, not to assume every price increase is recoverable because the product is engineered.

Purchased bearings and specialised parts can be just as important as raw steel. A low-cost casting is of little use if the matching bearing, seal or control component is delayed. The supplier may hold safety stock to protect promised deliveries, increasing inventory even when the sales forecast is unchanged. Conversely, removing too much stock can improve one quarter's cash flow while making the next delivery schedule fragile. The relevant question is whether inventory is matched to confirmed demand and realistic lead times.

For PIX, raw-material consumption, stock changes and bought-out goods must be read together. A fall in reported material consumption does not necessarily mean input prices fell by the same amount; product mix, production volume and inventory movements also matter. The material and inventory notes provide the accounting structure. The analyst should connect the physical production story to those notes before explaining every margin improvement as pricing power.

Hydraulic performance depends on manufacturing and assembly cleanliness as well as metal cost. Contaminants or poor surface quality can create leakage, wear or unreliable control. The commercial consequence is warranty exposure, rework and reputational damage. Spending more on inspection and testing can protect future economics, but the amount and effectiveness must be demonstrated. A company that reduces quality cost temporarily may report a better margin before the field consequences appear.

Heat treatment and finishing introduce another risk: a defect discovered late in production has already accumulated material, labour and machine time. The deeper the processing, the larger the potential loss on a rejected part. This is the practical extension of 080. Higher-value work can command a better price, yet it also requires control of the process. Ask about first-pass yield, rejection recovery and bottlenecks, not only the nominal capacity of the plant.

Currency affects both sides of the ledger. An exporter can receive more rupees for a foreign-currency invoice while paying more for imported inputs or overseas inventory. Hedging changes the timing and presentation of those effects. A consolidated group can also report translation movements outside ordinary operating profit. The right question is net exposure by currency and maturity, together with the accounting treatment, rather than whether a weaker rupee is universally good for the company.

Maintenance and energy costs are embedded in production economics. A specialised machine that is unavailable during a critical delivery window can delay acceptance of the whole order. Investment in backup capability may reduce risk but depress near-term asset turnover. There is no universally correct level of redundancy: it depends on the consequences of failure, customer promises and the availability of external processing. Management should explain that trade-off in the language of delivery and cash, not only factory size.

9 · The operating calendar is not the stock calendar

Project equipment, replacement components and distribution have different calendars. A complete hydraulic press may have a long order-to-delivery interval, whereas a replacement belt can be bought when a machine needs it. A lubrication project may be tied to a customer's commissioning schedule. The same quarter can therefore contain strong orders, weak invoicing and rising inventory without those observations being contradictory.

Bemco's FY26 March revenue of ₹37.6652 crore was much larger than June 2026 revenue of ₹15.5840 crore. Fluidomat's March revenue was ₹29.2422 crore against June ₹11.8477 crore. Their June statements demonstrate uneven delivery timing. They do not prove that every March will be the strongest quarter or that every June decline is harmless. The order mix, execution stage and customer acceptance conditions must explain the pattern in each year.

Indian financial-year ends can encourage completion and billing activity, while customers approve new budgets at different points in their own planning cycles. That is a plausible mechanism to investigate, not a measured causal explanation for this basket's April share-price record. An equity price anticipates future earnings and also responds to liquidity, risk appetite and valuation. It can rise before the operating numbers improve or fall despite strong current dispatches.

Export businesses add other calendars. Overseas customers may have different fiscal years, holiday shutdowns and inventory-replenishment schedules. A shipment can leave India near a reporting date while the overseas channel still holds the goods. Group revenue recognition, subsidiary inventory and final demand must be separated. The PIX group accounts and Elecon management discussion are useful starting points for asking about those distinctions, without assuming all export demand follows one seasonal template.

Service demand is often described as defensive because installed equipment still needs attention. That is only partly sufficient. Customers can defer noncritical maintenance, run down spare stocks or postpone an overhaul during weak conditions. Emergency failures may support urgent demand, but emergency business is not a desirable plan for the customer and may be difficult for the supplier to forecast. A durable service franchise should show repeat relationships, capability and cash collection.

For an investor's calendar, write down the next observable event rather than relying on a month name. That could be a confirmed order conversion, trial completion, capacity commissioning, collection of retention, customer approval or a disclosed change in input-cost recovery. Each event has a testable consequence for revenue, margin or cash. A seasonal share-price tendency can help organise attention, but it cannot replace that operating evidence.

11 · What actually changes earnings and valuation

Order intake is the first visible driver for many equipment suppliers, but its quality matters more than the gross total. A new order can have better pricing, a longer delivery period, a larger bought-out content or more demanding guarantees than the existing book. Those differences determine its contribution and cash needs. Management should explain whether an increase reflects more units, richer specifications, a price change or one unusually large project.

Elecon's June-quarter discussion reports order intake around ₹755 crore and order book around ₹1,518 crore, with growth in overseas activity. The same discussion describes a multi-year capital-expenditure programme. These are reasons to investigate future execution, not completed revenue. An order book is a stock of commitments at a date; sales are a flow recognised during a period. Dividing one by the other can suggest coverage, but does not reveal the delivery schedule or cancellation terms.

Shanthi's order book of ₹378 crore in the June release coexists with lower quarterly sales and profit. The useful follow-up is the conversion timing and margin of the unexecuted work, together with the reason for current utilisation and cost absorption. The presence of orders cannot by itself establish that the earnings trough has passed. Shareholders are buying the outcome after execution costs, not the face value printed on order acknowledgements.

For belts and standardised components, channel inventory can be more informative than a formal project order book. Strong dispatches to distributors may precede stronger final demand, or may simply fill shelves. A later period of destocking can depress the manufacturer's sales even when end users are consuming a steady amount. Ask how management measures sell-through, what credit terms support the channel and whether receivables have lengthened alongside sales.

Mix can change margin without a broad demand shift. A customised gearbox, routine replacement belt, bought-out hydraulic item and complete press have different value added. Even inside one company, an export order may carry different freight, warranty and service obligations from a domestic sale. The analyst should avoid attributing every margin change to raw materials when the product and customer mix has moved.

Capacity is another driver only after identifying the bottleneck. More floor space may not increase output if machining, skilled assembly or testing remains constrained. Conversely, one upgraded process can improve throughput without a dramatic new factory. Fluidomat's capacity plan is therefore best monitored through commissioning, product range, qualified output and accepted orders. The announced unit capacity is a physical capability, not a revenue forecast.

Valuation changes when investors alter their view of the durability, growth and risk of earnings. A price increase can anticipate a future recovery that has not yet appeared. It can also overshoot what operating evidence supports. The distinction must remain open: the correct response to a rising stock with weak current profit is to test the forward thesis, not automatically label the market irrational or invent an order catalyst that has not been disclosed.

The final driver is capital allocation. Cash generated by the factory may be reinvested in productive capacity, distributed, used for acquisitions or placed in financial assets. PIX's investment gains and AG's investment segment demonstrate why the use of cash matters to reported earnings. A good industrial franchise and an attractive capital-allocation record are related questions, but one cannot be assumed from the other.

13 · Global competition and the route to exports

The competitive set is wider than the Indian listed roster. Global motion suppliers can combine product design, application engineering, distribution and field service. A domestic company may compete successfully in a particular duty or customer programme without offering the full range of a global group. Conversely, a foreign brand's broad catalogue does not establish that every local customer receives the best delivery, price or support from it.

Flender's diagnostic-services offering illustrates competition beyond the original gearbox. It includes on-site measurements, remote monitoring and specialist evaluation. The lesson is that installed equipment can support a service relationship when the supplier has the capability and customer access. It does not prove that an Indian gearbox maker already earns the same service mix or recurring revenue.

Gates' belt product information highlights compound, reinforcement and application selection. This is a useful reminder that a belt is an engineered product rather than an undifferentiated strip of rubber. The specific performance claims apply to the named products and operating assumptions; they are not used here as universal industry improvements or as evidence of PIX's own product performance.

Bosch Rexroth's discussion of variable-displacement hydraulics describes how matching flow to machine demand can reduce wasted energy and heat in suitable applications. The competitive implication is that buyers may evaluate a system's running cost and control performance, not just the pump's purchase price. Any savings case for an Indian supplier still needs a specific duty cycle, baseline and measured result.

Exporting adds qualification, logistics and service obligations. A customer may require approval of the process, documentation and consistency before ordering. Passing the initial test is valuable, but the supplier must repeat that performance across batches and resolve problems at a distance. Freight, local stock, warranty response and credit can consume part of the apparent price premium. Export revenue should therefore be accompanied by a margin and cash discussion.

Elecon's management transcript describes overseas revenue and order growth. The analyst should ask how much comes from established customers, new applications or a temporary project concentration. A wider geographic mix can reduce dependence on one domestic cycle while adding currency and execution complexity. Diversification is an outcome to measure, not a benefit to assume merely because more countries appear on a presentation map.

For smaller suppliers, a specialist niche can be a sensible route abroad. The company may focus on a product range where response time, customisation or installed references matter. The risk is dependence on a limited number of distributors or customers. A loss of one approval can be material even when the addressable market is described as enormous. Customer concentration and the cost of winning a replacement programme belong in the analysis.

There is no unsupported national market-share table in this primer. Different sources define industrial gears, power transmission, fluid power and motion control differently, and the listed companies span several of those definitions. A precise-looking market-size number would be less useful than a verified customer, product and geography bridge. The investable question is the portion of demand that each company can serve profitably with its existing and planned capability.

14 · Technology: better control, measurable value

Technology can improve the product, the manufacturing process or the service relationship. Those are separate routes to value. A sensor on a gearbox may support condition monitoring; a better grinding process may improve consistency; a redesigned hydraulic circuit may reduce energy loss. The investor needs to know which route the company owns, what the customer pays for it and what new cost the supplier must carry.

Condition monitoring gathers signals such as vibration or temperature and uses them to assess equipment condition. The commercial value depends on interpretation and action. A dashboard that produces an alarm without helping the customer decide what to inspect is less valuable than a reliable diagnosis tied to maintenance. Flender's service example shows the combination of data and expertise; it should not be converted into an assumption of software-like margins for every gearbox manufacturer.

Hydraulic control can evolve through variable displacement, variable-speed drives, proportional valves and integration with electronic controls. Each changes the way the system responds to demand. The economic case is application-specific: the machine's operating cycle, required force, speed, heat and existing equipment determine the benefit. A general energy-efficiency theme is insufficient to forecast orders for Yuken, Veljan or any particular listed supplier without product-level evidence.

Electromechanical actuation can compete with hydraulics in some applications, while hydraulics remains useful where its force and operating characteristics fit the task. The correct research question is which applications face substitution and how the supplier participates in the new design. It is not helpful to predict that all hydraulic systems disappear or that every existing hydraulic product is permanently protected. Customers redesign machines for total performance and cost, not to preserve a supplier category.

Materials and process knowledge also matter. Belt compounds and reinforcement must work together through bending, temperature and load. Gear manufacture depends on geometry, treatment and finishing. Cylinder restoration depends on assessment and the integrity of the repaired surface. These capabilities often look less dramatic than a new digital product, but inconsistent execution can destroy the customer relationship. PIX, Elecon and United Van Der Horst provide distinct examples of where process knowledge sits.

Manufacturing automation can improve repeatability and throughput, but the return depends on volume and flexibility. A machine configured for a narrow range may be highly productive when orders fit that range and underused when the mix changes. The relevant measure is not the purchase price of the machine or the number of robots photographed. It is saleable output, yield, labour deployment, maintenance cost and the capital tied up per unit of contribution.

Testing deserves particular attention in capital-expenditure plans. A supplier may be able to machine more parts but unable to qualify and dispatch more completed assemblies. Additional test capability can therefore unlock revenue that floor-space capacity alone cannot. Fluidomat's expansion discussion should be followed through that sequence: installation, trials, acceptable output, customer acceptance and collection. Each stage reduces a different risk.

Technology claims become investable evidence when they change a measurable outcome. That can be a won specification, a repeat order, lower rejection, better energy performance verified at the customer, faster delivery or a service contract. A product launch without those outcomes remains an option. The analyst should preserve the distinction while recognising that early development work may be necessary before commercial evidence is visible.

15 · Policy and contracts: read the actual entitlement

Policy can influence customer capital spending, manufacturing investment, imports and the cost of labour. The financial effect on a listed supplier depends on the specific rule, eligible product, timing and contract. This primer does not assume a blanket subsidy or quality-control protection for every gearbox, belt, coupling or hydraulic component. A broad manufacturing theme should be traced to an actual order or a documented entitlement before it enters an earnings forecast.

Fluidomat's presentation describes eligibility for a 40% capital subsidy on qualifying production machinery and civil construction within its expansion plan. That wording is narrower than a guarantee that 40% of all announced spending will arrive immediately in cash. Eligible expenditure, approvals, milestones and collection timing must be checked. Until then, the base funding plan should distinguish committed company resources from a prospective government receipt.

The same presentation describes about ₹35 crore of expansion spending and a planned capacity increase from 1,500 to 3,500 units over the stated development period. These are management plans, not completed capacity or a forecast of proportional sales growth. A larger product mix can also change the relationship between units and revenue. The analyst should track the actual commissioning and customer qualification rather than multiply historical revenue by the unit-capacity ratio.

Labour-code-related provisions appear in several primary statements. Shanthi reported a ₹4.78 crore FY26 exceptional charge, KPT ₹0.5504 crore and Yuken ₹6.209 crore. Their annual, quarterly comparative and result notes explain the company-specific accounting. These disclosed amounts are used as earnings bridges; they are not presented as legal advice about every employer or as proof that all future labour costs are settled.

Procurement terms can matter more to an individual supplier than a broad policy headline. Price escalation, performance guarantees, liquidated damages, advance payments and retention determine who funds the job and who bears delay. An order linked to public or infrastructure spending is still a contract with commercial obligations. The investor should ask whether the reported order value includes bought-out content, taxes or work not yet released for execution.

Import substitution needs the same discipline. A local product must meet the customer's specification, delivery requirement and total cost. A tariff or procurement preference can create an opening, but an unqualified product cannot automatically fill it. The evidence is a completed approval and repeat commercial supply. Claims about an entire imported market becoming addressable should be discounted until manufacturing capability and customer adoption are demonstrated.

Corporate actions are another policy-and-disclosure boundary that affects the investor directly. A bonus issue changes the share count and the adjusted price history; a demerger changes the business represented by the old ticker. AG's chemical demerger and Veljan's bonus-related price adjustment show why legal events cannot be ignored in a long chart. The correct remedy is a documented shareholder-return or adjusted-price series, not an unexplained mathematical smoothing.

18 · Useful proxies and misleading shortcuts

Customer capital expenditure is a useful demand context for gearboxes, couplings and complete machines, but the mapping must be specific. A steel or cement expansion may require mechanical transmission equipment, yet the listed supplier must win the relevant specification and order. A broad capex announcement is not the supplier's booked revenue. The nearer proxy is the company's own qualified enquiries, order intake and conversion, with the limits of management disclosure recognised.

Industrial production and capacity utilisation can provide context for replacement and maintenance demand. Higher machine use can increase wear and the need for service. However, a customer can also run existing equipment harder while postponing new purchases, or delay noncritical maintenance despite high utilisation. The direction of the effect differs for a belt maker, a complete press supplier and a restoration workshop. One macro series cannot substitute for their separate business evidence.

Distributor stock and receivable days are useful for standard products, but a change requires interpretation. More inventory can reflect a deliberate service improvement, a new range, longer lead times or unsold goods. More receivables can reflect growth, a change in customer mix or weaker collection. The analyst should ask which explanation is supported by ageing, subsequent receipts and management's operating detail.

Raw-material prices are another incomplete proxy. A gearbox maker's margin depends on the timing of its purchases and contracts; a belt maker's cost depends on its compound and reinforcement mix; a hydraulic-system supplier may carry substantial purchased-component content. The relevant relationship is between realised selling prices and the cost of the goods actually delivered. A spot commodity chart alone does not establish the margin earned on old orders.

Share prices are useful evidence of expectations, not an independent measure of factory output. Shanthi's 2026 rise alongside weaker June operations makes this distinction concrete. Elecon's price decline alongside growing orders shows the converse. In both cases, valuation, earlier expectations and future risks may matter. The analyst should articulate the assumptions needed to reconcile the price and business evidence instead of treating one as proof that the other is wrong.

Use financial ratios with a clear perimeter. A consolidated return on capital can include overseas subsidiaries, financial investments or a foundry; a parent-only ratio may exclude those assets and profits. Cash and debt should be compared on the same basis, and restricted or operationally necessary cash should not automatically be treated as distributable surplus. AG's changed group is a particularly strong reminder that the business behind a ratio can change even when the ticker remains familiar.

The most misleading shortcut is a single sector multiple applied to all fourteen names. A holding company, a new listing, a manufacturer, a trader and a repair business have different earnings quality and capital needs. A sensible valuation starts from each company's sustainable owner earnings and the cash required to maintain and grow them. Only then should comparable companies be used to test whether the proposed price is reasonable.

19 · Twelve questions for management

1 · Which part of the order book is executable in the next reporting periods?

Ask for business, delivery timing, customer readiness and cancellation or rescheduling terms. A large number with no conversion timetable is difficult to model. Follow up on advances, engineering approvals and bought-out components. The answer should explain why unexecuted orders become recognised revenue and collected cash, rather than simply repeat headline order-book growth.

2 · Who owns the specification and what must happen before a customer can change supplier?

Ask whether approval applies to the company, factory, process or specific product. Then ask what repeat business has followed that approval. Qualification can create switching friction, but a customer may approve several suppliers. The useful evidence is programme duration, share of supply, repeat orders and performance obligations, not a generic claim that the product is critical.

3 · How much of the margin change is mix, price, input cost or utilisation?

Request a bridge using the same definition in both periods. Higher revenue can still come with lower contribution if the mix changes. Lower material consumption can also reflect inventory movements. An answer that isolates the drivers allows the analyst to test persistence; a statement that all improvement is operational does not.

4 · How are investment gains, associate results and exceptional items separated?

PIX, Elecon, Yuken and AG demonstrate different accounting routes. Ask for statutory captions, tax effects and the owner-profit numerator used for EPS. Do not accept an adjusted PAT that subtracts a pre-tax gain without explaining tax. The bridge should also remain consistent when an item is a loss rather than a gain.

5 · What cash is tied up between order acceptance and final collection?

Break the cycle into raw material, work in progress, finished goods, receivables and retention. Identify customer advances and supplier credit separately. Ask which balances increased and when they are expected to reverse. A forecast of higher revenue is incomplete without the funding needed to deliver it.

6 · Which manufacturing or testing process constrains the next increase in output?

The answer should identify a specific bottleneck and the proposed remedy. More floor area or nominal unit capacity may not resolve it. Ask about commissioning, trial output, rejection, skilled labour and customer acceptance. Link the capital expenditure to saleable production and cash rather than to an equipment-purchase announcement.

7 · How much demand comes from end users rather than channel stocking?

For belts and distributed hydraulic products, distinguish sell-in from sell-through. Ask where inventory is held, how ageing is monitored and whether credit terms changed. Strong shipments accompanied by slower collection need an explanation. A wide distributor network is valuable only if it supports repeat profitable consumption.

8 · What happens to cost recovery if the delivery schedule slips?

Ask which materials and purchased parts are covered by escalation clauses, when prices reset and who pays for storage or redesign. A fixed-price order with a delayed customer site can carry a very different risk from a standard component sale. The contract, not the product label, determines the exposure.

9 · What does the service business actually earn and collect?

Ask for the distinction between spares, routine maintenance, emergency repair and complete refurbishment. Each has different labour, inventory and warranty requirements. Installed equipment is an opportunity, but the company needs access, capability and a commercial agreement to monetise it. A service narrative should be backed by repeat revenue and cash.

10 · What is the correct group boundary and share denominator this quarter?

ITL changed its subsidiary perimeter, AG demerged chemicals and Yuken has weighted-share effects. Ask for a consistent comparable base and owner-profit EPS reconciliation. A closing share count should not replace weighted average shares where capital changed. This question often prevents a more serious error than another discussion of market size.

11 · What proportion of announced expansion funding is already available?

Separate operating cash, borrowing, fresh equity and prospective subsidy. An offer for sale funds selling shareholders, while fresh issue proceeds fund the company. A subsidy eligibility statement is not cash in the bank. Ask for the timing of each funding source and the consequences if commissioning or reimbursement is delayed.

12 · What result would show that the current strategy is not working?

Ask management to identify a measurable threshold: delayed qualification, poor order conversion, persistent rejection, weak cash collection or inadequate return on new assets. A strategy without a failure condition is difficult to evaluate. The investor should be able to compare the next disclosure with the stated test and update the view without rewriting history.

20 · Debates worth having

Is replacement demand automatically defensive? It can be more recurring than new-project demand, but customers can defer maintenance or reduce spare inventory. A belt and an expensive gearbox overhaul have different replacement decisions. The evidence required is repeat consumption, channel behaviour and the consequence of postponement. A product that wears out has a source of demand; it does not automatically give its supplier pricing power or reliable cash conversion.

Does a high margin prove a moat? It may reflect valuable engineering, but it can also reflect mix, a temporarily favourable input position, other income or low investment in the period. The operating bridge and return on capital are necessary checks. PIX's investment gain and Fluidomat's metric-definition difference show why the caption must be understood before comparing percentages across companies.

Is an order book the same as earnings visibility? It provides more information than an empty pipeline, but its value depends on timing, margin and customer readiness. An order that requires heavy advance spending or carries penalty risk can be less attractive than a smaller repeat order. Shanthi's current book alongside weaker sales illustrates why conversion and profitability remain live questions.

Should AG be included because it owns Duncan? Its engineering subsidiary is real, but the parent share also represents a changed post-demerger perimeter and substantial investment activity. The June segment profit is dominated by that investment activity. Excluding it from this operating basket is therefore a classification decision, not a denial of the subsidiary. The sensitivity showing a higher April hit rate if it were included is disclosed to keep that decision transparent.

Should ITL be excluded because it also makes machines? Its hydraulics and engineering trading exposure is material, so it remains in the merged basket with an explicit mixed-business label. A narrower pure-manufacturing study could reasonably remove it. That alternative changes April from 12/15 to 13/15, which is why the present result should not be described as invariant. The analytical purpose determines the boundary before the backtest is inspected.

Does a rising share price validate the next earnings recovery? It indicates that investors are willing to pay more, which may reflect a forward thesis. It does not establish that the thesis has already occurred. Shanthi requires an operating follow-through test; Elecon requires a fair comparison after exceptional items. Price and accounts should challenge each other rather than being forced into agreement.

Is a subsidy enough to make expansion attractive? A grant can reduce funding cost if received, but it cannot create profitable demand or eliminate commissioning risk. The project still needs saleable output, customer acceptance and a satisfactory return on the company's own capital. Eligibility, recognition and cash receipt are separate stages. The expansion case should be viable under a clearly stated funding and utilisation scenario.

Does one corrected price observation undermine the whole dataset? It shows why the audit is necessary. Veljan's single duplicated adjustment was checked against official daily closes and repaired with a preserved trail. The correction did not change monthly endpoints. Unresolved or much broader problems, such as United Van Der Horst's long gap, receive a different treatment: exclusion from the backtest with continued business coverage.

A useful disagreement

The strongest debate is over what evidence would change the conclusion. An investor optimistic about Shanthi should specify the order conversion and margin recovery expected next. An investor optimistic about PIX should state the belt earnings case without capitalising investment remeasurement as recurring operations. An investor focused on Elecon should reconcile the exceptional comparison before arguing about growth. Those are disagreements that can be tested in future filings.

21 · Glossary: the words behind the accounts

Motion and transmission

Torque
The turning effect of a force about an axis. A gearbox changes the speed–torque relationship; the useful comparison includes the required duty and losses rather than torque alone.
Rotational speed
How quickly a shaft turns, commonly expressed in revolutions per minute. The motor's preferred speed may differ from the driven machine's requirement, creating a role for transmission equipment.
Gear ratio
The relationship between the speeds of connected gear elements. Selecting a ratio is only part of designing an assembly that can carry the required load reliably.
Gearbox
An assembly of gears, shafts, bearings and a housing that transmits power with a chosen speed and torque relationship. Lubrication, sealing and testing affect its reliability.
Helical gear
A gear with angled teeth. Its geometry affects the engagement and forces in the assembly; the label alone does not establish suitability for every duty.
Bevel gear
A gear form used to transmit motion between intersecting shaft axes. Application geometry and load determine the appropriate design and supporting arrangement.
Planetary gear
An arrangement with planet gears around a central gear, typically within a ring gear. Its compactness and load-sharing characteristics must be assessed within the complete design.
Backlash
Clearance between mating gear teeth in the direction of motion. The required clearance depends on the application; neither zero nor excessive clearance is universally desirable.
Service factor
A design allowance reflecting aspects of operating duty and load. It is meaningful only with the manufacturer's selection method and the customer's actual operating conditions.
Duty cycle
The pattern of operation, load, rest and repeated movements over time. It helps determine thermal, mechanical and energy requirements rather than relying only on maximum rated power.
V-belt
A belt with a section that engages a matching pulley groove to transmit power by friction. Compound, cords, tension and pulley condition affect performance.
Synchronous belt
A toothed belt engaging a matching pulley profile. It transmits motion through tooth engagement; correct selection and installation remain necessary for reliable operation.
Pulley
A rotating wheel that guides or drives a belt. Its profile, diameter, alignment and condition affect the performance of the complete belt drive.
Tensile cord
Reinforcement within a belt that carries tension. Cord material and its integration with the belt body influence dimensional stability and service behaviour.
Elastomer compound
The formulated rubber-like material used in a product. Its ingredients and processing are chosen for the required mechanical and environmental performance.
Belt tension
The force with which a belt is installed and operates. Incorrect tension can affect transmission, wear and loads on associated shafts and bearings.
Fluid coupling
A hydrodynamic device that transfers rotating power through moving fluid between input and output elements. It differs from a hydraulic pump driving a cylinder.
Slip
The difference in speed between input and output elements in a transmission context. In a fluid coupling, operating slip is part of how power is transferred and heat is generated.
Impeller
The rotating input element that imparts energy to fluid in a coupling. It must be considered together with the runner and the complete application's load.
Runner
The output element receiving energy from moving fluid in a coupling. Its operating behaviour depends on the interaction with the impeller and driven equipment.
Soft starting
Reducing the severity of starting behaviour for a machine or load. The appropriate method depends on the motor, transmission and mechanical duty rather than one universal product.
Alignment
The relationship between connected shafts or components. Misalignment can impose additional loads and shorten service life even when the individual parts meet their specifications.

Hydraulics, lubrication and repair

Hydraulic system
A system using liquid to transmit power and control movement. Pumps, valves, actuators, fluid conditioning and controls work together; one component cannot describe the whole system's economics.
Hydraulic pump
A device supplying fluid flow. Pressure arises as the system resists that flow; a pump's suitability depends on flow, pressure, duty and control requirements.
Displacement
The volume moved by a pump or motor per cycle or revolution. It is distinct from actual delivered flow, which also depends on speed and losses.
Variable displacement
A design that can change the volume displaced per revolution. It can match flow to demand in suitable applications, but savings require an application-specific comparison.
Hydraulic motor
An actuator converting fluid power into rotary motion. Its operating range and efficiency must match the load and the rest of the circuit.
Hydraulic cylinder
An actuator converting fluid power into linear force and movement. Bore, rod, seals, stroke and mounting must suit the application's duty.
Stroke
The travel available to a linear actuator. A longer stroke can change packaging, stability, manufacturing and testing requirements, so it is not simply more of the same cylinder.
Directional valve
A valve controlling the path of fluid through a circuit. Its function concerns where flow goes, distinct from the circuit's separate pressure and flow-control requirements.
Proportional valve
A valve whose response varies with a control input. It can support more controlled movement than simple on/off operation when integrated into an appropriate system.
Pressure relief
A protective function limiting pressure by providing a flow path at the required setting. It is part of system design, not a substitute for correct component selection.
Power pack
An assembled source of hydraulic power, commonly including a pump, drive, reservoir and supporting components. Its value depends on integration and the duty it serves.
Reservoir
The fluid-holding part of a hydraulic system. Its design supports the system's operating needs; fluid volume alone is not a complete measure of suitability.
Filtration
Removing unwanted particles from fluid. Cleanliness requirements depend on the components and duty, making filtration a reliability issue as well as a maintenance cost.
Seal
A component controlling leakage between surfaces or regions. Material, geometry, finish and operating conditions affect its performance and replacement requirements.
Hydraulic fitting
A connection component in a fluid circuit. Dimensions, material and sealing performance must match the application; a small invoice can still protect a costly machine.
Leakage
Unintended fluid flow within or outside a system. It can affect performance, efficiency and reliability, with consequences determined by the circuit and operating conditions.
Hydraulic press
A machine using hydraulic force to perform a pressing operation. Project delivery, tooling, acceptance and customer readiness can make revenue uneven across quarters.
Lubrication system
Equipment delivering lubricant to working surfaces. Its main purpose differs from using fluid to actuate a load, although both contribute to machine reliability.
Reconditioning
Restoring an existing component to an acceptable condition for its intended use. Inspection, repair limits and testing determine what can safely be recovered.
Job work
Processing or repair performed for a customer under a specific commercial arrangement. Revenue, material ownership and acceptance terms can differ from selling a newly manufactured product.
Surface treatment
A process modifying a component's surface properties. Its value depends on the required service conditions and process control, not merely the presence of a coating.
Condition monitoring
Collecting and interpreting evidence about equipment condition. Its commercial value comes from useful maintenance decisions, not simply installing a sensor or displaying data.

Manufacturing and commercial economics

Specification
The technical and performance requirements a product must meet. Who defines and approves it influences qualification, competition and the supplier's ability to change the product.
Qualification
The process of demonstrating that a product, process or supplier meets a customer's requirements. Approval creates an opportunity; repeat commercial orders establish its economic value.
First-pass yield
The proportion of output meeting requirements without rework. It helps reveal whether a higher-value product is being made efficiently rather than losing its margin in correction.
Rework
Additional processing needed to make an item acceptable. It uses capacity and cost that may not produce additional revenue, especially when defects are discovered late.
Heat treatment
Controlled heating and cooling used to obtain required material properties. Process consistency and subsequent finishing matter to the performance of precision components.
Grinding
A finishing process using abrasives to remove material accurately. Its economic importance depends on the tolerances, surface requirements and throughput of the specific product.
Test bed
Equipment used to test components or assemblies under specified conditions. Testing capacity can constrain dispatch even when machining capacity is available.
Bottleneck
The process limiting useful output in a production sequence. Expanding another process may add capital without increasing completed, acceptable products.
Installed base
Equipment already in use at customers. It can support spares and service demand, but the supplier needs customer access and a viable service offering to earn that revenue.
Aftermarket
Sales supporting equipment after its original installation. Replacement demand can be recurring, while still depending on maintenance behaviour, channel stock and competition.
OEM
Original equipment manufacturer: a customer building a machine or system that incorporates supplied components. Its approval and purchasing terms differ from an end user's replacement purchase.
System integrator
A business combining components into a functioning system. It may own the design, customer relationship and acceptance obligation even when components are purchased from others.
Order intake
New orders received during a period. The figure should be examined for scope, pricing, delivery timing and customer readiness before being treated as future earnings.
Order book
Unexecuted orders at a particular date. It provides demand information but does not guarantee the timing, margin or cash collection of the eventual deliveries.
Execution
The sequence of completing contracted work and meeting delivery requirements. Revenue recognition, customer acceptance and cash collection may occur at different stages.
Acceptance
The customer's confirmation that contractual requirements have been met. Depending on the agreement, it can influence final billing, retention release and warranty commencement.
Retention
An amount held back by the customer under contractual terms. It can delay cash collection after work is completed and needs separate monitoring from ordinary receivables.
Escalation clause
A contract provision allowing price adjustment under specified conditions. Its usefulness depends on eligible costs, timing and documentation rather than the clause's existence alone.
Liquidated damages
Contractually specified compensation for defined failures such as delay. The actual exposure depends on the agreement and circumstances; order value alone does not describe the risk.
Sell-in
Shipments into a distribution channel. They can differ from final customer consumption, particularly when distributors build or reduce inventory.
Sell-through
Sales moving through the channel to the next customer or final user, depending on the definition. The analyst should confirm which level management measures.
Capacity utilisation
Actual use of available productive capacity. Its meaning depends on product mix and the bottleneck; a unit-based measure can hide differences in complexity and value.

Accounts, capital and price evidence

Other Income
A statutory income caption outside operating revenue. Summaries may expand it to include other items, so the primary statement and notes control the earnings classification.
Operating EBITDA
Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation on a stated operating basis. This primer normally removes ordinary Other Income; company definitions can differ and require reconciliation.
PBIT
Profit before interest and tax. Segment PBIT often excludes unallocated group costs and is not directly interchangeable with consolidated EBITDA or owner profit.
PAT
Profit after tax. Group PAT may include amounts belonging to minority owners, so it is not always the numerator used for the listed parent's EPS.
Owner profit
Profit attributable to shareholders of the parent company after minority interests. This is the relevant numerator for checking the parent's earnings per share.
Minority interest
The share of subsidiary results attributable to owners outside the parent group. A minority loss can make parent-owner profit slightly higher than total group PAT.
Associate result
The investor's share of an associate's profit or loss under the relevant accounting treatment. It should be distinguished from sales of the investor's own products.
EPS
Earnings per share, calculated using the appropriate profit and share denominator. Printed rounding means EPS multiplied by shares is a cross-check, not a replacement for audited profit.
Weighted average shares
The time-weighted share count used for a reporting period. It can differ from closing shares after new issues or other capital changes.
FVTPL
Fair value through profit or loss. Remeasurement of qualifying financial assets affects reported profit even when the industrial business has not sold additional products.
OCI
Other comprehensive income, reported separately from profit or loss. An investment remeasurement in OCI should not be added to PAT when discussing operating earnings.
Goodwill impairment
A reduction in the carrying value of goodwill when the accounting assessment requires it. It can materially affect reported earnings without being the current quarter's cash operating cost.
Exceptional item
An item presented separately because of its nature or significance under the company's reporting. Its tax treatment and recurrence need examination before calculating adjusted profit.
Operating cash flow
Cash generated or used by operations after relevant working-capital and tax movements. It can differ sharply from PAT when inventory or receivables absorb funding.
Working capital
Capital tied up in the operating cycle, including inventory and receivables offset by relevant operating liabilities. Growth can increase this requirement before cash arrives.
Free cash flow
Cash remaining after a specified measure of investment spending. Definitions differ; a company-reported number should be read with its calculation and reporting period.
Intersegment elimination
Removal of transactions between parts of the same consolidated group. Adding segment revenues without this adjustment can overstate sales to outside customers.
Fresh issue
New shares issued by the company to raise funds. The proceeds belong to the company, subject to issue costs and disclosed uses.
Offer for sale
Existing shares sold by their holders in an offering. The proceeds go to those sellers, so the total offer size is not the amount raised by the company.
Bonus adjustment
An adjustment to historical prices and share counts for a bonus issue. Applying it twice can create a false daily collapse and rebound.
Equal-weight basket
A basket giving each available constituent equal weight at the stated rebalancing frequency. It differs from a market-value-weighted industry index and from a simple average of annual returns.
Survivorship bias
Distortion from studying today's surviving companies rather than the complete historical opportunity set. It can make a retrospective strategy look more attractive than one available at the time.

22 · Check your understanding

Why merge gears and hydraulics?

They connect the motor, machine and load through different forms of power transmission and control. The merge covers two watchlist sections and fourteen names, while preserving product-level distinctions. A belt, coupling and hydraulic cylinder are not interchangeable businesses.

Why are there only nine basket companies?

AG Ventures, KPT, RRP Electronics India, Hy-Tech and United Van Der Horst are excluded for disclosed business-boundary or price-history reasons. All remain covered in the text. Exclusion from a backtest is not the same as declaring that a company has no operating activity.

What was wrong with the Veljan price?

One May 2024 observation had been adjusted twice. Official BSE daily closes established the correct relationship with adjacent days, allowing a documented repair. It was not a month-end observation, so the repair did not change monthly basket returns.

Does Elecon's PAT decline measure the fall in gearbox demand?

No. Prior exceptional and arbitration items materially distort the comparison. The primary filing supports a separate revenue and operating-profit bridge. A normalised after-tax figure requires a tax bridge; subtracting pre-tax gains directly from PAT is insufficient.

Why is PIX's investment gain separated?

It affects profit but does not represent more belts sold. The same investment accounting produced a loss in the March expense line. Assess belt revenue, operating contribution and cash separately from financial-asset remeasurement before deciding how much earnings are repeatable.

Why can Yuken owner profit exceed group PAT?

A minority share of losses reduces the amount attributed to outside owners, leaving slightly more profit attributable to the parent than total group PAT. EPS must be checked against owner profit and the correct weighted share count.

What does AG's low Other Income line miss?

Investment and trading activity is also presented as a segment within revenue. Its segment profit dominates the June result. Reading the ordinary Other Income caption is necessary, but the segment note is also needed to identify the source of earnings.

Why not annualise Fluidomat's March quarter?

Deliveries and profit were concentrated in that quarter, and the June result was much smaller. The annual statement provides the full-year anchor. A recurring quarterly run rate requires repeat execution evidence rather than multiplication of the strongest quarter.

Does April provide a mechanical trading rule?

No. The selected basket beat Nifty in twelve of fifteen Aprils, with explicit exceptions and sensitivities. Removing ITL changes the count. Size, liquidity, survivorship, monthly clipping and transaction costs limit the interpretation of the historical tendency.

Why is Shanthi a useful counterexample?

Its shares rose strongly in 2026 while June revenue and operating profitability weakened. The price may anticipate recovery, but the order book still needs to convert at an adequate margin. The next operating result tests that expectation rather than being predetermined by the chart.

Is the entire Hy-Tech offer cash for the company?

No. Fresh shares raise company funds, while an offer for sale pays the selling holders. The June EPS also uses the pre-IPO share basis. Mixing these distinctions overstates funding and produces an incorrect per-share comparison.

What makes an expansion economically successful?

Commissioned machinery must produce acceptable output, win orders, earn a sustainable contribution and collect cash. Announced capacity and subsidy eligibility are earlier stages. Track the return on capital after working-capital needs rather than treating equipment installation as the final result.
