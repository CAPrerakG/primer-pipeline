1 · The industry in sixty seconds

Explain it to a ten-year-old

Imagine moving water from a well to a tank on a hill. A pump gives the water enough energy to climb. A different pump might move thick toothpaste through a factory, while another keeps hot water circulating inside a power station. The machine must suit the liquid, the height and the amount needed. Buying the biggest pump is like buying the biggest shoes: it can make the job worse. The company supplying the right machine also needs to install it properly, maintain it and collect its bill.

Say it to a client in one minute

The watchlist contains eight companies, but the businesses span industrial equipment, international projects and government-supported solar irrigation. Seven qualify for the price basket; GK Energy's short listed history excludes it from the backtest, not from the research. Through 18 September 2026 the basket lost 15.1%, against Nifty's 10.7% fall. That headline hides a positive return from the four established industrial-pump names and much weaker solar-pump shares.

The commercial distinction is who specifies the machine, who finances delivery and how long collection takes. A specialist pump installed in a critical process can support repeat service income. A large solar installation programme can grow unit volumes while tender prices and cash collection deteriorate. Both move liquids; they need different investment questions.

The central lesson is that a pump is part of a working system. Its casing, impeller or rotor, seals, motor, drive, pipework and control logic must function together. A manufacturer can have impressive machining capability and still supply a badly chosen duty point. An installer can own little manufacturing equipment and still provide valuable coordination. An equity analyst must identify the actual responsibility sold to the customer before deciding what margin or return on capital should be possible.

2 · What the customer actually buys

Product / requirementFunctionSelection and economics
A duty point, not a horsepower label

A customer needs a specified flow at a specified head. Flow is how much liquid moves in a given time. Head expresses the energy added per unit weight of liquid, commonly as an equivalent height. The system imposes its own requirement through elevation, pressure and friction. The intersection between the pump's performance curve and the system curve determines the working point. A brochure's maximum flow and maximum head generally describe different conditions; placing both maxima together in a sales comparison can imply a capability the pump does not have. KSB operating-point explanation

The best efficiency point is where a particular pump converts input power into useful hydraulic output most efficiently. A real installation operates across changing conditions, so the question is how much time it spends in an acceptable operating range. Oversizing followed by heavy throttling may waste energy and move operation away from favourable conditions. A variable-speed drive can help match changing demand, but its economics depend on the system, operating hours and control strategy. It cannot remove static lift or repair unsuitable suction conditions.

Centrifugal and positive-displacement machines

A centrifugal pump uses a rotating impeller to transfer energy to a liquid. Its usefulness spans water supply, buildings, irrigation, industrial circulation and demanding process duties. A multistage arrangement adds energy through successive stages. A submersible arrangement places the assembly under the liquid, with corresponding requirements for motor protection, materials and retrieval. Horizontal, vertical, end-suction and split-case are configuration choices, not a simple ladder from inferior to superior. Suitability depends on the duty, maintenance access and installation geometry.

A positive-displacement pump moves a defined volume through enclosed spaces. Roto's progressive-cavity designs use a rotor moving within a stator, while twin-screw designs address another set of handling duties. Viscosity, solids, shear sensitivity and dosing requirements can make these designs appropriate where a generic clean-water centrifugal selection would be poor. The application creates opportunities in wastewater, food processing, chemicals and oil-related operations, but each requires the appropriate material and sealing system. Roto's product literature should be read as a map of applications, not proof that every application has equal profitability. Progressive-cavity product range Roto oil and gas applications

The inlet can defeat the whole installation

Cavitation occurs when local pressure falls sufficiently for vapour bubbles to form and subsequently collapse as pressure recovers. It can cause noise, vibration, performance loss and damage. The relevant comparison is between the suction conditions available in the actual system and those required by the selected pump, with an appropriate engineering margin. A larger motor does not automatically solve this. Pipe losses, liquid temperature, tank level and installation height can be decisive. This is why competent selection and commissioning can create more value than a modest discount on the metal casting. KSB explanation of cavitation

A solar pump is an installation package

Solar irrigation adds modules, mounting structures, a controller or inverter, wiring, protection, installation, monitoring and maintenance obligations around the hydraulic machine. The pump must still deliver the required water under the available solar input. Component compliance, field conditions and after-sales coverage become part of the supplier's promise. The panel's nameplate capacity is not a guarantee of daily water delivery under every seasonal or site condition. The customer may buy an approved system through a programme rather than choose an isolated pump from a dealer shelf. PM-KUSUM implementation guidelines

These distinctions explain why pump counts, motor power, megawatts of modules and project rupees cannot be added into one output measure. A large engineered pump and a small irrigation unit may each count as one. A solar EPC order can include substantial non-pump equipment. Comparisons should state the product, scope, unit and acceptance condition before presenting growth. Without that discipline, apparent scale can reflect a change in package content rather than more hydraulic equipment sold.

3 · From specification to cash

The chain starts before a foundry receives an order. A plant owner, consultant, utility or programme authority defines the service conditions and acceptance requirements. Engineers translate those conditions into hydraulic design, material selection and component specifications. The company then buys or makes castings, shafts, impellers, rotors, stators, seals, motors and controls. Machining, balancing, assembly and testing turn those inputs into an accountable product. Distribution or project execution takes it to the site; installation and acceptance turn a dispatched machine into a working system. Collection completes the commercial cycle.

The Engineering-family tests already established in see 078, see 080 and see 081 apply at specific points here. A consultant-approved material and performance specification can strengthen a qualified pump supplier's position. Precision hydraulic surfaces, reliable seals and validated performance explain why two machines with similar metal weight can have different economics. A fixed-price package ordered before input costs rise exposes whoever accepted that price commitment. These are applications of the earlier tests; pump volume alone answers none of them.

The value of testing and the cost of a failure

A performance test makes a hydraulic promise measurable. Material traceability, vibration checks, leak integrity and the appropriate certification can matter as much as nominal factory capacity. The more costly a customer's shutdown, the less useful a cheap replacement becomes if it cannot be qualified quickly. Yet an approved position is not permanent freedom to set prices. Customers can qualify alternatives, standardise specifications and negotiate service contracts. The analyst should distinguish an actual installed-base service stream from the theoretical possibility of one.

After dispatch, responsibility may widen rather than shrink. A project supplier can face delayed civil works, missing power connections, incomplete foundations or a customer's inability to accept delivery. A solar contractor may need inspection and programme documentation before a payment milestone is triggered. Inventory can become receivables without becoming cash. A growing order book therefore carries a funding requirement as well as potential revenue. The useful operational question is who controls the next milestone and who pays while it is delayed.

Fund-manager lens

The attractive position is a combination of technical responsibility and commercial discipline. A solar-system supplier may agree a price before completing procurement and installation. Service mix and the economics of the installed base

Service can contribute expertise, access to the installed base and smaller repeat jobs. Capacity investment can move the break-even point

New production adds depreciation, staffing, utilities and commissioning needs before utilisation reaches plan. In-house module or cell production also brings process and technology risks different from pump machining. The correct comparison is the incremental cash return on the new investment against continued procurement, including working capital and qualification. Management's expected margin improvement after commissioning is a forecast. It should be tracked against commissioning date, eligible output, utilisation and cash expenditure before being embedded as an achieved advantage.

The honest read

Shakti's volume increase and profit decline make the cleanest lesson on this page: more installations do not guarantee better economics. Oswal's wider collection cycle adds a second constraint. A convincing recovery requires evidence that realised system margins and cash conversion improve together. A proposed policy expansion or a new factory can support that recovery, but neither is the recovery itself.

8 · Critical inputs and the boundaries of integration

The critical input is not always the most expensive item on the purchase order. A seal with the wrong material can stop a valuable installation. A delayed qualified casting can hold up the whole delivery. An ineligible solar component can prevent programme acceptance. A scarce test slot can delay recognition even when every physical part is ready. Input analysis should begin with the specification and delivery bottleneck, then examine the bill of materials.

Input / contractEvidenceMargin and funding test
Metals, electrical components and working surfaces

Cast iron, steel and application-specific alloys provide casings and hydraulic parts. Copper and other electrical inputs enter motors and controls. Elastomers matter for seals and progressive-cavity stators; their suitability depends on temperature, chemistry and the fluid's characteristics. Buying a cheaper substitute may reduce material cost while increasing wear or failure risk. This is the pump-specific application of the specification test in see 078: the customer and application determine which savings are genuinely available.

Machining, tolerances, balance and assembly convert purchased material into reliable performance. That applies the processing-depth test from see 080. A company's tonnage or casting capacity alone cannot establish its competitive position. The relevant evidence is whether it delivers the required hydraulic performance, materials traceability and field reliability economically. An external foundry can be appropriate if quality and delivery are controlled; owning a foundry can be valuable without making every other capability automatic.

Solar components create a second procurement system

For a solar installation, module eligibility, cell origin, electronics and structures sit beside the pump's own input needs. Domestic-content requirements are specific. MNRE's March 2025 clarification states that crystalline cells manufactured domestically from undiffused wafers meet the relevant domestic manufacturing interpretation; merely using imported diffused wafers does not. The analyst should therefore distinguish domestic module assembly, eligible cell manufacture and pump manufacture. Pass-through must exist in a contract

The investor should ask whether a tender permits escalation, the reference index used, the lag and the share of the package covered. A verbal expectation that the next round will have better prices is not recovery on the current round. Similarly, an export currency benefit can be offset by imported inputs, hedges or local overseas costs. The exposure belongs in a contract and currency schedule rather than a one-line claim that depreciation of the rupee helps exporters.

Working capital connects these inputs to the balance sheet. Securing material early can protect delivery but increase inventory. Waiting for price declines can improve expected gross margin but risk a missed milestone. Suppliers may extend credit at first and reduce it when an industry slows. The right evidence is the purchase commitment, inventory ageing, customer advance and payment schedule together. A favourable spot commodity price is only one part of that decision.

9 · The operating calendar is not the share-price calendar

The calendar has several overlapping clocks. Farmers need water around cultivation and local conditions. Industrial customers schedule maintenance and capital projects. Governments sanction programmes, allocate funds, issue tenders and approve payments. Companies report results on their own year ends. The stock market anticipates these events and also responds to broader risk appetite. A seasonal share-price pattern cannot establish which operational clock caused it without additional evidence.

11 · What actually drove the historical returns

The annual mover analysis is a check against stories that sound plausible but are not demonstrated by the data. In 2014, Roto's roughly 457% individual gain and strong moves in several peers contributed to a very large basket return. In 2017, Shakti was a particularly strong contributor. In 2024, Shakti's roughly 526% individual rise was far more dramatic than the moves in some established industrial peers. The monthly basket clips extreme individual monthly returns, so its annual return is not the simple average of these buy-and-hold figures.

Those observations establish contribution and breadth. They do not, by themselves, establish that a particular policy, order or commodity move caused the gain. A proper causal history would require dated company and market evidence for each period. The annual notes therefore distinguish measured returns from present-day interpretations. It is better to state that a rally was broad across the selected stocks than attach an unverified macro explanation to every company.

Large winners can hide an uneven year

In 2020, Jyoti's large positive return helped a basket in which several industrial names declined. A positive basket result was not equivalent to universal improvement. In 2018, WPIL rose while most selected peers fell. In 2019, Roto rose while the overall basket declined. These differences are commercially plausible because the companies have different markets and balance sheets, but the price data alone do not identify the precise cause. The point is to prevent one sector return from being used as every company's operating history.

The reverse is true in broad rallies. The 2023 gain involved several large winners, including WPIL, Kirloskar Brothers, Shakti and Jyoti. It was not solely a new solar listing, because Oswal had not yet joined the listed history. The subsequent 2025 decline then affected the established group widely. A strong multi-year share-price run can reflect rising expectations as well as current results; later disappointment need not imply the physical need for pumps has vanished.

2026 separates the businesses again

Through the fixed date, Kirloskar Brothers gained 12.5%, KSB 7.7% and WPIL 5.2%. Roto declined 8.9%, Jyoti 28.6%, Shakti 36.4% and Oswal 47.2%. The practical conclusion is to work from the company up. First establish operating scope, earnings quality, cash needs and valuation assumptions. 14 · Technology: improve the system, measure the result

Efficiency belongs to the operating installation

A high-efficiency component is useful only within a suitable system. Pump selection, pipework, controls, liquid conditions and maintenance determine the result the user sees. The operating point moves when speed or system resistance changes. A measured reduction in energy per useful unit of liquid delivered is stronger evidence than a motor-efficiency label considered alone. Where demand varies, controls may improve the match; where static lift dominates, the savings opportunity differs. Operating-point explanation KSB control methods

For the investor, this suggests a concrete service proposition: diagnose the installation, specify a change, measure the outcome and retain the maintenance relationship. The commercial questions follow naturally. Who pays for the audit? Who bears performance risk? Does the supplier receive a one-time equipment margin, recurring service revenue or a share of verified savings? A technology presentation should show how the value reaches the supplier's cash flow.

Condition monitoring needs an actionable service response

Sensors can help identify changes in vibration, temperature or operating behaviour. The value comes from interpreting the change and acting before a costly failure, not simply transmitting a number. A monitoring offer needs baseline data, false-alarm management, connectivity and access to technicians and parts. If a customer ignores the alert or a replacement cannot be supplied, the potential benefit is not realised. The analytical question is whether the digital feature strengthens the installed-base relationship and earns a return after its support costs.

The same discipline applies to remote solar-system monitoring. A record of operation can support maintenance and programme oversight, but connectivity and field response remain practical constraints. Product availability, compliance and monitoring features should be checked against the actual contract. Materials and application design remain important

Better controls do not remove abrasion, corrosion or incompatibility between the liquid and a seal or stator. Specialist applications may require adapted geometries, materials or handling arrangements. 15 · Solar policy: distinguish an extension from a new scheme

Policy matters because it shapes eligibility, procurement and payment. It should be read from the operative documents, not reconstructed from a company's expectation. The official PM-KUSUM framework distinguishes decentralised generation, standalone solar agricultural pumps and solarisation of existing grid-connected agricultural pumps or feeders. Those activities have different project structures. A company reporting solar megawatts may therefore be discussing a different scope from a company reporting standalone pump installations.

What the 2026 extension actually says

The 28 March 2026 memorandum, attached to MNRE's 25 May clarification, extends the relevant implementation timeline to 31 March 2027 for projects whose PPAs or notices to proceed were signed or issued on or before 31 December 2025. The clarification preserves the requirements around individual scheduled commissioning dates, sanction validity and the applicable guidelines. This is not a blanket permission to assume that every new tender has the same deadline or funding authority. Official extension and clarification

The same memorandum describes PM-KUSUM 2.0 as a proposal. Companies may reasonably plan for a successor programme and discuss expected timing. Those expectations remain management forecasts unless supported by an operative approval. The primer therefore does not turn a call's hoped-for launch quarter into a government commitment. Nor does it assume a successor's tender prices, volumes or payment terms will match the earlier programme.

The May letter contains a reference-year inconsistency when citing the earlier memorandum; the attached original is dated March 2026. Reading the attachment prevents that typographical detail from being carried into the timeline. This is a useful example of why an apparent date in a summary is not enough for a policy-dependent investment assumption.

An award, a notice and a payment are different events

MNRE's September 2025 clarification concerning existing SECI component-B letters of award limits what can be done under expired price validity. It distinguishes existing awards and valid sanctioned quantities from a fresh award issued on an expired price. The economic implication is straightforward: an old empanelment or price schedule does not establish unlimited new executable volume. Even a valid order has a collection path. Installation, inspection, documentation and the programme's payment conditions determine when the supplier can claim and receive funds. A government-linked counterparty may be expected to pay while still paying later than planned. The March 2025 domestic-cell clarification draws a manufacturing boundary at the relevant wafer-processing stage. The May 2025 communication on solar photovoltaic inverters reinforces the applicable quality-control requirement for the stated power range. These rules are specific to components and programme compliance. They do not establish that any listed pump company owns an exclusive market or can ignore competitive tenders. 17 · A monitoring sheet that can change the thesis

Start with a consistent operating bridge

For every quarterly update, record operating revenue, ordinary other income, exceptional items, associate or JV contribution, tax, group profit and owner profit. Record the weighted share count and the period covered. Then compare like with like. A change in consolidated perimeter or accounting presentation belongs beside the growth calculation. This prevents an acquisition, tax credit or minority-interest change from being mistaken for an improvement in the core pump margin.

For KSB, retain the calendar-year convention and use first-half cash flow when available. For Kirloskar Brothers, distinguish domestic and international execution and ask what changed in service mix. For Roto, follow pre-tax operating improvement separately from tax provisions and check spares or application commentary against actual disclosure. For WPIL, record the owner-profit share and the status of restricted tender access alongside project and product revenue. These are different monitoring sheets because the economic questions differ.

Make the order book auditable

An order entry should state the date, customer, scope, currency, execution schedule and cancellation or conditionality where disclosed. The book should bridge opening orders, new intake, execution, cancellations and closing orders. A pipeline is a separate column. A number of pumps is not a rupee amount; a megawatt solar EPC book is not a pump count. If management gives only a total, preserve that limitation rather than inventing a product split.

KSB's institutional presentation and the solar-company calls use different order categories and dates. A spreadsheet that labels all of them simply “June order book” would be wrong before any forecast formula is written. Similarly, a replacement order, a framework approval and a binding project award have different conversion probabilities. Track collections by cohort

Separate bills raised in the current period from old receivables collected. Ask how much remains unbilled because a milestone is incomplete, how much is billed but not due, and how much is overdue. Keep retentions and disputed amounts visible. If management expects a large release next quarter, record the amount and date of the expectation, then compare it with actual cash flow or subsequent disclosure. Track capital spending through useful output

For a new plant, record approved spend, cash paid, construction progress, commissioning, eligible output and utilisation. An inaugurated building is not equivalent to stable commercial production. For solar cells and modules, retain the relevant domestic-content and quality requirements. For engineered pumps, identify qualification, testing and customer approval. A forecast margin uplift should move into reported performance only when the underlying cost and revenue evidence appears.

Finally, record what would invalidate the thesis. A technical franchise thesis weakens if field failures or service economics disappoint. A solar recovery thesis weakens if tender prices remain aggressive and collection stretches further. A project recovery thesis weakens if payment releases fail to occur or order restrictions persist. Naming those conditions before the next result makes the research more useful than explaining every surprise after it happens.

18 · Proxies, comparisons and valuation discipline

No single macro proxy represents the whole basket. Agricultural conditions can matter for irrigation, but a nuclear-qualified pump, an industrial positive-displacement unit and an overseas water project have different drivers. Industrial capital expenditure, maintenance activity, programme awards and public-project funding can each be relevant. The proxy must be attached to the portion of the business it plausibly influences. Otherwise a broad economic indicator can create confidence without improving the forecast.

Adjacent industries can answer narrow questions

Bearings, seals, motors, drives, valves and pipework are part of a pumping installation. Bearings and rotating equipment provide useful context for maintenance and reliability; see 084. Motion-control hydraulics uses pressurised fluid for a different purpose from much of this primer's liquid-transfer equipment; see 085. The products can interact without making their listed companies interchangeable comparables. An equipment supplier's demand is only a proxy if its end-market and order timing align with the pump business being analysed.

The three Engineering-family tests remain useful for comparison. The specification owner and wear relationship from see 078 help assess repeat demand. The processing depth from see 080 helps explain differences in product economics. The quotation exposure from see 081 helps compare tender suppliers. A comparable should share the relevant economic feature, not merely contain the word engineering in its description.

Choose an earnings numerator that belongs to shareholders

A parent-company P/E needs parent-owner earnings. WPIL makes that requirement visible because the minority share is substantial. Annual EPS must use weighted average shares when the capital changed during the year; GK and Shakti demonstrate the effect. Oswal's conflicting original EPS presentations require resolution before a printed quarterly EPS can be used mechanically. A low multiple calculated from the wrong numerator or denominator is not evidence of cheapness.

Enterprise-value comparisons also require care. Consolidated operating earnings can include businesses with non-controlling owners, while the listed market capitalisation represents only the parent's equity. Debt, leases, cash, minority claims and non-operating investments must be treated consistently with the earnings perimeter. A group that reports large associate earnings should not have those earnings counted in an operating multiple and the associate's value added again without adjustment. This page does not produce a target price because those valuation choices need a separate company-specific model.

Normalise economics, not just an exceptional line

Removing a labour-code charge can improve comparability, but it does not automatically establish a sustainable margin. A year may also contain unusually favourable tender prices, product mix, customer advances or input costs. Conversely, a weak quarter may contain a temporary commissioning expense without indicating permanent impairment. Normalisation requires a view on repeat operating conditions, supported by contracts and subsequent evidence. It should not mean selecting whichever earnings number makes the valuation attractive.

The cash requirement is equally important. Two companies with the same operating margin can have different equity economics if one receives advances and the other waits through a long inspection and payment cycle. A growing debtor balance can require more borrowing or equity even while profit rises. New manufacturing capacity adds another call on funds. The valuation must ask how much cash is left after sustaining the business and funding credible growth, and how uncertain its arrival is.

A scenario framework without invented precision

For an industrial supplier, vary order conversion, service mix and working-capital release. For a solar supplier, vary realised tender margin, executable awards and collection timing separately. For a project supplier, vary acceptance milestones, recoveries and the ownership share of profits. Do not make every assumption improve together by default. A company could win more orders at worse prices, or improve margins while collections remain slow. A useful downside case captures those combinations rather than simply reducing revenue by an arbitrary percentage.

The share-price sensitivity on this page serves a different purpose. Removing Oswal or selecting the industrial subset shows how the historical basket result changes with composition. It does not value the remaining companies or establish that they are safer at any price. Business quality, current expectations and the price paid must all be examined before a portfolio decision.

19 · Questions worth asking on the next call

For an industrial pump supplier

Ask which applications drove order intake and whether the increase came from units, package scope or pricing. Ask for the execution period and customer acceptance conditions. Then ask what share of the installed base actually generates service revenue and whether service growth improved group margin. If overseas revenue rises while margin weakens, request a bridge covering product mix, service mix, local costs, exchange translation and specific underperforming entities. Ask whether inventory is tied to customer orders, common components or speculative procurement. Which delivery dates support the balance? How much has been held beyond the expected production cycle? A technical milestone can be valuable without representing immediate revenue, and it should be labelled accordingly.

For the financial controller of any member

Ask for the exact weighted share count underlying EPS and the parent-owner profit used. If the published figures conflict, request a written reconciliation or corrected disclosure. Ask which items the company's EBITDA includes, whether the margin denominator is operating revenue or total income, and where associates and exceptionals enter. These are ordinary analytical questions. They keep the investment debate focused on the actual business rather than differences created by presentation.

20 · Myths, disputed claims and useful distinctions

“Water is essential, so pump earnings are defensive.”

The need is persistent, but the purchase can be a capital project, a replacement, a government programme or a maintenance decision. Timing, financing and competitive price determine the supplier's result. This basket's historical drawdowns and current margin differences show why essential demand is not equivalent to stable shareholder returns. The stronger claim is narrower: a correctly specified installed pump may support ongoing maintenance and replacement demand if the supplier retains that relationship.

“An integrated solar manufacturer must have the best margin.”

Integration changes control and capital commitments. It does not remove tender competition or guarantee favourable utilisation. Shakti and Oswal's current evidence requires attention to realisations and inputs; GK offers a different sourcing model with its own cash needs. Compare equivalent packages, periods and capital employed before assigning a structural advantage. A margin uplift forecast for new capacity is a proposition to test after commissioning.

“Government customers remove the risk.”

Expected recovery and timely recovery differ. Oswal's reported collection cycle and WPIL's receivable discussion demonstrate funding exposure even where management expects payment. Documentation, inspection, budget releases and disputes can influence timing. The analyst should avoid both extremes: treating every delayed bill as a loss, or treating every government-linked bill as cash already available. Ageing, contractual milestones and realised collections provide the evidence between them.

“The order book is next year's revenue.”

Some orders execute over longer periods; some need site readiness or additional conditions. A pipeline is less certain than a book, and even a book can contain slow-moving work. A megawatt figure, unit count and rupee amount are not interchangeable. Record the order's date and scope before using it in a forecast. KSB's order-intake mix, Shakti's July book and Oswal's August book answer different questions at different dates.

“Profit doubled, so the operating business doubled.”

Tax, associates, minorities, other income and consolidation can change the comparison. WPIL's owner profit grows differently from group profit. Roto's tax comparison amplifies the net-profit change. Jyoti's lower other income and tax charge obscure operating improvement. The correct response is an earnings bridge, not a dismissal of all reported profit and not an uncritical extrapolation.

“A strong April means an easy seasonal trade.”

Thirteen positive relative Aprils out of fifteen are worth noticing. They do not eliminate costs, valuation risk, sample selection or the possibility of a new regime. The wider April–July window has failures, and the 2026 full-basket YTD remains negative despite a strong April. The pattern can improve the timing of research preparation; a current thesis still has to justify the investment.

“A fall from the peak is enough margin of safety.”

The old peak may have embedded unusually optimistic growth, margins or policy expectations. A lower price can still be demanding relative to sustainable owner cash flow. Equally, a business can deserve a higher value after improving its recurring earnings and balance sheet. Compare the price with a defensible future cash claim, not solely with a point on a chart.

The judgement to retain

In pumps, the promise sold to the customer is hydraulic performance; the promise relevant to shareholders is profitable delivery followed by cash collection. Technical capability supports the first. Contract terms, execution, ownership and capital discipline connect it to the second. The strongest research keeps both promises visible and checks where the connection is breaking.

21 · Glossary

Hydraulics and selection

Pump
A machine that adds energy to a liquid so it can move through a system. The appropriate design depends on the liquid and required duty.
Flow rate
The quantity of liquid delivered per unit of time. Always retain the unit and operating condition when comparing quoted capacities.
Head
Energy per unit weight of liquid, commonly expressed as an equivalent liquid height. It includes more than the vertical height of a pipe.
Static head
The part of the system's required head associated with elevation and pressure differences, rather than flow-dependent friction losses.
Friction loss
Energy lost as liquid moves through pipes, fittings and other restrictions. It changes with flow and the characteristics of the installation.
Duty point
The specified operating requirement, usually a flow and head combination. A valid selection also considers liquid properties and the range of expected operation.
Operating point
The actual intersection of the pump and system characteristic curves. It can move when speed, liquid level or system resistance changes.
Pump curve
A representation of a pump's performance under stated conditions, often showing head against flow. It is not the complete installation's performance by itself.
System curve
The relationship between the head a system requires and the flow through it. Combining it with the pump curve identifies the operating point.
Best efficiency point
The point of maximum efficiency for a pump under specified conditions. Real operating conditions may vary around it rather than remain exactly there.
Centrifugal pump
A pump in which a rotating impeller transfers energy to liquid. Selection depends on flow, head, liquid properties and operating conditions.
Impeller
The rotating hydraulic component that transfers energy to liquid in a centrifugal pump. Its design and condition affect performance and efficiency.
Volute
A shaped casing passage that collects flow leaving an impeller. It forms part of the hydraulic design rather than simply enclosing the rotating component.
Diffuser
A stationary arrangement that guides flow and helps convert velocity into pressure. Its geometry must suit the pump's intended operating conditions.
Multistage pump
A pump using successive stages to add head. More stages are useful for particular duties, not a universal measure of superior product quality.
Submersible pump
A pump arrangement designed to operate submerged in liquid. Motor protection, materials, installation and retrieval become relevant parts of selection and maintenance.
Cavitation
Formation and collapse of vapour cavities when local pressure conditions permit. It can cause noise, vibration, performance loss and damage to hydraulic surfaces.
NPSH available
Net positive suction head provided by the actual system at the pump inlet. It depends on site conditions, liquid properties and suction losses.
NPSH required
A pump's specified suction-head requirement under defined test criteria. Engineering selection compares it with the available value and an appropriate margin.
Throttling
Restricting flow through a valve or similar device. It changes system resistance and can dissipate energy that the pump has already supplied.
Variable-speed drive
Equipment controlling motor speed. It can help match pump output to demand, but benefits depend on the system and operating pattern.
Hydraulic efficiency
The effectiveness of converting supplied energy into useful liquid movement. State the measured boundary because pump, motor and whole-system efficiencies differ.

Components, applications and service

Positive-displacement pump
A pump that moves liquid by repeatedly enclosing and displacing volumes. Fluid characteristics and pressure requirements determine the appropriate design and protection.
Progressive-cavity pump
A positive-displacement design using a rotor within a stator to move cavities of liquid. Application suitability depends on fluid and operating conditions.
Rotor
The rotating element of a machine. In a progressive-cavity pump, its geometry works with the stator to create the moving cavities.
Stator
A stationary component working with the rotor. In relevant pump designs its material compatibility and wear condition influence reliability and replacement demand.
Twin-screw pump
A positive-displacement design using interacting screws to move fluid. It serves particular handling requirements and should not be treated as interchangeable with every pump.
Viscosity
A liquid's resistance to flow. It affects pump selection, losses and handling behaviour, making water-based performance assumptions unsuitable for every process liquid.
Slurry
Liquid carrying suspended solids. Particle characteristics, concentration and abrasiveness influence materials, geometry, wear and the maintenance schedule.
Shear sensitivity
The tendency of a handled material to change or suffer damage under mechanical shear. It can influence the choice of pump and operating speed.
Mechanical seal
An assembly limiting leakage where a rotating shaft passes through a stationary enclosure. Materials, lubrication and operating conditions influence its reliability.
Elastomer
A flexible polymer used in components such as seals and stators. Chemical and temperature compatibility matter as well as purchase price.
Wear part
A component expected to deteriorate through use and require replacement. Repeat revenue depends on utilisation, replacement access and the supplier retaining the customer.
Material traceability
Records connecting a component to its material and manufacturing history. It helps demonstrate compliance with the specified application and quality requirements.
Performance test
A controlled check of equipment against stated operating requirements. The test conditions and acceptance criteria determine what the result actually demonstrates.
Commissioning
The process of bringing installed equipment into working service and confirming required operation. Physical delivery alone does not establish commissioning completion.
Acceptance
The customer's contractual recognition that a specified delivery or performance condition has been met. It can affect revenue milestones and payment rights.
Installed base
Equipment already operating at customer sites. It creates potential service opportunities, but does not guarantee the original supplier captures every maintenance order.
Aftermarket
Products and services supplied after the original equipment sale, including spares and repairs. Its economics depend on the installed base and competitive access.
Retrofit
A modification to existing equipment or a system. The economic case depends on measured benefits relative to the modification cost and downtime.
Condition monitoring
Observation of machine indicators to detect deterioration or abnormal operation. Value requires interpretation and an effective maintenance response, not sensors alone.
Planned maintenance
Maintenance scheduled to preserve reliability or performance. Its timing may create recurring demand while differing from the customer's new-capacity investment cycle.
Lifecycle cost
The cost of acquiring, operating, maintaining and eventually replacing an installation. Energy, downtime and service can change the apparent cheapest choice.
Scope of supply
The products and responsibilities included in a contract. A pump-only order and a complete solar installation can have very different revenue and risk.

Solar programmes and contracts

Solar pump system
A pumping installation powered through solar equipment and associated controls. The package may include modules, structures, installation, monitoring and maintenance obligations.
PV module
A photovoltaic panel converting light into electrical output. Its nameplate capacity alone does not establish the water a complete installation delivers each day.
Solar cell
A photovoltaic device used within a module. Cell manufacturing and module assembly are distinct activities for technology, investment and applicable domestic-content requirements.
DCR
Domestic content requirement. In the relevant programme it concerns specified component manufacturing eligibility; it should not be inferred solely from a domestic brand name.
Undiffused wafer
A wafer before the relevant diffusion processing. MNRE's cited clarification uses this manufacturing-stage distinction when explaining eligible domestic crystalline-cell manufacture.
Diffused wafer
A wafer already subjected to diffusion processing. The cited MNRE clarification distinguishes imported diffused wafers from the required domestic cell-manufacturing process.
Inverter
Equipment converting electrical power into the required form for an application. Product quality and compliance matter alongside the pump's own hydraulic performance.
Controller
Equipment managing system operation. Its functions and protections depend on the installation and should be checked against the actual contract and specification.
PM-KUSUM
The government framework discussed in this primer for specified solar agricultural energy applications. Its components have different project and procurement structures.
Component B
The PM-KUSUM component concerning standalone solar agricultural pumps. Do not treat every solar EPC order as a Component-B pump installation.
Component C
The PM-KUSUM component concerning solarisation of grid-connected agricultural pumps or feeders. Its scope differs from standalone pump deployment.
CFA
Central financial assistance under an applicable programme. Eligibility, sanction and payment conditions must be established before treating assistance as assured supplier cash.
Sanction
Formal approval under a programme or funding framework. It is a distinct step from a supplier's final executable order and subsequent payment.
Empanelment
Inclusion among eligible or approved suppliers under stated conditions. It does not itself guarantee an order quantity, revenue or collection date.
Letter of award
A document awarding a stated scope under specified terms. Its validity, conditions and relationship with subsequent instructions must be read before forecasting execution.
Notice to proceed
An instruction permitting work to begin under the relevant arrangement. Its date is material to the cited PM-KUSUM extension conditions.
PPA
Power purchase agreement. Its relevance depends on the solar project's structure; it is not automatically the contract used for every standalone pump installation.
SCOD
Scheduled commercial operation date. An extension must be interpreted with the applicable project agreement, sanction validity and programme conditions.
Pipeline
Potential opportunities being pursued. It has less certainty than booked orders and should be recorded separately rather than added as assured revenue.
Tender vintage
The period and terms under which an order was won. Different vintages can carry different prices, input assumptions and remaining execution margins.
Escalation clause
A contract provision allowing price adjustment under specified conditions. The covered inputs, index, lag and limits determine its economic protection.
Retention
A portion of payment held back under contractual conditions. It can remain a cash requirement after delivery or installation has otherwise been completed.

Accounts, ownership and investment evidence

Operating revenue
Income from the company's ordinary business activities as reported. Package scope and consolidated perimeter must be consistent when comparing its growth.
Other income
Income reported outside operating revenue. Read the original components because summary presentations may also combine associates or exceptional adjustments into an expanded line.
Operating EBITDA
A defined measure of operating earnings before finance cost, tax and depreciation. Check which income and adjustments are included before comparing company margins.
Exceptional item
An item separately identified in the financial presentation. Removing it may aid comparison but does not by itself establish sustainable operating profitability.
Associate profit
The investor company's share of an associate's result under the applicable accounting method. It is distinct from directly consolidated operating revenue and cash receipts.
Joint venture
An arrangement under joint control. Its contribution and reporting date should be identified separately when interpreting consolidated earnings.
Non-controlling interest
The portion of a consolidated subsidiary owned by others. Group profit includes it, while parent-owner earnings exclude the share belonging to those owners.
Owner profit
Profit attributable to the parent company's equity holders. It is the relevant numerator for the parent's EPS reconciliation.
Weighted average shares
The time-weighted share count used for EPS. It can differ from closing shares after an issue, requiring care in annual profit cross-checks.
Basic EPS
Earnings attributable to ordinary shareholders divided by the relevant weighted average shares. It should reconcile to the original profit and share-count disclosures.
Diluted EPS
EPS reflecting applicable potential shares under accounting rules. Its denominator and assumptions can differ from the basic calculation.
Consolidated perimeter
The businesses included in a group's financial statements. Acquisitions, disposals and changes of control can affect comparisons even when the ticker remains unchanged.
Operating cash flow
Cash generated or consumed by operating activities over a stated period. It is different from profit and does not subtract all investment spending.
Free cash flow
A cash measure after relevant investment requirements, defined consistently for the analysis. Positive operating cash alone does not establish positive free cash flow.
Receivable ageing
A breakdown of outstanding amounts by time and due status. It helps distinguish recent billing, not-yet-due claims and overdue or disputed balances.
Unbilled revenue
Recognised revenue not yet billed under the applicable contract process. It can require funding before a formal receivable or payment claim emerges.
Cash-conversion cycle
A measure linking inventory, collection and supplier-payment timing. Definitions differ, so use consistent company methodology when comparing periods.
Customer advance
Cash received before the corresponding delivery or performance. It can help fund execution but creates an obligation to supply the contracted scope.
ARC
Asset reconstruction company. Its lending or recovery arrangement should be read from actual terms; a small interest charge does not resolve the repayment claim.
Going concern
The basis that a business will continue operating. Management's supporting assumptions and the auditor's actual wording matter when financial stress is disclosed.
Survivorship bias
Distortion from examining companies that remain in the current universe while omitting those that disappeared. It limits interpretation of a long historical basket.
Relative return
Basket return minus benchmark return over the same period. A positive spread can occur even when both investments lose money in absolute terms.

22 · Check your understanding

Try answering before opening each explanation. The aim is to connect the product promise with the shareholder’s cash claim.

Why does the largest advertised pump not necessarily suit the installation?

The duty is a flow and head requirement under actual system conditions. The operating point comes from the pump and system curves together. Oversizing can create inefficient operation; motor size cannot repair poor suction conditions.

Why is a specialist rotor or stator potentially a recurring revenue source?

It can wear in service and require replacement. The supplier still needs an operating installed base, access to the customer and a competitive replacement offer. Wear creates an opportunity, not guaranteed revenue.

Which three Engineering-family tests apply here?

Use the existing specification and wear test, the processing-depth test and the quote-to-delivery input-risk test. In pumps they apply to qualified hydraulic performance, machining and testing, and the costs inside a fixed-price installation package.

What does Shakti’s latest quarter teach about volume?

Installation growth and higher revenue coexisted with a sharply lower operating margin and profit. Tender realisations, inputs and mix must be checked before treating more units as better shareholder economics.

Why is WPIL’s group PAT unsuitable for a parent P/E without adjustment?

A substantial portion belongs to non-controlling owners. The parent shareholder’s profit is the relevant EPS numerator. Associate contributions and the consolidated perimeter also need consistent treatment.

Why can annual EPS differ from profit divided by closing shares?

An issue during the year changes the time-weighted denominator. GK and Shakti’s original annual calculations use weighted average shares. Closing capital is not a substitute for that denominator.

What is unresolved in Oswal’s EPS disclosure?

The annual-report EPS and weighted shares reconcile, but the quarterly filing prints inconsistent annual and June EPS values. The discrepancy is disclosed; the printed quarter EPS is not used as an unquestioned valuation input.

Does a government-linked receivable remove working-capital risk?

No. Expected recovery and payment timing are different. The supplier may still fund components, labour and interest while waiting. Read not-yet-due amounts, ageing, milestones and realised collections separately.

What is the actual boundary of the cited PM-KUSUM extension?

It concerns qualifying existing projects with PPAs or notices to proceed by the specified December 2025 deadline, subject to the stated conditions. It is not proof of blanket new awards or an approved successor scheme.

Why does Jyoti require more than an ordinary earnings-growth comparison?

Its borrowing note and CARO statement conflict about overdue status, and aged receivables and financial stress require reconciliation. Low finance expense does not establish a harmless debt arrangement.

What can thirteen positive Aprils tell an investor?

It identifies a historical relative-return tendency useful for scheduling research. It does not guarantee an absolute gain, a profitable four-month window or a sound current company thesis.

Why does the seven-name basket not have seven members throughout history?

Available series begin at different dates. Oswal joins after its 2025 listing and Shakti after its late 2011 history begins. GK is excluded for insufficient observations. The methodology uses available eligible members and must disclose that changing composition.
