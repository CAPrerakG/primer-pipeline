## What this industry actually contains

The economically useful dividing line is between selling a new handling system, selling wear parts and service into an installed system, and renting a machine that the operator finances and maintains. A conveyor transfers material; the equity return depends on who owns the design, who replaces the belt, who bears downtime and whether invoices become cash. Those businesses can react differently to the same mine, steel-plant or construction expansion.

Thejo combines manufacturing with conveyor-related services and wear protection. Somi supplies industrial conveyor belts. TRF supplies bulk-handling equipment/projects and services. These three are the proposed industrial core, subject to price-history approval and the remaining audit work below. International Conveyors is relevant technically, but financial investments dominate its current earnings and asset allocation; the recommended treatment is an explicitly separate sensitivity. ## Engineering family applications

**See 078, Castings:** ask who specifies the conveyor duty and component, and what wears out. A belt, splice or abrasion liner is repeatedly consumed; replacement frequency alone does not establish supplier loyalty or pricing power. The useful next evidence is replacement/service mix, qualifications and the cost of a failed splice or stopped line. For elevators and cranes, distinguish the installed manufacturer's service rights from the ability of third parties to maintain equipment.

**See 080, Forgings:** margin follows the difficult work retained, not the headline tonnes handled. Belt compound formulation, reinforcement, joining know-how, engineered liners and reliable site service can matter more than gross fabricated-steel tonnage. Distinguish bought-out equipment/trading from retained design, process control, manufacture and field execution. TRF's project scope is economically different from a belt supplier and from Crown's fleet utilisation.

**See 081, Metal Fabrication:** identify who carries input-price and acceptance risk between quotation and collection. Equipment projects can lock in steel, bought-out drives and labour well before acceptance. Belt producers carry rubber/reinforcement inputs and inventories; service businesses carry labour and receivables; rental fleets carry depreciation, financing and idle time. A strong order book is not a substitute for a margin/working-capital bridge.
